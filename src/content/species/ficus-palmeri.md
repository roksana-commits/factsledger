## Overview

Ficus palmeri, known as the rock fig or tescalama, is a strangler fig native to the Baja California peninsula in Mexico, in the family Moraceae ([Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum](https://www.desertmuseum.org/programs/succulents_rockfigs.php); [University of Arizona Campus Arboretum](https://apps.cals.arizona.edu/arboretum/taxon.aspx?id=1080)).

## Growth on bare rock

Unlike typical tropical strangler figs, which germinate on host trees, Ficus palmeri seedlings establish directly on cliff faces and boulders. Without access to moist soil, a plant can remain a dwarfed, rock-bound saxicole for its entire life; individual specimens are described as capable of clinging to a hilltop boulder for centuries. Where roots eventually reach soil or a streambed, the same species can instead grow into a substantial tree, potentially reaching 20 to 50 feet ([Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum](https://www.desertmuseum.org/programs/succulents_rockfigs.php); [University of Arizona Campus Arboretum](https://apps.cals.arizona.edu/arboretum/taxon.aspx?id=1080)).

## Description and cultivation

Mature plants have large, dark green, fuzzy leaves that are ovate to cordate in shape, yellowish and sometimes scaly bark, and, in older specimens, a large swollen caudex. The University of Arizona Campus Arboretum, which holds a specimen under accession number 18988, notes it prefers rocky, well-drained soil and partial shade, tolerates moderate drought, is hardy to about 28 degrees Fahrenheit, and is commonly grown as a bonsai subject ([University of Arizona Campus Arboretum](https://apps.cals.arizona.edu/arboretum/taxon.aspx?id=1080)).
