## Overview

Ilya Sutskever is a computer scientist who co-created the AlexNet neural network in 2012, co-founded the artificial intelligence company [OpenAI](/organizations/openai/) in 2015 and served as its chief scientist, and in 2024 left to found Safe Superintelligence Inc., a company devoted solely to developing safe superintelligent AI ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/ilya-sutskever-leader-ai-and-its-responsible-development-receives-u-t-honorary-degree); [TechCrunch, June 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/19/ilya-sutskever-openais-former-chief-scientist-launches-new-ai-company)). He was one of the OpenAI board members who voted in November 2023 to remove [Sam Altman](/people/sam-altman/) as chief executive, a decision he publicly said he came to regret within days ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2023/11/20/sam-altman-fired-openai-board-illya-sutsever-regrets); [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2023/11/20/ilya-sutskever-openai-cofounder-deeply-regrets-resign)).

According to the University of Toronto, Sutskever was born in Russia, raised in Israel, and emigrated to Canada with his family as a teenager ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/ilya-sutskever-leader-ai-and-its-responsible-development-receives-u-t-honorary-degree)).

## Education and early career

Sutskever was admitted directly into the University of Toronto's mathematics program and graduated with an honours Bachelor of Science in mathematics in 2005. He went on to earn a master's degree and a Ph.D. in computer science at the same university, supervised by Geoffrey Hinton — later a University Professor Emeritus and 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics laureate ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/ilya-sutskever-leader-ai-and-its-responsible-development-receives-u-t-honorary-degree)). His doctoral research applied neural networks to language modeling; the university describes it as producing "a program that used neural networks...to learn about language and generate text — a crude forerunner to ChatGPT" ([University of Toronto, Department of Computer Science](https://web.cs.toronto.edu/news-events/news/ilya-sutskever-a-leader-in-ai-and-its-responsible-development-receives-u-of-t-honorary-degree)). His dissertation, "Training Recurrent Neural Networks," is dated 2013 and lists Hinton as supervisor ([Ph.D. thesis, University of Toronto](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~ilya/pubs/ilya_sutskever_phd_thesis.pdf)).

In 2012, while still a graduate student, Sutskever worked with Hinton and fellow graduate student Alex Krizhevsky to develop AlexNet, a convolutional neural network that won that year's ImageNet computer-vision competition by a wide margin ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/ilya-sutskever-leader-ai-and-its-responsible-development-receives-u-t-honorary-degree); [University of Toronto, Department of Computer Science](https://web.cs.toronto.edu/news-events/news/ilya-sutskever-a-leader-in-ai-and-its-responsible-development-receives-u-of-t-honorary-degree)). Sutskever and Hinton subsequently co-founded a company, DNNResearch, which was acquired by Google; Sutskever then worked as a research scientist at Google Brain, where he contributed to training the AlphaGo game-playing system and co-developed sequence-to-sequence neural network models used in machine translation ([University of Toronto, Department of Computer Science](https://web.cs.toronto.edu/news-events/news/ilya-sutskever-a-leader-in-ai-and-its-responsible-development-receives-u-of-t-honorary-degree)).

## OpenAI

In 2015, Sutskever co-founded OpenAI, initially serving as its research director and later as chief scientist ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/ilya-sutskever-leader-ai-and-its-responsible-development-receives-u-t-honorary-degree)).

### The November 2023 board crisis

On November 17, 2023, Sutskever voted with OpenAI's board to remove Sam Altman as chief executive ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2023/11/20/sam-altman-fired-openai-board-illya-sutsever-regrets)). Fortune's contemporaneous reporting described him as the board's "ringleader" in that decision, driven by concern that Altman was pushing the pace of AI development too far, too fast ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2023/11/20/ilya-sutskever-openai-cofounder-deeply-regrets-resign)). According to his own account, given in May 2026 trial testimony, he had spent roughly a year gathering material documenting what he considered a "consistent pattern of lying" by Altman before the vote ([Associated Press via WTVB](https://wtvbam.com/2026/05/11/former-openai-executive-sutskever-discloses-nearly-7-billion-stake-in-ai-firm/)). That same testimony indicated OpenAI's board, after removing Altman, discussed a possible merger with rival AI company [Anthropic](/organizations/anthropic/) — a proposal Sutskever said he was "not excited" about ([Associated Press via WTVB](https://wtvbam.com/2026/05/11/former-openai-executive-sutskever-discloses-nearly-7-billion-stake-in-ai-firm/)).

By his own later account, described in 2026 trial testimony, Sutskever largely avoided the internet over the weekend immediately following the vote, and so missed Microsoft's offer to hire OpenAI's staff and the rapid spread of an employee letter demanding the board's resignation ([Business Insider via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/openai-board-member-didnt-look-225722212.html)). On November 20, 2023, he posted a public statement of regret on X: "I deeply regret my participation in the board's actions. I never intended to harm OpenAI. I love everything we've built together and I will do everything I can to reunite the company" ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2023/11/20/ilya-sutskever-openai-cofounder-deeply-regrets-resign); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2023/11/20/sam-altman-fired-openai-board-illya-sutsever-regrets)). Around the same time, an employee letter demanding the board's resignation and Altman's reinstatement was already circulating and gathering signatures ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2023/11/20/ilya-sutskever-openai-cofounder-deeply-regrets-resign)); within days of the original vote, Sutskever himself signed it ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2024/05/14/tech/openai-chief-scientist-ilya-sutskever-departs); [TechBuzz.ai](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/ilya-sutskever-stands-by-his-role-in-sam-altman-s-openai-ouster-i-didn-t-want-it-to-be-destroyed)). Altman was reinstated as CEO on November 21, 2023, under a reconstituted board, and Sutskever returned to the company alongside him ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2024/05/14/tech/openai-chief-scientist-ilya-sutskever-departs)).

More than two years later, testifying in [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk/)'s lawsuit against OpenAI, Sutskever stood by his original vote even as he had reversed it in practice, telling the court, "I didn't want it to be destroyed" ([TechBuzz.ai](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/ilya-sutskever-stands-by-his-role-in-sam-altman-s-openai-ouster-i-didn-t-want-it-to-be-destroyed)).

### Departure

On May 14, 2024, Sutskever announced he was leaving OpenAI: "After almost a decade, I have made the decision to leave OpenAI. The company's trajectory has been nothing short of miraculous, and I'm confident that OpenAI will build AGI that is both safe and beneficial...I am excited for what comes next — a project that is very personally meaningful to me about which I will share details in due time" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/14/ilya-sutskever-openai-co-founder-and-longtime-chief-scientist-departs)). Sam Altman responded, "This is very sad to me; Ilya is easily one of the greatest minds of our generation, a guiding light of our field, and a dear friend. OpenAI would not be what it is without him" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/14/ilya-sutskever-openai-co-founder-and-longtime-chief-scientist-departs)). Research director Jakub Pachocki, who Bloomberg reported had effectively been performing the chief scientist role since the previous November, took over the title ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-05-14/openai-chief-scientist-ilya-sutskever-is-leaving-the-company)). CNN described the departure as ending "months of speculation in Silicon Valley" about Sutskever's future at the company following his role in the Altman ouster ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2024/05/14/tech/openai-chief-scientist-ilya-sutskever-departs)).

## Safe Superintelligence Inc.

On June 19, 2024, Sutskever announced Safe Superintelligence Inc. (SSI), co-founded with Daniel Gross, a former Y Combinator partner, and Daniel Levy, a former OpenAI engineer, with offices in Palo Alto and Tel Aviv ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/19/ilya-sutskever-openais-former-chief-scientist-launches-new-ai-company)). Unlike OpenAI's original nonprofit structure, SSI was set up as a for-profit company from its founding. Its announcement described a singular focus: "SSI is our mission, our name, and our entire product roadmap, because it is the sole focus" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/19/ilya-sutskever-openais-former-chief-scientist-launches-new-ai-company)).

SSI raised $1 billion in its first funding round, announced September 4, 2024, at a reported $5 billion valuation, with Andreessen Horowitz and Sequoia Capital among the lead investors ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/safe-superintelligence-1-billion-ilya-sutskever); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/ai/startup-safe-superintelligence-unicorn-raise/)). In April 2025, the company raised a further round reported at $2 billion, led by Greenoaks Capital Partners with participation from Andreessen Horowitz, Lightspeed Venture Partners, DST Global, Alphabet and Nvidia, at a reported $32 billion valuation — roughly six times its valuation seven months earlier ([CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/hjfywdtajl); [Yahoo Finance, citing Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/alphabet-nvidia-invest-ai-startup-102359815.html)). As of that report, SSI had released no public product, model or technical demonstration ([CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/hjfywdtajl)).

Testifying in May 2026 in the federal trial of Elon Musk's lawsuit against OpenAI, Sutskever said his OpenAI equity stake — separate from his position at SSI — was worth about $5 billion as of November 2025 and about $7 billion at the time of his testimony ([Associated Press via WTVB](https://wtvbam.com/2026/05/11/former-openai-executive-sutskever-discloses-nearly-7-billion-stake-in-ai-firm/)).
