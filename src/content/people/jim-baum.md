## Overview

Jim Baum is an American technology executive best known as president and chief executive of [Netezza](/organizations/netezza/) from January 2008 until shortly after the company's 2010 acquisition by IBM. He holds a bachelor's degree in mechanical engineering from Worcester Polytechnic Institute and a master's degree in engineering from Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute ([Newforma via Marketwired](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/jim-baum-joins-newforma-board-141322183.html)).

## Career before Netezza

Before Netezza, Baum spent 11 years at Parametric Technology Corporation (PTC), where the Martin Trust Center for MIT Entrepreneurship's profile of him describes him rising to executive vice president and general manager and helping grow the company's revenue from $11 million to more than $1 billion ([MIT Martin Trust Center](https://entrepreneurship.mit.edu/profile/jim-baum/)). The Worcester Business Journal separately reported that, before joining Netezza, Baum had worked at the Cambridge-based startup Endeca ([Worcester Business Journal](https://www.wbjournal.com/article/shop-talk-qa-with-jim-baum-netezza-corp)); DataStax's own bio of Baum and Newforma's 2013 board-appointment release both go further, describing him as having served as Endeca's president and later chief executive before Oracle's 2011 acquisition of that company ([DataStax](https://www.datastax.com/our-people/jim-baum); [Newforma via Marketwired](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/jim-baum-joins-newforma-board-141322183.html)).

## Leading Netezza and the IBM acquisition

Effective January 31, 2008, Baum, then Netezza's president and chief operating officer, succeeded co-founder Jit Saxena as chief executive, with Saxena remaining chairman of the board ([Worcester Business Journal](https://wbjournal.com/article/netezza-profits-double-ceo-leaves/)). On September 20, 2010, IBM announced it would acquire Netezza for $27 a share, valued at approximately $1.7 billion net of cash, a deal that closed that November. Roughly a year after the acquisition closed, Boston.com reported that Baum had left IBM for a next venture described only as "non-competitive" with Netezza and IBM, with "details" that were, in the article's words, "sketchy"; the article noted that Netezza's own website no longer listed him on its executive team page ([Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/uncategorized/noprimarytagmatch/2011/09/30/netezza-ceo-baum-out-at-ibm-on-to-new-non-competitive-venture/)).

## After Netezza

That next venture became clear within weeks: in November 2011, Boston.com reported that Baum had become chief executive of CasePick Systems, a low-profile Wilmington, Massachusetts startup then developing robots for warehouse order-picking, with roughly 100 employees. Baum told the paper the shift into robotics was appealing precisely because it differed from software: "Selling software is very much driven by volume, but robotics involves much bigger purchase prices, and a lot of effort to get the technology to work right in each customer deployment" ([Boston.com](http://archive.boston.com/business/technology/innoeco/2011/11/new_ceo_at_casepick_systems_de.html)).

Baum went on to join several corporate boards. On October 29, 2013, project-information-management software company Newforma announced he had joined its board, with chief executive Ian Howell saying Baum "brings rich operating experience to help us meet the challenges of a rapidly scaling company"; the same release reported that Worcester Polytechnic Institute had given Baum its inaugural Innovator of the Year Award in 2011 and that he served as a trustee there ([Newforma via Marketwired](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/jim-baum-joins-newforma-board-141322183.html)). DataStax's own team page separately confirms he sits on its board of directors ([DataStax](https://www.datastax.com/our-people/jim-baum)), and Applitools' team page confirms a board role there, describing his career as "over 25 years in technology leadership" and crediting the Netezza-to-IBM transaction as a deal Information Week named a top "disruptive acquisition" of 2010 ([Applitools](https://applitools.com/about/jim-baum/)). The Martin Trust Center for MIT Entrepreneurship's profile additionally identifies Baum as a venture partner at OpenView and a lecturer in entrepreneurship at MIT's Sloan School of Management ([MIT Martin Trust Center](https://entrepreneurship.mit.edu/profile/jim-baum/)).
