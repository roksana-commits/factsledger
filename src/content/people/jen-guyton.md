## Overview

Jen Guyton — published academically as Jennifer A. Guyton — is a conservation ecologist and wildlife photographer.
While a doctoral student in the Department of Ecology and Evolutionary Biology at Princeton University, she was
the fourth author on a 2017 paper in Nature, "A theoretical foundation for multi-scale regular vegetation
patterns," alongside Corina E. Tarnita, Juan A. Bonachela, Efrat Sheffer, Tyler C. Coverdale, Ryan A. Long and
Robert M. Pringle, addressing the mechanisms behind regularly spaced vegetation formations including Namibia's
"fairy circles" ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature20801)). Princeton's own news office confirmed
her role as a doctoral student contributing fieldwork to the research
([Princeton University](https://www.princeton.edu/news/2017/01/19/african-fairy-circles-template-natures-many-patterns)).

## Recognition

In 2018, the Natural History Museum in London named Guyton the category winner for Plants and Fungi in its
Wildlife Photographer of the Year competition, for a photograph of a welwitschia plant taken in the Namib Desert
([Natural History Museum](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/wpy/gallery/2018-desert-relic)). In 2020, the American Society of
Mammalogists gave her its Murie Family Conservation Award, citing doctoral research in Mozambique's Gorongosa
National Park that combined biological surveys with work on interactions between large herbivores and plants,
including a guide to the park's bats and research on how reintroducing large herbivores could help control the
invasive shrub Mimosa pigra ([American Society of Mammalogists](https://mammalogy.org/2020-award-recipients)).
