## Overview

Elinor Tatum is the publisher and editor-in-chief of the New York Amsterdam News, a historic Black weekly
newspaper her father, Wilbert Tatum, acquired in 1971. She joined the paper's staff in the late 1980s, working
her way up through production and editorial roles before becoming chief operating officer and associate
publisher in 1996 and being named publisher and editor-in-chief in 1997
([Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/leading-with-grace-and-power,252691)). By 2005,
independent of the Amsterdam News itself, NPR identified her as the paper's editor-in-chief and interviewed her
as an expert commentator on how Black Americans get their news
([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2005/04/20/4608042/how-will-african-americans-get-the-news)).

## Leadership and recent growth

In 2021, Tatum appointed Siobhan Bennett as the paper's president and chief revenue officer, and together they
carried out a five-year strategic plan that grew the paper's newsletter subscriber base from about 900 to 27,000,
tripled print subscriptions, and doubled the size of its newsroom through grants and partnerships including
Report for America; the paper won more than 30 awards over the following two years
([Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/leading-with-grace-and-power,252691)).

## 2024 Publisher of the Year

In 2024, Editor & Publisher magazine named Tatum its Publisher of the Year — the first Black woman to receive
the award in its 140-year history ([Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/leading-with-grace-and-power,252691)).
