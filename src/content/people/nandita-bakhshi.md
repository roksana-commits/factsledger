## Overview

Nandita Bakhshi is an American banking executive who holds a Bachelor of Arts in history from the University of Calcutta and a Master of Arts in international relations from Jadavpur University, both in India ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/584021/quaker-houghton-appoints-nandita-bakhshi-and-lucrece-foufopoulos-de-ridder-to-board/); [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/81362/000008136224000106/kwr8-kxjuly2024exx991.htm)). She moved from India to Albany, New York in 1986 and began her banking career as a part-time teller at Northeast Savings Bank ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/nandita-bakhshi-former-bank-of-the-west-ceo-the-exit-interview)). Over the following three decades she held executive roles at Banc One Corp., Home Savings of America, FleetBoston, First Data, Washington Mutual and TD Bank, before joining Bank of the West as CEO-in-training on April 1, 2016 and formally taking over as President and CEO on June 1, 2016, succeeding [Michael Shepherd](/people/michael-shepherd/), who moved to chairman of BNP Paribas' U.S. holding company ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/can-nandita-bakhshi-turn-bank-of-the-west-into-a-growth-story)). She held the Bank of the West post until February 2023, concurrently serving as co-CEO of BNP Paribas USA, Bank of the West's parent company ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/most-powerful-women-in-banking-no-6-bank-of-the-wests-nandita-bakhshi)).

## Bank of the West tenure and ESG policy

As CEO, Bakhshi described her role as leading the transformation of a roughly $100 billion-asset bank across technology, culture, environmental policy and risk and compliance. Under her leadership, Bank of the West became one of the few U.S. banks to restrict financing of activities it deemed harmful to the environment, a policy that drew public criticism from U.S. Senator John Barrasso of Wyoming, who called it "a direct attack on hard-working families in Wyoming and across the country that depend on fossil fuels" and said it was "a serious mistake to pick winners and losers in energy markets" ([The Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-bank-of-the-west-ceo-wins-praise-and-draws-criticism-for-esg-approach/)). She was repeatedly named to American Banker's "Most Powerful Women in Banking" list for her financial performance and her leadership on diversity and environmental issues.

## Departure and later roles

Bakhshi stepped down as CEO in February 2023 following BMO's completed acquisition of Bank of the West from BNP Paribas, serving as a special advisor through the transition before formally retiring on December 15, 2023, after a 37-year banking career ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/nandita-bakhshi-former-bank-of-the-west-ceo-the-exit-interview)). She subsequently joined BMO's board and continued serving on the board of Grameen America, a microfinance organization, alongside a term on the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco's board and seats on The Clearing House's supervisory board and the U.S. India Strategic Partnership Forum's board ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/most-powerful-women-in-banking-no-6-bank-of-the-wests-nandita-bakhshi)).

## Corporate board service since 2024

In May 2024, Bakhshi joined the board of directors of Beyond Meat, Inc., where she later chaired the nominating and corporate governance committee and served on the audit committee ([SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1655210/000165521025000074/bynd-20250408.htm)). On July 31, 2024, she also joined the board of directors of [Quaker Chemical Corporation, which does business as Quaker Houghton](/organizations/quaker-houghton/) ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/584021/quaker-houghton-appoints-nandita-bakhshi-and-lucrece-foufopoulos-de-ridder-to-board/); [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/81362/000008136224000106/kwr8-kxjuly2024exx991.htm)). In September 2025, Beyond Meat disclosed that Bakhshi would resign from its board effective upon settlement of an exchange offer intended to restructure more than $800 million of the company's convertible debt, as part of a broader board reshuffle tied to that transaction ([SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1655210/000119312525221793/d863131d8k.htm)).
