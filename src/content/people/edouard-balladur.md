## Overview

Édouard Balladur was born on 2 May 1929 in İzmir (Smyrna), Turkey, and graduated from
France's École nationale d'administration in 1957. He joined the Office of Radio and
Television Broadcasting in 1962 and was recommended to Prime Minister Georges Pompidou,
serving on his staff through the 1960s and into the 1970s. After Pompidou's death in 1974,
he moved into industry, chairing two subsidiaries of the national electric company
([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Edouard-Balladur)).

## Political career

Balladur returned to public life advising Jacques Chirac in the 1980s, was elected to the
National Assembly as a Paris deputy in 1986, and joined Chirac's cabinet as minister of
economy, finance and privatization — a role in which he helped develop "cohabitation," the
power-sharing arrangement with Socialist President François Mitterrand. After conservatives'
landslide March 1993 legislative win, Mitterrand appointed him prime minister, a post he held
until 1995. As prime minister, he oversaw the privatization of major state companies
including Société Générale, Paribas and the broadcaster TF1, and implemented a 1993 pension
reform ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Edouard-Balladur);
[GV Wire](https://gvwire.com/2026/08/31/former-french-pm-balladur-dies-at-97/)). In 1995 he
ran for president, placing third in the first round as voters balked at his challenging his
former mentor Chirac; he endorsed Chirac, who won
([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Edouard-Balladur)).

## The Karachi affair

During Balladur's premiership, France sold submarines to Pakistan and frigates to Saudi
Arabia. Investigators later alleged the deals generated roughly €2.8 million in kickbacks,
part of which prosecutors said was funneled into his unsuccessful 1995 presidential
campaign — pointing to a 10.25-million-franc cash deposit made three days after his defeat.
Balladur maintained his campaign funds came from merchandise sales at rallies
([France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/europe/20210119-french-ex-pm-balladur-goes-on-trial-over-karachi-affair-kickback-allegations)).
A 2002 bombing in Karachi killed 15 people, including 11 French engineers working on the
submarine contract; investigators explored whether it was retaliation for Chirac's decision,
after defeating Balladur, to halt the commission payments — a theory that was never publicly
settled but whose investigation helped expose the alleged kickback scheme
([France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/europe/20210119-french-ex-pm-balladur-goes-on-trial-over-karachi-affair-kickback-allegations)).

Balladur went on trial in January 2021, at age 91, charged with complicity in the misuse of
corporate assets. In March 2021, he was acquitted of all charges. His former defense
minister, François Léotard, was convicted and given a suspended two-year sentence and a
€100,000 fine; two intermediaries in the kickback scheme, Ziad Takieddine and Abdul Rahman
El-Assir, were separately sentenced to five years in prison each
([France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/france/20210304-france-acquits-ex-pm-balladur-in-karachi-corruption-trial-convicts-ex-defence-minister)).

## Death

Balladur died in Paris on 31 August 2026 at age 97. French Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu
said he "is closely associated with fifty years of the history of the Fifth Republic"
([GV Wire](https://gvwire.com/2026/08/31/former-french-pm-balladur-dies-at-97/)).
