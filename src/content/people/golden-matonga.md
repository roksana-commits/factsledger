## Overview

Golden Matonga is a Malawian investigative journalist based in Lilongwe, with 17 years of journalism experience. He serves as Director of Investigations at the Platform for Investigative Journalism-Malawi (PIJ-Malawi), and has held the roles of Vice-Chairperson of the Media Institute of Southern Africa (MISA) and Chairperson of MISA's Malawi chapter ([GIJN](https://gijn.org/stories/africa-focus-10-questions-golden-matonga/)).

## International investigative work

Matonga became a member journalist of the International Consortium of Investigative Journalists (ICIJ) in October 2021, and has since contributed to ICIJ's collaborative Pandora Papers and FinCEN Files investigations. His byline work has appeared in the Financial Times, The New York Times, Mail & Guardian and The Continent, including a June 2024 story on wealthy nations' opposition to a United Nations global tax plan and a November 2022 story on a drug-cartel figure's attempt to purchase Egyptian military aircraft ([ICIJ](https://www.icij.org/journalists/golden-matonga/); [GIJN](https://gijn.org/stories/africa-focus-10-questions-golden-matonga/)).

## Domestic reporting and fellowship

Domestically, Matonga has led investigations into corruption and abuse of power in Malawi, including a multi-part investigation into a UK businessman that reporting describes as having resulted in arrests by the UK's National Crime Agency. In 2023, he was named a Hubert Humphrey Fellow at Arizona State University. Reflecting on the obstacles facing investigative reporters, he has said: "Even where the law has created an enabling framework for journalists to access information, you still meet resistance from duty bearers to provide information, particularly when you're doing investigative journalism" ([GIJN](https://gijn.org/stories/africa-focus-10-questions-golden-matonga/)).
