## Overview

Ana Luz Porzecanski is a conservation biologist born in Uruguay and raised in rural Brazil
([Carnegie Corporation of New York](https://carnegie.org/great-immigrants/ana-luz-porzecanski/)). She holds a biology
degree from the Universidad de la República in Uruguay and graduate degrees, including a PhD in ecology and
evolutionary biology, from Columbia University
([American Museum of Natural History](https://www.amnh.org/research/staff-directory/ana-luz-porzecanski)). Since
2014 she has directed the Center for Biodiversity and Conservation at the American Museum of Natural History
([Carnegie Corporation of New York](https://carnegie.org/great-immigrants/ana-luz-porzecanski/)), where her work
focuses on understanding biodiversity and how to sustain it, and she has taught conservation biology and evolution
courses at New York University and Columbia University
([American Museum of Natural History](https://www.amnh.org/research/staff-directory/ana-luz-porzecanski)).

## Research

Porzecanski is the lead and corresponding author of "A systems framework for planning and evaluating capacity
development in conservation," published in the journal Oryx in 2022 with eleven co-authors from institutions
including the American Museum of Natural History and partner conservation organisations
([Oryx](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/oryx/article/systems-framework-for-planning-and-evaluating-capacity-development-in-conservation-recommendations-for-practitioners/4AB8EDA3E48812D69769C2EC2B88659F)).
She is also the second author, alongside Francisca C. Almeida, Joel L. Cracraft and Sara Bertelli, of a 2021 paper
in the Zoological Journal of the Linnean Society on the molecular phylogeny of tinamous, a group of South American
birds
([Zoological Journal of the Linnean Society](https://academic.oup.com/zoolinnean/article/195/1/106/6395171)).

## The "¡Cuba!" exhibition

In 2016, Porzecanski co-curated "¡Cuba!", a bilingual, binational traveling exhibition on Cuban biodiversity and
culture at the American Museum of Natural History. She told Newsweek the exhibition aimed to "take people beyond
the headlines to what they may not know or expect about Cuba," adding that "biodiversity of Cuba is also part of
Cuban identity"
([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/cuba-exhibit-american-museum-natural-history-524186)). The exhibition drew on a
2015 scientific expedition to Cuba's Alejandro de Humboldt National Park that she co-led; in an interview with
Mongabay about that expedition, she noted that "one of every two plants in Cuba are found only there," and that for
amphibians endemism runs even higher
([Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/2016/08/field-notes-expedition-to-cuba-seeks-out-new-and-endemic-species/)).

## Recognition and public engagement

The Carnegie Corporation of New York named Porzecanski a 2016 "Great Immigrants, Great Americans" honoree, an
annual recognition of naturalized U.S. citizens
([Carnegie Corporation of New York](https://carnegie.org/great-immigrants/ana-luz-porzecanski/)). In May 2021 she
appeared as a guest on PBS's Amanpour & Company to discuss pandemics and human ecology
([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/wnet/amanpour-and-company/guests/ana-porzecanski/)).
