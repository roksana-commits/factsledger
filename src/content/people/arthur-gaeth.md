## Overview

Arthur Gaeth (circa 1905–1984) led the first Latter-day Saint mission established in a Slavic country
before later becoming a radio journalist who reported live from one of the twentieth century's most
consequential trials. He was serving as a missionary in Germany in the late 1920s when, on 24 July 1929,
European Mission president John A. Widtsoe dedicated the land for missionary work in Czechoslovakia and
called the 24-year-old Gaeth to preside over the new Czechoslovak Mission
([Ensign Peak Foundation](https://ensignpeakfoundation.org/czechoslovak-mission-monument/)). He led the
mission until 1936, by which point 128 people had joined the Church under its direction — a number that
stopped growing once Nazi occupation began.

## From mission president to war correspondent

After the Second World War, Gaeth returned to Europe as a journalist covering post-war diplomacy, and in
1946 reported live from Nuremberg, Germany for the Mutual Broadcasting System on the execution of Nazi
leaders convicted of war crimes at the Nuremberg trials
([Brigham Young University Special Collections](https://archives.lib.byu.edu/repositories/ltpsc/resources/upb_mss1934)).
A recording of that broadcast survives and is independently catalogued by Stanford University's Hoover
Institution and by Stanford University Libraries — two separate archival records with no institutional
connection to the church Gaeth had served
([Hoover Institution](https://digitalcollections.hoover.org/objects/124/arthur-gaeth-radio-news-broadcast);
[Stanford SearchWorks](https://searchworks.stanford.edu/view/4086580)). His personal papers, spanning 1923
to 1983 — news reports, newspaper clippings, draft articles and photographs — are held at Brigham Young
University's L. Tom Perry Special Collections.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not assert a precise birth year: Brigham Young University's own finding aid for his
papers gives two different years (1905 and 1915), and this profile follows 1905 because a separate source
independently describes him as 24 years old at the time of his 1929 mission call.
