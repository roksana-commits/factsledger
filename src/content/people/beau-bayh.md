## Overview

Birch Evans "Beau" Bayh IV is a seventh-generation Hoosier, a U.S. Marine Corps veteran and
attorney. He is the son of Evan Bayh, a former Indiana governor and U.S. senator, and the
grandson of Birch Bayh, also a former U.S. senator — a family political lineage spanning
three prior generations of Indiana officeholders. He holds degrees from Harvard University,
served as a Marine Corps infantry officer, and worked as a judicial law clerk for a federal
appeals court judge until August 2025
([WTHR](https://www.wthr.com/article/news/politics/beau-bayh-son-former-indiana-governor-and-us-senator-evan-bayh-launches-campaign-indiana-secretary-of-state-diego-morales/531-c6f6f4a2-2cda-4fc2-a498-9be3b1f428e0)).

## 2026 Secretary of State campaign

On 6 October 2025, Bayh filed to run for Indiana Secretary of State as a Democrat,
challenging Republican incumbent Diego Morales. He framed his campaign around cleaning up
the office, citing Morales's hiring of a brother-in-law and questions about travel spending
and vehicle purchases. His platform included eliminating wasteful spending, expanding voter
access in a state that ranks 50th nationally in voter participation, ending straight-ticket
voting, and pledging to serve as a nonpartisan administrator
([WTHR](https://www.wthr.com/article/news/politics/beau-bayh-son-former-indiana-governor-and-us-senator-evan-bayh-launches-campaign-indiana-secretary-of-state-diego-morales/531-c6f6f4a2-2cda-4fc2-a498-9be3b1f428e0);
[Indiana Capital Chronicle](https://indianacapitalchronicle.com/2025/10/06/democrat-beau-bayh-to-run-for-indiana-secretary-of-state/)).

On 6 June 2026, Bayh won the Democratic nomination at the state convention, defeating
Blythe Potter with 61 percent of delegate votes (1,385 to 883). He entered the convention
with a substantial fundraising advantage, $1.9 million on hand compared with Potter's
$66,600, and campaigned on an independent audit of the secretary of state's office, expanded
voter participation, and stronger election security including voter ID support
([Indiana Capital Chronicle](https://indianacapitalchronicle.com/2026/06/06/beau-bayh-defeats-blythe-potter-for-democratic-secretary-of-state-nomination/)).

Speaking to a Lafayette television station a few months later, Bayh described the family
tradition behind his run: "I was raised in a family of public servants. And I was always
raised to believe that no one forces you to enter public life, but if you decide to do
that, you better do it to help your community, your state, and your country." He said the
secretary of state controls "the health of our democracy, our electoral process," and
named voter turnout, election security and reducing what he calls wasteful office spending
as his top priorities ([WLFI](https://www.wlfi.com/2026/08/12/meet-secretary-state-candidate-beau-bayh/)).

## Republican opponent and the general election

Bayh's Republican opponent was decided two weeks after his own nomination. At the state
GOP convention in Fort Wayne on 20 June 2026, Max Engling — a central Indiana regional
adviser to U.S. Senator Jim Banks — won the Republican nomination for secretary of state,
defeating Knox County Clerk David Shelton and eliminating incumbent Diego Morales, who
finished third with just 8.2 percent of the vote on the decisive second ballot (867 votes
for Engling and 627 for Shelton, out of 1,628 cast). Morales had led the first ballot's
field of four with backing from a smaller bloc of delegates but lost the support of Banks
and Attorney General Todd Rokita beforehand, both of whom withdrew their endorsements amid
controversy over Morales's staffing decisions. Engling said afterward, "We're not looking
at what the other folks are doing, we're moving forward as a Republican team," while
Morales called himself the "people's secretary of state" and said Republicans who had
endorsed him "suddenly stabbed me in the back." Bayh, for his part, characterized Engling's
nomination as the product of "political insiders pulling his strings"
([Indiana Capital Chronicle](https://indianacapitalchronicle.com/2026/06/20/republican/);
[The Indiana Citizen](https://indianacitizen.org/engling-nominated-republican-convention-delegates-boot-sos-morales-off-the-november-ballot/)),
which independently reported the same vote totals.

The November general election field also includes former Indianapolis Mayor Greg Ballard,
running under the Lincoln Party banner, and Libertarian candidate Lauri Shillings
([Indiana Capital Chronicle](https://indianacapitalchronicle.com/2026/08/11/beau-bayh-ad-kicks-off-potentially-expensive-indiana-secretary-of-state-campaign/)).

## Campaign advertising and fundraising

Bayh's campaign released its first television ad on 11 August 2026, a 30-second spot that
shows him target shooting with a rifle while describing his Marine Corps marksmanship
training; in it, he pledges to "expose every wasted dollar and no-bid contract" in the
secretary of state's office. The campaign described an "initial six-figure buy" on
television and digital advertising, with FCC filings showing purchases in the Indianapolis
and South Bend markets. As of the 30 June 2026 campaign-finance filing deadline, Bayh
reported roughly $2.3 million on hand — by that report the strongest financial position of
any Indiana Democratic statewide candidate in several years — compared with about $150,000
for Engling, who had entered the race only in May. The Engling campaign responded to
Bayh's early ad by saying "their campaign is behind, which is why they're getting ads up
early"
([Indiana Capital Chronicle](https://indianacapitalchronicle.com/2026/08/11/beau-bayh-ad-kicks-off-potentially-expensive-indiana-secretary-of-state-campaign/)).

By early September, Bayh's campaign had reported about $450,000 in additional large
contributions since 1 July, while Engling's campaign had raised roughly $200,000 in the
same period; the Indiana Republican Party then announced a $1 million advertising
commitment, split between the party and Governor Mike Braun's campaign, to help close the
gap, with Engling's own ads beginning to air about a month after Bayh's
([Indiana Capital Chronicle](https://indianacapitalchronicle.com/2026/09/10/indiana-republicans-starting-ads-for-engling-a-month-after-democrat-bayh-hit-the-airwaves/)).

## Candidate forum and policy positions

On 26 August 2026, Bayh joined Engling, Ballard and Shillings at the Indy Chamber's annual
HobNob forum in downtown Indianapolis, one of the first joint appearances of the general
election. Bayh, Ballard and Shillings all said they supported an independent audit of the
secretary of state's office; Engling was the only candidate who declined to commit to one.
On a federal proposal requiring additional voter-ID verification (the SAVE America Act),
Engling was the only candidate to back it in full, while Shillings called it a "barrier to
voting" that "disenfranchises people who absolutely should be able to vote" and Ballard
opposed it as federal overreach; Bayh also opposed the measure. The candidates also split
over a disputed recount involving improperly stamped absentee ballots in a state Senate
district (Senate District 23): Bayh, Ballard and Shillings said disputed ballots should be
counted based on voter intent, while Engling defended election officials who had rejected
them, saying they "followed the law." Bayh told the forum, "When the facts and the law are
on your side, maybe it does feel like three to one ... hopefully, folks saw our vision,"
and said of election administration generally that "when a voter goes into the voting
booth, they should have faith that their vote is counted if they've done everything right"
([Indiana Capital Chronicle](https://indianacapitalchronicle.com/2026/08/26/indiana-secretary-of-state-hopefuls-clash-over-voting-rules-office-audit/)).
