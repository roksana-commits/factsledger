## Overview

Rochelle Bilal is the Sheriff of Philadelphia, the first woman and first Black woman
elected to the office in its 181-year history. Before running for sheriff, she spent
nearly 20 years with the Philadelphia Police Department, leaving in 2013 amid an
investigation into a second job as public-safety director in Colwyn Borough, and led the
Guardian Civic League, an organization representing roughly 2,000 Black police officers
([The Philadelphia Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/news/philadelphia-sheriff-rochelle-bilal-foreclosure-sales-election-20190524.html);
[The Philadelphia Citizen](https://thephiladelphiacitizen.org/untouchable-sheriff-rochelle-bilal/)).
In 2009, she brought a whistleblower lawsuit that led to the shutdown of Domelights.com, a
message board where officers had posted racist content
([The Philadelphia Citizen](https://thephiladelphiacitizen.org/untouchable-sheriff-rochelle-bilal/)).

## Election

In 2019, running as a first-time candidate without the Democratic Party's formal
endorsement, Bilal won the Democratic primary for sheriff with 41% of the vote against
two-term incumbent Jewell Williams's 27%, campaigning as a reformer focused on foreclosure
prevention. She called the city's factional politics "disturbing" after several party
figures quietly continued backing Williams despite the Democratic City Committee having
rescinded his endorsement
([The Philadelphia Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/news/philadelphia-sheriff-rochelle-bilal-foreclosure-sales-election-20190524.html)).
She was sworn in on January 6, 2020, and was re-elected in November 2023 with 73.7% of the
vote ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Rochelle_Bilal)).

## Early controversies in office

Within 36 hours of Bilal's swearing-in, 101 firearms were found missing from the sheriff's
office armory; a follow-up count three years later found only 26 had been recovered. Bilal
attributed the losses to her predecessor's tenure. The Philadelphia Citizen's investigation
also reported a first-term firing of the office's chief financial officer, Brett Mandel,
after roughly 100 days, following his scrutiny of off-book spending; Mandel later settled a
wrongful-termination lawsuit for an undisclosed six-figure sum
([The Philadelphia Citizen](https://thephiladelphiacitizen.org/untouchable-sheriff-rochelle-bilal/)).
Bilal has characterized such criticism as political targeting rather than a reflection of
her office's performance, saying: "When you use words like 'scandal' or 'corruption,' it's
'got you' journalism. You're accusing me of a crime and yet I haven't been arrested"
([The Philadelphia Citizen](https://thephiladelphiacitizen.org/untouchable-sheriff-rochelle-bilal/)).

## 2024 campaign fabricated-news finding

In February 2024, Philadelphia Inquirer reporter William Bender found that Bilal's
re-election campaign website featured about 31 favorable "news" headlines attributed to
NBC10, CBS3, WHYY and the Inquirer itself — none of which those outlets could verify as
real published stories. NBC10 and WHYY both told the Inquirer they had no record of the
specific stories cited. The campaign initially did not respond, removed the page, then
restored it with a disclaimer making "no representations or warranties" about its accuracy
([The Philadelphia Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/news/rochelle-bilal-sheriff-campaign-news-20240205.html)).
NBC News independently confirmed the underlying facts and reported the campaign's later
acknowledgment that an outside consultant had used an AI service that "generated fake news
articles to support the initiatives that were part of the AI prompt"
([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/philly-sheriffs-campaign-takes-bogus-news-stories-posted-site-generate-rcna137534)).

## 2026 ICE remarks

On January 9, 2026, at a news conference with District Attorney Larry Krasner responding to
the fatal ICE shooting of Renee Nicole Good in Minneapolis, Bilal said of ICE agents
committing crimes in Philadelphia: "If any ICE agents want to come in this city and commit
a crime, you will not be able to hide," adding, "you don't want this smoke, cause we will
bring it to you." Video of the remarks circulated widely, drawing criticism from Fox News,
Newsweek, HuffPost and Pennsylvania Republican officials, and support from civil rights
attorney Ben Crump
([The Philadelphia Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/news/philadelphia/philadelphia-sheriffs-office-rochelle-bilal-immigration-customs-ice-20260110.html)).

## 2026 court order over sheriff's-sale delays

In May 2026, Judge Paula Patrick, who supervises the county court's commerce division,
ordered Bilal to address a chronic backlog in processing deeds following sheriff's sales,
finding the office had not been "timely performing its essential, nondiscretionary,
ministerial duties" — leaving buyers waiting a year or more for deeds and properties unable
to be renovated, rented or resold. In a July 2026 filing, deputy undersheriff Steven
Wakefield attributed the backlog to a former director of deeds and said all deeds had since
been issued. Bilal appeared before Judge Patrick in August 2026 to argue against the
appointment of a "special master" to oversee the office's auctions and deed processing
([The Philadelphia Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/news/sheriff-philadelphia-rochelle-bilal-auction-court-hearing-20260825.html)).
