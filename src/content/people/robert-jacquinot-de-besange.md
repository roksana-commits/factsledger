## Overview

Robert Jacquinot de Besange was a French Jesuit priest, born in 1878, who joined the
Society of Jesus in 1894 and was sent to Shanghai as a missionary in 1913. An accidental
explosion in 1914 cost him an arm, and he became known afterward as "the one-armed priest"
([La Civiltà Cattolica](https://www.laciviltacattolica.com/fr-robert-jacquinot-de-besange-and-the-shanghai-safe-zone-1937/)).

## Early humanitarian work

Before 1937, Jacquinot had already built a reputation for wartime civilian protection in
Shanghai: he helped shelter women and children during the city's 1927 civil conflict, and
negotiated a ceasefire during the 1931 "January 28th Incident" that helped rescue several
hundred refugees. By 1937 he was president of the Shanghai Chinese and Foreign Volunteer
Relief Association
([La Civiltà Cattolica](https://www.laciviltacattolica.com/fr-robert-jacquinot-de-besange-and-the-shanghai-safe-zone-1937/)).

## The Shanghai Safe Zone

As Japanese forces advanced on Shanghai in 1937, Jacquinot proposed a civilian refuge zone
to the city's mayor. The South District Safe Zone opened on 9 November 1937, covering about
a third of the old walled city, after Jacquinot negotiated directly with both Chinese and
Japanese military commanders to keep it free of fighting. TIME magazine, reporting on the
zone a year later, described him as a "square-bearded, black-robed, one-armed French
Jesuit" who had persuaded both sides to respect it; the magazine put the zone's peak
population at roughly 250,000 refugees, with about 100,000 still sheltering there in
November 1938
([TIME](https://time.com/archive/6779754/war-in-china-safety-zones/)).

Within the zone, Jacquinot organized nine administrative sections with elected leaders under
an international oversight committee, and it came to include its own police, schools,
factories and courts. He secured funding that included roughly $700,000 from the Roosevelt
administration, American wheat shipments, and $1 million from the American Red Cross
([La Civiltà Cattolica](https://www.laciviltacattolica.com/fr-robert-jacquinot-de-besange-and-the-shanghai-safe-zone-1937/)).

## Later life and legacy

In 1938, Jacquinot established a similar safe zone in Wuhan, sheltering roughly 40,000
people. He returned to Europe in June 1940 and continued refugee work through and after the
Second World War, before dying in Berlin on 10 September 1946, of leukemia, at 68. His
Shanghai model is credited with influencing the Nanking Safety Zone set up weeks later, and
more broadly with shaping the civilian-protection provisions later written into the 1949
Fourth Geneva Convention
([La Civiltà Cattolica](https://www.laciviltacattolica.com/fr-robert-jacquinot-de-besange-and-the-shanghai-safe-zone-1937/)).
