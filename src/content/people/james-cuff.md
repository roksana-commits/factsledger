## Overview

James Cuff is a high-performance-computing (HPC) research technologist whose career spans some of the most
prominent scientific-computing institutions in genomics and academia. Before joining Harvard, he built
computing infrastructure for the Ensembl genome-annotation project at the Wellcome Trust Sanger Institute and
worked at the European Bioinformatics Institute and the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard. He holds a D.Phil.
in Molecular Biophysics from the University of Oxford and a B.Sc. in Chemistry from the University of
Manchester ([insideHPC](https://insidehpc.com/2018/08/hpc-elder-james-cuff-joins-witnix-startup/)).

## Harvard research computing and the North East Storage Exchange

As Harvard's assistant dean and distinguished engineer for research computing, Cuff built a research-computing
organization spanning green HPC data centers, petascale storage and low-latency networks. In 2016 he led the
North East Storage Exchange (NESE), a regional research-storage system he said was designed to retrieve data
about ten times faster than equivalent commercial cloud storage, while remaining complementary to rather than
competing with the cloud: "This isn't a competitor to the cloud. It's a complementary cloud storage system"
([Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2016/11/for-bigger-data-more-storage/)).

## After Harvard

Cuff took early retirement from Harvard in 2017, then returned to the field in 2018 to join the startup Witnix,
and again in March 2022 to become Chief of Scientific Computing and Partnerships at GigaIO, a composable-computing
company, where he works on next-generation scientific-computing architecture. He described the move as addressing
a longstanding problem in his field: "configuration discrepancy and data locality have been challenges" throughout
his career ([Bio-IT World](https://www.bio-itworld.com/trends-from-the-trenches/james-cuff-on-computing-s-evolution-and-the-next-fascinating-challenge)).
