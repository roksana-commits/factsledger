## Overview

Jit Saxena is a Massachusetts-based technology entrepreneur who founded and led two publicly traded companies: Applix, Inc., beginning in 1983, and [Netezza](/organizations/netezza/), which he co-founded in September 2000. He holds a bachelor's degree in electrical engineering from the Indian Institute of Technology, Bombay, a master's degree in electrical engineering from Michigan State University, and an MBA from Boston University ([India New England News](https://indianewengland.com/chai-manju-entrepreneur-jit-saxena-talks-life-entrepreneurial-journey/)).

## Applix and early career

Before founding Applix, Saxena held senior management roles at Data General, according to a 2010 Actifio board-appointment release ([Actifio via Data Storage Connection](https://www.datastorageconnection.com/doc/actifio-names-industry-veteran-jit-saxena-to-0001)). He founded Applix, an analytical and, later, financial-performance software company based in Westborough, Massachusetts, in 1983, serving as its chairman and chief executive until 2000, and took the company public in 1994 ([MIT Martin Trust Center](https://entrepreneurship.mit.edu/profile/jit-saxena/)). Saxena had already left Applix by the time Cognos announced, on September 5, 2007, that it would acquire the company for approximately $339 million in cash, adding Applix's roughly 3,000 customers and its TM1 in-memory analytics software to its own business ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/enterprise-apps/cognos-to-acquire-applix-for-339-million/)).

## Netezza

In September 2000, Saxena co-founded Netezza, a Massachusetts data warehouse appliance maker, and served as its chief executive and a board member from its inception. Effective January 31, 2008, he was succeeded as chief executive by then-president and chief operating officer Jim Baum, remaining chairman of the board. IBM completed its acquisition of Netezza on November 11, 2010, for $27 a share, approximately $1.7 billion net of cash. Saxena's role as Netezza's founder is independently confirmed by both the Martin Trust Center for MIT Entrepreneurship and an India New England News interview with him ([MIT Martin Trust Center](https://entrepreneurship.mit.edu/profile/jit-saxena/); [India New England News](https://indianewengland.com/chai-manju-entrepreneur-jit-saxena-talks-life-entrepreneurial-journey/)).

## Later board and investment roles

After leaving Netezza's day-to-day leadership, Saxena joined a series of technology-company boards. In August 2010, Actifio announced he had joined its board of directors, with its chief executive saying the company was "very fortunate" that Saxena had accepted the invitation ([Actifio via Data Storage Connection](https://www.datastorageconnection.com/doc/actifio-names-industry-veteran-jit-saxena-to-0001)). On April 12, 2012, software-testing company uTest announced Saxena's addition to its board along with a personal investment in the company, with uTest's chief executive, Doron Reuveni, crediting him with "a track record of success, serving as CEO and founder of two companies that disrupted incumbents and went public" ([uTest via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2012/04/12/969096/0/en/uTest-Announces-Addition-of-Netezza-Founder-Jit-Saxena-to-Its-Board-of-Directors.html)). Marketing-automation company Mautic later announced that Saxena had joined its board and made a personal investment as well, alongside fellow board members Michael Skok and Bill Wiberg, with Mautic's chief executive, Matt Johnston, citing his "incredible track record of success" as "an entrepreneur, an investor and a board member of technology companies" ([Mautic via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mautic-adds-former-netezza-ceo-jit-saxena-to-its-board-of-directors-300470713.html)). Saxena has also served as a board member at venture firm Pillar VC, whose team page credits him with the same Applix and Netezza track record ([Pillar VC](https://www.pillar.vc/team/jit-saxena/)). Separately, the India New England News interview reports that Saxena, who lives with end-stage renal disease, has advocated for kidney-transplant awareness ([India New England News](https://indianewengland.com/chai-manju-entrepreneur-jit-saxena-talks-life-entrepreneurial-journey/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not repeat the 2009 acquisition year or $1.78 billion price given in two of Saxena's own board-appointment press releases, since they conflict with the independently corroborated November 2010 closing date and $1.7 billion price established elsewhere on this site (see "openItems" above). It does not repeat a 2003 date given in one interview for Cognos's acquisition of Applix, following instead an independent 2007 trade-press report. It does not independently verify Saxena's reported 2003 and 2008 award recognitions, which come from a single press release, and it does not attempt to list every board he has served on, since his later career includes numerous private-company board seats not all independently documented.
