## Overview

Evans Chan is a Hong Kong-born, New York-based independent filmmaker, critic and playwright whose work blends
documentary and fiction to examine overlooked historical moments through individual perspectives. His 1991 film
To_Liv(e), which explored post-Tiananmen anxiety in Hong Kong, was later named one of Time Out's "100 Greatest
Hong Kong Films" ([MCLC Resource Center](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/2023/09/25/evans-chan-retrospective/)).

## Themes and critical reception

Academic critic Kin-Yan Szeto describes Chan's cinema as concerned with postcolonialism, diaspora and
displacement, particularly around Hong Kong's 1997 handover to China, in films such as To_Liv(e) and Journey to
Beijing. Szeto situates Chan within a mode of "accented cinema" that demands "critical border thinking" beyond
conventional film-studies categories, noting the influence of Jean-Luc Godard and Susan Sontag on his work
([Jump Cut](https://www.ejumpcut.org/archive/jc57.2016/-SzetoEvansChan/index.html)).

## Later work and recognition

Chan's 2011 film Datong: The Great Society, about the reformer Kang Youwei, won the Chinese-language Movie of
the Year award from Southern Metropolitan Daily; the same year he released Chinatopia, on Kang Youwei's
suffragist daughter. His films have screened at Berlin, Rotterdam, London and other major international
festivals ([MCLC Resource Center](https://u.osu.edu/mclc/2023/09/25/evans-chan-retrospective/)).
