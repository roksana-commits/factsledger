## Overview

Kirsten Hughes chaired the Massachusetts Republican Party from January 2013 to January 2019. Before
entering party leadership she worked as a professional singer and actress — including a year-long run as
The Little Mermaid at Walt Disney World — before earning degrees in theater arts from NYU and law from the
New England School of Law, working in the Suffolk County district attorney's office, and serving as a
Quincy City Councilor
([The Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/metro/2013/02/02/quincy-singer-takes-less-glamorous-role-massgop-chairman/wpgRHQbdWDrRNE6JdEk6GP/story.html)).

## A contested election

Hughes was elected state party chairwoman in January 2013 at age 35, following Senator Scott Brown's
decision not to seek another term. Nearly half of the Republican State Committee voted against her, and
some of the votes she did receive came only at Brown's urging. She took office six weeks after giving
birth to her son, while continuing to serve on the Quincy City Council and searching for a Senate candidate
to succeed Brown. Party spokesman Tim Buckley said she "has routinely worked three jobs since she was 16,"
and a colleague, Janet Fogarty Kelley, called her "a little dynamo." Hughes herself said, "I always found
the Republican Party has been the party of the working people."
([The Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/metro/2013/02/02/quincy-singer-takes-less-glamorous-role-massgop-chairman/wpgRHQbdWDrRNE6JdEk6GP/story.html)).

## Six years as chair

Hughes led the party for six years, a tenure that included two gubernatorial victories for Governor
Charlie Baker, a net gain of 17 Republican state legislators, and more than $3.5 million in party spending
supporting GOP candidates — set against Republican losses in most other statewide races and three
legislative seats over the same period. She announced her departure on November 16, 2018, saying, "While
it has been my honor and privilege to serve our party, I have decided the time is right for me to pass the
torch to a new leader," and left office in January 2019
([WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2018/11/28/kirsten-hughes-massachusetts-gop-chair-leaving)).
