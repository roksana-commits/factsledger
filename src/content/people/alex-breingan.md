## Overview

Alex Breingan (also referred to in court records as Alexander Breingan) was the managing director of Stripe Media, a New Zealand television production company also operating under the name Stripe Studios, which financed and produced television programmes through a network of at least 14 associated entities ([B2B News NZ](https://b2bnews.co.nz/news/stripe-media-14-point-5m-sfo-case-governance-warning/)).

## Collapse and fraud charges

The company collapsed in 2024, leaving creditors owed more than $20 million; Breingan has said the operation employed roughly 250 contractors annually. In November 2023 the New Zealand Film Commission raised concerns about rebate claims tied to his productions, and in February 2024 New Zealand's Serious Fraud Office filed 33 fraud and forgery charges against him in Auckland District Court, each carrying a maximum penalty of seven to ten years' imprisonment. The SFO alleges Breingan made false representations and used forged documents to simultaneously obtain $4.3 million in government-funded rebates and $10.2 million in private lending — $6.91 million from Kiwibank and $3.26 million from Fulcrum Media Finance — across 13 television productions, for a total of $14.5 million. Receivers appointed in March 2024 identified financial irregularities in the collapsed entities. Breingan has denied the charges, saying television production financing in New Zealand "is complex," and has pleaded not guilty ([B2B News NZ](https://b2bnews.co.nz/news/stripe-media-14-point-5m-sfo-case-governance-warning/)).

## Departure and extradition proceedings

Breingan left for the United States in April 2024, about a month after learning his financial conduct had been referred to the SFO. An arrest warrant was issued in November 2025, and the SFO lodged a formal extradition application with the U.S. Department of Justice in May 2026. On 31 August 2026, appearing via video link from Los Angeles, Breingan sought to have the arrest warrant lifted and to appear remotely rather than in person; Judge David Clark dismissed the request, ruling that the risk of Breingan not voluntarily returning to New Zealand for trial was too high, since bail conditions would only be enforceable domestically. He was ordered to return to New Zealand immediately, with his next court appearance scheduled for 25 November 2026; the extradition application remained pending with the U.S. Department of Justice ([Scoop](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/AK2609/S00020/tv-producer-alexander-breingan-ordered-to-return-to-nz-to-face-charges.htm)).
