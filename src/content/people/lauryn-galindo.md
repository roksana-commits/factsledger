## Overview

Lauryn Galindo is an American former adoption facilitator who, working from Cambodia between 1990 and 2001, arranged the placement of roughly 700 to 800 Cambodian children with U.S. families — a scheme that later became the subject of a federal fraud prosecution documented in FactsLedger's [Cambodian adoption scandal](/events/cambodian-adoption-scandal/) profile. She is separately known for having facilitated actress Angelina Jolie's adoption of her son Maddox, and, decades later, for a Hawaii property dispute that reached the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit twice.

## Background and entry into adoption work

Before working in Cambodia, Galindo was, in [The Cambodia Daily](/organizations/the-cambodia-daily/)'s description, a semi-professional hula dancer in Hawaii ([The Cambodia Daily](https://english.cambodiadaily.com/2002/06/22/is-lauryn-galindo-a-baby-buyer-a-benefactor-or-both/)). She arrived in Cambodia in 1990 accompanying the actor Haing Ngor, who had been asked by Cambodian officials to help build a school; the newspaper reported that Galindo was impressed by Cambodian leaders' stated commitment to helping orphans, settled in Phnom Penh, and created what it described as the country's first formal adoption-documentation system, including registration forms, release letters and certifications of orphan status.

## An adoption business that grew through the 1990s

The Garden Island, a Kauai newspaper, reported that Galindo's placement business expanded dramatically over the following decade, from roughly 40 to 50 children a year in the early 1990s to about 100 a month by the time the United States suspended Cambodian adoptions in December 2001 ([The Garden Island](https://www.thegardenisland.com/2004/07/02/news/adoption-nightmare-continues-galindo-convicted-but-heartbreak-lives-on/)). On the U.S. side, her sister [Lynn Devin](/people/lynn-devin/) operated the agency Seattle International Adoptions, which screened prospective American families.

The Cambodia Daily's 2002 investigative profile — titled "Is Lauryn Galindo a Baby-Buyer, a Benefactor, or Both?" — described both sides of her reputation. Supporters credited her with funding five orphanages and donating roughly $600,000 a year to children's causes, including dental programs, literacy initiatives and school construction; two of her former drivers went on to found their own orphanages. The same article, however, reported a specific allegation from a Cambodian woman, Meas Bopha, who said that in 1997 Galindo took three of her recently born children for HIV tests without permission and then offered her $700 to give them up for adoption; Meas Bopha refused, though the paper reported that a neighbor gave up a child for $100 the same day. The Cambodia Daily described this as the only publicly known case directly implicating Galindo in trafficking at that time, and it did not report any financial contribution from Galindo to the senior Cambodian officials — including then-Senate President Chea Sim, whom Galindo described as "like a father to me" — that she counted as friends ([The Cambodia Daily](https://english.cambodiadaily.com/2002/06/22/is-lauryn-galindo-a-baby-buyer-a-benefactor-or-both/)).

## Facilitating Angelina Jolie's adoption of Maddox

Independent of the fraud case, Galindo is separately known for having facilitated actress Angelina Jolie's adoption of a Cambodian son, later named Maddox. Today.com, an NBC News property, reported in 2004 that Galindo "helped actress Angelina Jolie adopt her Cambodian baby son" ([Today.com/NBC News](https://www.today.com/popculture/jolies-adoption-agent-charged-fraud-wbna3916277)), and Vice's later reporting likewise states that Jolie adopted Maddox "as a seven-month-old in 2002" through Galindo ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/cambodian-adoption-fraud-united-states/)). This connection to a prominent celebrity adoption is part of why Galindo's case drew wider press attention than a typical adoption-fraud prosecution.

## Guilty plea and sentencing

On June 23, 2004, Galindo pleaded guilty in federal court in Seattle, before Magistrate Judge Mary Alice Theiler, to conspiracy to commit visa fraud, conspiracy to commit money laundering, and structuring financial transactions, stemming from the misrepresentation of Cambodian children's orphan status and identities on U.S. visa applications between January 1997 and December 2001 ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/archive/opa/pr/2004/June/04_crm_434.htm)). On November 19, 2004, U.S. District Judge Thomas Zilly sentenced her to 18 months in prison, ordered her to forfeit a $1.4 million home on Kauai, pay more than $60,000 in restitution to seven sets of adoptive parents, and complete 300 hours of community service ([The Spokesman-Review](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2004/nov/21/woman-gets-18-months-in-adoption-conspiracy/)). Separately, a Kauai resident, Summer Harrison, sued Galindo after adopting a daughter who had been represented as merely premature but who, Harrison said, actually had serious prenatal injuries; Harrison and another client each said they paid roughly $11,000 for their adoptions, including $3,500 in cash described as an "orphan donation fee" ([The Garden Island](https://www.thegardenisland.com/2004/07/02/news/adoption-nightmare-continues-galindo-convicted-but-heartbreak-lives-on/)). Vice later reported that Galindo denied wrongdoing, saying it was "the job of the Cambodians" to verify children's identities ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/cambodian-adoption-fraud-united-states/)).

## Later legal history: a Hawaii property dispute

More than a decade after her conviction, Galindo became a defendant in unrelated federal litigation in Hawaii over a Princeville, Kauai apartment. In 2010, [Daniel Susott](/people/daniel-susott/) — described in the case record as a close associate of Galindo's — transferred the property to her by quitclaim deed. Creditors of Susott sued in 2016, arguing the transfer was made to shield the property from debts he owed them; the district court agreed, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed on July 26, 2021 that Susott had made the 2010 transfer "with the actual intent to hinder, delay, or defraud" the plaintiffs ([Ninth Circuit memorandum, filed July 26, 2021](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/memoranda/2021/07/26/19-15537.pdf)). Within days of a related 2019 district court ruling, Susott deeded the same apartment to Galindo a second time, this time framed as compensation for her attorney's fees and emotional distress. The district court again found the transfer fraudulent, voided it, and imposed a constructive trust for the creditors' benefit; the Ninth Circuit affirmed that ruling on February 13, 2025, holding that Galindo remained bound by the earlier findings and had provided no reasonably equivalent value for the property ([Ninth Circuit via Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/23-16216/23-16216-2025-02-13.html); [U.S. District Court for the District of Hawaii via CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9665778/auld-susott-v-galindo/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not treat the Meas Bopha allegation, or any other unadjudicated trafficking claim reported by The Cambodia Daily, as a proven fact — it was never the subject of a separate criminal charge or civil judgment found in the sources read here, and is stated only as a reported allegation. It does not assert an audited or independently verified figure for Galindo's charitable giving, which is drawn from claims reported by The Cambodia Daily rather than from any accounting record. It does not resolve the exact month of Angelina Jolie's adoption of Maddox, where sources vary between late 2001 and March 2002, nor does it draw any conclusion about Maddox's own adoption beyond what is stated: that Galindo is independently reported to have facilitated it. It does not repeat a Wikipedia claim, already addressed and left unconfirmed in FactsLedger's main Cambodian adoption scandal profile, that Galindo made financial contributions to senior Cambodian officials — this profile's own independent source, The Cambodia Daily, likewise does not report any such contribution. Finally, this profile does not characterize Galindo's 2010-2025 Hawaii property litigation as connected in any way to the Cambodian adoption case; the court records describe it as a separate financial dispute between Galindo, Daniel Susott, and Susott's creditors.
