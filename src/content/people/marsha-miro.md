## Overview

Marsha Miro was a longtime art critic for the Detroit Free Press and, alongside gallery owner Susanne Hilberry, one of the driving forces behind the founding of the Museum of Contemporary Art Detroit (MOCAD). Beginning in the mid-1990s, Miro and Hilberry discussed the idea of a contemporary art museum for the city and approached the Detroit Institute of Arts (DIA) with a proposal to integrate a modern-art wing into the DIA's own collection ([Model D](https://modeldmedia.com/mocad67/)).

## From a rejected DIA proposal to MOCAD

After roughly five years of study, the DIA declined to fold modern works into its art-historical mission. DIA board chairman Richard Manoogian, however, remained enthusiastic about the underlying idea and committed Manoogian Foundation funds toward purchasing a building on Woodward Avenue. With the DIA path closed, Miro and Hilberry built an independent board of trustees and fundraising infrastructure to launch the museum on its own, with Miro serving as MOCAD's volunteer acting director during its founding and helping secure funding for its operations and inaugural exhibitions. The Model D profile describes Miro and Hilberry as "respected champions of the local art scene" with roots in Detroit's Cass Corridor art community dating to the 1960s and 1970s ([Model D](https://modeldmedia.com/mocad67/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile relies on a single independent source and covers Miro's role in MOCAD's founding as described there; it does not attempt a full account of her decades-long career as a critic at the Detroit Free Press.
