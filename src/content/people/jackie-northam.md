## Overview

Jackie Northam is an international correspondent who spent roughly four decades covering conflicts and
foreign affairs, about 18 of them at NPR. She began without formal journalism training: "I've never had
any formal education in journalism. I had no experience when I set off from northern Alberta, Canada, to
London," she said, having previously worked for the Canadian Broadcasting Corporation before her career
took her further afield ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/11/nx-s1-5866592/a-veteran-foreign-correspondent-looks-back-on-a-career-covering-conflicts)).

## Covering the Gulf War and the Rwandan genocide

In 1990, Northam covered the buildup to the Gulf War from Saudi Arabia, staying in the region for about
seven months ([NextTribe](https://nexttribe.com/magazine/war-correspondent/)). In April 1994, she
traveled to Rwanda four days after the genocide began, hitchhiking in from Burundi after airports closed,
alongside a French Associated Press photographer. At a checkpoint, Hutu militiamen holding machetes
mistook her for Belgian; she and her colleague talked their way past the threat, with his French-language
skills and rapport-building credited with the escape. She later described witnessing "pure butchery" in
Rwanda that "hurt my soul" ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/11/nx-s1-5866592/a-veteran-foreign-correspondent-looks-back-on-a-career-covering-conflicts); [NextTribe](https://nexttribe.com/magazine/war-correspondent/)).

## Afghanistan, Pakistan and a shift in beat

For several years, Northam made reporting trips to Afghanistan and Pakistan nearly every other month,
saying she worried "constantly about being kidnapped." Around 2015, after feeling "saturated by all the
turmoil, cruelty, and suffering" of three decades of conflict reporting, she moved toward an
international-affairs beat that gave her more control over her travel schedule, later covering Saudi
Arabia under Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman ([NextTribe](https://nexttribe.com/magazine/war-correspondent/)).

## Retirement

Northam retired from NPR in July 2026, prompting a career retrospective from the network on her four
decades of conflict reporting. She has said that the cumulative weight of covering violence and suffering
left her unable to consume sad or violent news content in her own time ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/11/nx-s1-5866592/a-veteran-foreign-correspondent-looks-back-on-a-career-covering-conflicts)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile draws on two interview-based, independent feature pieces published years apart; it does not
attempt a comprehensive list of every assignment or country Northam covered over a 40-year career.
