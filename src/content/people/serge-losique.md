## Overview

Serge Losique founded and, for decades, directed the Montreal World Film Festival, one of Montreal's longest-running international film events. In July 2018, Revenu Québec took Losique and the festival to court over roughly $500,000 in alleged back taxes; he paid close to $33,000 toward the debt so that year's festival could proceed as planned, though the broader dispute over the festival's public funding and financial transparency remained unresolved ([CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/montreal/article/serge-losique-pays-portion-of-back-taxes-so-montreal-world-film-festival-can-take-place/)).

## The Telefilm Canada dispute and a rival festival

The funding dispute stemmed from government officials' concerns that Losique had not been transparent about how his festival used public money. After losing government support, Losique sued Telefilm Canada for $2.5 million over the federal agency's backing of a new, competing festival in Montreal ([CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/entertainment/new-montreal-filmfest-folds-1.585164); [CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/entertainment/montreal-film-fest-sues-telefilm-to-stop-proposed-new-festival-1.473362)). He continued to run his own festival independently of the funding cut. When the rival "New Montreal FilmFest" folded after its own first year, reportedly losing $850,000, Losique called for "a public inquiry into the 'resounding and humiliating failure'" of the competing event, framing it as vindication of his festival's approach ([CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/entertainment/new-montreal-filmfest-folds-1.585164)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile covers a specific, well-documented 2018-2019 institutional dispute involving Losique, his festival's public funding, and a rival festival; it does not attempt a full account of the Montreal World Film Festival's multi-decade history or programming, which lies beyond what the sources read here establish.
