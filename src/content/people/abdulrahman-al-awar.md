## Overview

Dr Abdulrahman Al Awar has served as the United Arab Emirates' Minister of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MoHRE) since September 25, 2021, and additionally as Acting Minister of Higher Education and Scientific Research since July 14, 2024 ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/government/uae-cabinet-reshuffled-meet-the-new-ministers); [Al Etihad](https://en.aletihad.ae/news/uae/4500358/mohammed-bin-rashid-announces-the-appointment-of-hamdan-bin)). As MoHRE minister he oversees the country's push to raise the share of Emirati nationals working in the private sector. Under the mandatory Emiratisation policy, private companies employing 50 or more skilled staff must increase their Emirati hiring by 2% annually, calculated as roughly one Emirati hire for every 50 skilled employees per year ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/kt-exclusive-uae-minister-explains-mandatory-emiratisation-target-calculations-fines)).

## Career before the ministry

Al Awar holds a Ph.D. in Geology and Geochemistry from the Colorado School of Mines in the United States, awarded in 1995 ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/government/uae-cabinet-reshuffled-meet-the-new-ministers); [UAE Cabinet](https://uaecabinet.ae/en/fetch-council-members/his-excellency-dr-abdul-rahman-bin-abdulmanan-al-awar)). Before entering government, he held senior roles in the oil and gas, aluminium and banking sectors, including at Dubai Petroleum, Emirates Global Aluminium (DUBAL), Mubadala Investment, Dolphin Energy and HSBC. In 2009 he became Director-General of the Federal Authority for Government Human Resources (FAHR), a federal role he held before his 2021 cabinet appointment ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/government/uae-cabinet-reshuffled-meet-the-new-ministers)).

He was appointed Minister of Human Resources and Emiratisation in a UAE cabinet reshuffle announced on September 25, 2021, succeeding Nasser bin Thani Juma Al Hamli, and was sworn in along with the reshuffled cabinet on September 29, 2021 ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/government/uae-cabinet-reshuffled-meet-the-new-ministers); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/2021/09/29/leaders-attend-swearing-in-ceremony-of-new-uae-cabinet/); [UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://www.mofa.gov.ae/en/mediahub/news/2021/9/25/25-09-2021-uae-government)). On July 14, 2024, he was additionally named Acting Minister of Higher Education and Scientific Research, alongside his existing portfolio ([Al Etihad](https://en.aletihad.ae/news/uae/4500358/mohammed-bin-rashid-announces-the-appointment-of-hamdan-bin)).

## Emiratisation targets and enforcement

Emiratis employed in the private sector rose from 29,810 in 2021 to roughly 79,000 by July 2023, alongside a reported 27% year-on-year increase in 2022 compared with 2020, with about 85% of companies meeting their first-year targets ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/emiratisation-is-not-just-a-number-says-abdulrahman-al-awar-mohre-minister-1.1689172723503); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/kt-exclusive-uae-minister-explains-mandatory-emiratisation-target-calculations-fines)). Since January 1, 2023, companies that miss their targets face a fine of Dh72,000 annually for each unfilled Emirati position, monitored through a digital work-permit system linked to government entities. Al Awar has described the policy's goal as viewing Emiratis as productive contributors rather than numerical quotas, saying "we measure the objectives transparently, whether they are reached [or not]" ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/emiratisation-is-not-just-a-number-says-abdulrahman-al-awar-mohre-minister-1.1689172723503)).
