## Overview

Vladislav Mikosha (1909-2004) was a Soviet cinematographer and photojournalist born in Saratov, on the Volga River, to a ship captain's family. Health issues ruled out an early ambition toward a naval career, and in 1929 he enrolled at the State Film Technical School — later VGIK, the Soviet Union's main film school — despite having no prior photography experience ([Cineuropa](https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/262415/); [Multimedia Art Museum, Moscow](https://mamm-mdf.ru/exhibitions/heroic-sevastopol/)).

## Early career

From 1931, Mikosha worked as a cinematographer, documenting major Soviet events including the demolition of Moscow's Cathedral of Christ the Saviour and the 1934 rescue of the Chelyuskin expedition. In 1936 he moved into photography as a staff correspondent for the newspaper Izvestia, and a solo exhibition of his photographic work opened in Moscow on 1 May 1940 ([Multimedia Art Museum, Moscow](https://mamm-mdf.ru/exhibitions/heroic-sevastopol/)).

## Wartime work in Sevastopol

During the Second World War, Mikosha returned to filmmaking as a front-line cinematographer while continuing as a Pravda correspondent, documenting the Soviet defense and later liberation of Sevastopol between 1942 and 1944. His photographs from the siege — later exhibited by the Multimedia Art Museum, Moscow — are described as combining documentary record with compositional and emotional depth, capturing figures including the sniper Lyudmila Pavlichenko ([Multimedia Art Museum, Moscow](https://mamm-mdf.ru/exhibitions/heroic-sevastopol/)). In 1943, while visiting to exhibit his Sevastopol photographs, he met Ingrid Bergman, Gary Cooper and Hedy Lamarr, and received an invitation from Charlie Chaplin ([Cineuropa](https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/262415/)).

## Later recognition

Mikosha later worked for Pravda and contributed color photographs to early Soviet postage stamps, and was recognized as an early practitioner of color photography in the Soviet Union. In 2014, the Swedish director Gunilla Bresky released "I Stop Time," a documentary about his life with actor Jonas Karlsson voicing Mikosha, produced by Jan Blomberg for Bob Film Sweden; it won Best Swedish Documentary at Stockholm's Tempo Documentary Festival in March 2014 ([Cineuropa](https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/262415/)).
