## Overview

Sam Sidiqi was born in Afghanistan and spent part of his childhood in Anchorage, Alaska, attending Huffman Elementary School for a year before going on to graduate from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. He founded Afghan Peaks, a program aimed at improving mountain safety in Afghanistan through avalanche education, weather-monitoring systems, and sporting initiatives; the organization has sponsored the first woman's ascent of central Afghanistan's tallest peak and supports ski competitions in the country ([WebCenter Fairbanks](https://www.webcenterfairbanks.com/2021/05/13/afghanistan-born-mit-grad-raises-money-awareness-with-denali-climb/)).

## The Denali climb

Sidiqi planned a 2020 attempt to summit Denali, North America's tallest peak, to raise funds and awareness for Afghan Peaks, but the COVID-19 pandemic forced a postponement that gave him additional training time. In May 2021, ahead of a renewed attempt, he said he believed he would be "the first Afghanistan-born climber ever to summit Denali" if successful, citing both personal nostalgia for Alaska and a commitment to supporting his homeland's mountain communities ([WebCenter Fairbanks](https://www.webcenterfairbanks.com/2021/05/13/afghanistan-born-mit-grad-raises-money-awareness-with-denali-climb/)).

On June 14, 2021, Sidiqi reached the summit after a difficult final stretch. He later described doubts near the end of the climb — "I had some doubts there at the end... After I did that I couldn't eat, I could barely eat... I just didn't think I was going to go" — crediting his teammates' encouragement and ibuprofen with helping him persevere to the top ([Alaska's News Source](https://www.alaskasnewssource.com/2021/06/14/reaching-summit-sam-sidiqi-first-afghanistan-born-mountaineer-top-denali/)).
