## Overview

Helen Toner is an Australian-born artificial intelligence policy researcher. Raised and educated in Melbourne, where she earned a bachelor's degree in chemical engineering and a diploma in languages from the University of Melbourne, she relocated to the United States in 2015 and later earned a master's degree in security studies from Georgetown University ([Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/leadership/meet-helen-toner-the-aussie-holding-openais-altman-to-account/); [CSET](https://cset.georgetown.edu/staff/helen-toner/)). She is best known for two things: as an independent member of [OpenAI](/organizations/openai/)'s board from 2021 to 2023, she was one of the board members who voted to remove [Sam Altman](/people/sam-altman/) as chief executive in November 2023, and she later gave a detailed public account of the board's reasoning ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/meet-openai-board-members-ousted-sam-altman); [Fox Business, May 2024](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/former-openai-board-member-reveals-why-sam-altman-ousted-last-november)).

## Career and CSET

Before moving into AI policy work full-time, Toner worked as a senior research analyst at Open Philanthropy, advising policymakers and grantmakers on AI strategy, and spent time in Beijing as a research affiliate at Oxford University's Centre for the Governance of AI, studying China's AI ecosystem ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/meet-openai-board-members-ousted-sam-altman); [Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/leadership/meet-helen-toner-the-aussie-holding-openais-altman-to-account/)). Sources read for this profile do not agree on the precise order of these roles relative to her Georgetown degree; see "What this profile does not claim."

Toner has been at Georgetown's Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET) since its founding in 2019, most recently as Director of Strategy and Foundational Research Grants ([CSET](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/helen-toner-named-interim-executive-director/)). In October 2023, she co-authored a CSET report with Andrew Imbrie and Owen Daniels, "Decoding Intentions: Artificial Intelligence and Costly Signals," examining how governments and companies can credibly signal their intentions around AI development ([CSET](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/decoding-intentions/)). On August 26, 2025, CSET announced Toner as its interim executive director, succeeding Dewey Murdick, effective September 2, 2025 ([CSET](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/helen-toner-named-interim-executive-director/)).

## OpenAI board tenure and the November 2023 crisis

OpenAI announced Toner's appointment to its board on September 8, 2021, with Altman saying she "brings an understanding of the global AI landscape with an emphasis on safety" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/helen-toner-joins/)). On November 17, 2023, OpenAI's board removed Altman as CEO. Toner later said on the TED AI Show that the board numbered four voting members at the time and that "all four of us who fired him came to the conclusion that we just couldn't believe things that Sam was telling us" ([TED AI Show transcript](https://www.ted.com/pages/what-really-went-down-at-openai-and-the-future-of-regulation-w-helen-toner)). When OpenAI reinstated Altman on November 21, 2023, its new board consisted of Bret Taylor, Larry Summers, and returning member Adam D'Angelo — Toner was not retained ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/11/22/sam-altman-returns-ceo-new-board-bret-taylor-larry-summers)). Her departure from the board was publicly confirmed in the following days, in late November 2023 ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/openai-ditched-only-2-women-165119251.html)).

On December 7, 2023, Toner told Fortune that an OpenAI lawyer had warned her that pursuing Altman's removal could cause "the company's downfall" and that she would breach her fiduciary duty if she did not resign immediately; she said the board's decision was about its ability to supervise the company, not primarily about AI safety, adding, "our goal in firing Sam was to strengthen OpenAI and make it more able to achieve its mission" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/12/07/helen-toner-openai-board-member-speaks-out-sam-altman-intimidation)).

### The 2024 public account

In May 2024, Toner gave the most detailed public account of the board's reasoning. On May 26–27, she and fellow former board member Tasha McCauley published an op-ed in The Economist alleging that senior OpenAI leaders had described a "toxic culture of lying" at the company and conduct that could be "characterised as psychological abuse" ([Capital Brief](https://www.capitalbrief.com/article/openais-former-australian-board-member-claims-toxic-culture-of-lying-was-behind-sam-altman-firing-291133b8-e41c-4e3a-92e3-fadaba71a733/)). Days later, on the TED AI Show, Toner said the board first learned OpenAI had launched ChatGPT "on Twitter" rather than being informed in advance, that Altman had not disclosed his personal ownership of the OpenAI Startup Fund while describing himself as an independent board member, and that he had given the board "inaccurate information about the small number of formal safety processes" the company had ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/former-openai-board-member-reveals-why-sam-altman-ousted-last-november); [TED AI Show transcript](https://www.ted.com/pages/what-really-went-down-at-openai-and-the-future-of-regulation-w-helen-toner)).

Toner also connected the firing to her own report: after CSET published "Decoding Intentions," which Altman reportedly perceived as critical of OpenAI's safety approach relative to a rival lab, she said Altman "started lying to other board members in order to try and push me off the board" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/05/29/ex_openai_board_member_accuses_altman_lying/)). In the same interview, discussing an unrelated, earlier episode — Altman's 2019 departure from [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/) — Toner said the real reasons for that departure were "hushed up at the time"; this quote concerns the 2019 Y Combinator exit specifically, not the 2023 OpenAI board decision ([TED AI Show transcript](https://www.ted.com/pages/what-really-went-down-at-openai-and-the-future-of-regulation-w-helen-toner)).

OpenAI responded to Toner's May 2024 statements by saying it was "disappointed that Miss Toner continues to revisit these issues," and pointed to an internal review it said had concluded that the prior board's decision "was not based on concerns regarding product safety or security, the pace of development, OpenAI's finances, or its statements to investors, customers, or business partners" ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2024/05/29/sam-altman-reputation-helen-toner-openai-chatgpt)). This profile states both Toner's account and OpenAI's response without adjudicating between them.

After leaving OpenAI's board, Toner continued her CSET research, telling Axios in June 2024 that AI risk analysis should look beyond narrow catastrophic scenarios to a broader range of "civilization-scale bad outcomes," and that the technology's development remained too opaque, "being developed and pushed forward by a very small group of people" ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/06/05/artificial-intelligence-helen-toner-openai-cset)).

## Musk v. OpenAI trial testimony

Toner was a witness in the federal trial of [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk/)'s lawsuit against OpenAI, Altman, and Microsoft, which began April 27, 2026, in Oakland, California ([Local News Matters](https://localnewsmatters.org/2026/04/27/musk-v-altman-day-1-jury-selected-as-blockbuster-openai-trial-begins-in-oakland/)). PBS NewsHour reported that the roughly three-week trial included testimony from Musk, Altman, [Greg Brockman](/people/greg-brockman/), Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella, and "several witnesses including two ex-board members, Helen Toner and Tasha McCauley," who "said there were concerns about Altman's truthfulness." On May 18, 2026, a jury unanimously dismissed all of Musk's claims, finding he had waited too long to sue ([PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/jury-sides-with-openai-saying-elon-musks-lawsuit-was-not-filed-on-time)).
