## Overview

Reuven Yaron was born in Vienna, Austria, in 1924 and emigrated to Palestine in 1939. He
began his academic career teaching jurisprudence at the University of Aberdeen in 1956-57,
before joining the Hebrew University of Jerusalem in 1957, where he taught Roman law and,
from 1962, ancient Near Eastern law. He became dean of the university's Faculty of Law in
1967
([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/yaron-reuven)).

Beyond his academic posts, Yaron directed the Jewish National and University Library in
Jerusalem from 1973 to 1978 and later headed the Israel Broadcasting Authority.

## Scholarship

Yaron specialized in ancient Near Eastern, biblical and Roman law, working across source
analysis, philology and comparative legal history. His major English-language works include
*Gifts in Contemplation of Death in Jewish and Roman Law* (1960), *Introduction to the Law
of the Aramaic Papyri* (1961), and *The Laws of Eshnunna* (1969, revised 1988), alongside
Hebrew-language studies of the Elephantine papyri and Roman law. In 1995, the Israel Law
Review published a full retrospective bibliography of his work — five books and 74 journal
articles and book chapters spanning 1955 to 1995 — reflecting his standing as a leading
historian of ancient Near Eastern and Roman law
([Israel Law Review / Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/israel-law-review/article/abs/professor-reuven-yaron-selected-list-of-publications/6FE118AB80D2B69F0DB6DE3B63A71FB2)).

Yaron died in 2014.
