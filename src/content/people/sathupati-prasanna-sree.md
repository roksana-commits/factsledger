## Overview

Sathupati Prasanna Sree is a linguist and professor of English who, in February 2025, became
Vice-Chancellor of [Adikavi Nannaya University](/universities/adikavi-nannaya-university/) in
Rajamahendravaram, Andhra Pradesh, moving from a professorship in the English department at Andhra
University in Visakhapatnam ([The Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/andhra-pradesh/prof-prasanna-sree-appointed-as-vice-chancellor-of-aknu-947039);
[Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/andhra-pradesh/new-vcs-named-for-9-ap-universities-1862038)).
The Week, reporting on her appointment, described her as the first tribal woman to hold a
vice-chancellorship in the Telugu-speaking states. She belongs to the Yerakala tribal community and grew
up in Stuartpuram, a settlement near Guntur originally established by British colonial authorities under
the Criminal Tribes Act of 1871 for communities officially designated as "criminal tribes" ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/magazine/theweek/statescan/2025/06/06/s-prasanna-sree-andhra-pradeshs-first-tribal-woman-vice-chancellor-has-created-scripts-for-19-tribal-languages.html)).

## Tribal-language scripting work

Independent of her academic administrative career, Prasanna Sree is known for creating written scripts
for tribal languages that previously existed only in oral form. The Week reports that she has developed
scripts for 19 tribal languages over roughly three decades, a figure corroborated by Adikavi Nannaya
University's own biographical note, though the two sources do not list an identical set of languages (see
openItems). Speaking to ETV Bharat after receiving the Ramoji Excellence Award 2025, she described her
motivation in terms of protecting tribal cultural identity against the pressure of dominant languages,
saying "the pressure of other languages is weighing on our tribal identity" ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/ramoji-excellence-awards-other-languages-are-weighing-on-our-tribal-identity-says-sathupati-prasanna-sree-enn25111702736)).
Her work has focused on tribal communities in Andhra Pradesh as well as Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha and
Madhya Pradesh, according to the same report.

## Recognition

Prasanna Sree was named among the recipients of the Nari Shakti Puraskar, a national award recognising
women's contributions to society, at a ceremony presided over by then-President Ram Nath Kovind on
International Women's Day, 8 March 2022. Greater Kashmir's report on the ceremony lists her specifically,
describing her as "a Linguist – preserving minority tribal languages" from Andhra Pradesh, and confirms
that the award was one of fourteen designated for the year 2021 and presented as part of a combined 2020–21
ceremony held in 2022 ([Greater Kashmir](https://www.greaterkashmir.com/uncategorized/president-confers-nari-shakti-puraskars-on-29-women/)).
This resolves an apparent conflict between sources that describe the award as being from "2021" and others
that describe it as "2022" — both refer to the same honour, distinguished only by award year versus
presentation year.

In November 2025, she received the Ramoji Excellence Award 2025 in the art-and-culture category, presented
by Vice-President of India CP Radhakrishnan, for her work developing scripts for endangered tribal languages
([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/ramoji-excellence-awards-other-languages-are-weighing-on-our-tribal-identity-says-sathupati-prasanna-sree-enn25111702736)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not assert a single definitive count or list of the tribal languages for which she has
created scripts, since independent sources and the university's own biographical note give overlapping but
inconsistent figures and language names. It does not assert a specific total number of years of academic
service, since The Week, The Hans India and the university's own note give three different figures (34, 38,
and "34 years beginning 1987," which are not all mutually consistent as of 2025). It does not assert a
specific subject for her master's degree, since The Week and the university's own note conflict on whether
it was in archaeology and history or in English. No claim in this profile rests solely on the university's
own biographical note where that note conflicts with independent reporting.
