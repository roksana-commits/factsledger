## Overview

Minnie F. Abrams (1859-1912) was an American Methodist missionary, evangelist and revivalist who spent most of her career in India and whose writing on Spirit baptism is credited with helping spark the Pentecostal movement in Chile.

## Missionary career in India

Abrams trained at the Chicago Training School for Home and Foreign Missions before arriving in India in 1887 with the Woman's Foreign Missionary Society of the Methodist Episcopal Church ([Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/missiology/missionary-biography/a-c/abrams-minnie-f-1859-1912/)). In 1898 she joined Pandita Ramabai's undenominational Mukti mission, where she became involved in the spiritual revival movement sweeping the mission and promoted Wesleyan holiness theology, traveling widely in India to advance revival ([Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/missiology/missionary-biography/a-c/abrams-minnie-f-1859-1912/); [The United Methodist Church](https://www.umc.org/en/content/abrams-minnie-f-1859-1912)).

## "The Baptism of the Holy Ghost and Fire" and influence in Chile

In 1906, Abrams published "The Baptism of the Holy Ghost and Fire," articles arguing that baptism in the Holy Spirit was a distinct work of grace occurring after conversion; these first appeared in major Indian Christian newspapers before being compiled into a book ([Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/missiology/missionary-biography/a-c/abrams-minnie-f-1859-1912/)). Both sources consulted for this profile state that a copy of this work reached Methodist missionaries May and Willis Hoover in Chile, and that the revival which followed there led to the founding of the Methodist Pentecostal Church and the wider Pentecostal movement in that country ([The United Methodist Church](https://www.umc.org/en/content/abrams-minnie-f-1859-1912)).

Abrams died in India in 1912, having continued missionary and evangelistic work until her death ([The United Methodist Church](https://www.umc.org/en/content/abrams-minnie-f-1859-1912)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not independently trace the causal chain from Abrams's 1906 publication to the founding of Chile's Methodist Pentecostal Church beyond what both institutional sources used here consistently state; that claim is not verified against a Chilean primary source.
