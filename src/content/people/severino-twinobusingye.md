## Overview

Severino Twinobusingye is a Ugandan senior advocate best known for a single unprecedented legal-fee award and for
representing opposing sides of the country's biggest political disputes at different points in his career. Born
21 September 1969 in Kigarama Village, Kanungu District
([Twinobusingye Severino & Co. Advocates](https://www.tseverinoadvocates.com/attorneys/twinobusingye-severino/)),
he worked in legal and management roles at Uganda's Electoral Commission for roughly eight years before founding
his own firm, M/s Twinobusingye Severino & Co. Advocates, in 2012
([Monitor](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/special-reports/shs13b-given-to-me-for-mbabazi-case-was-peanuts-says-lawyer-1537700)).

## The Shs12.9 billion case

In 2011, Twinobusingye filed Constitutional Petition No. 47, challenging a parliamentary ad hoc committee's
authority to investigate Prime Minister Amama Mbabazi and two ministers over alleged oil-bribery. The
Constitutional Court ruled the committee unconstitutional, and Twinobusingye was subsequently awarded
approximately UGX 12.9 billion in legal fees — a sum he told the Monitor was, relative to his practice, actually
"peanuts," while critics including the Deputy Attorney General and a Commercial Court judge publicly disputed
its size and propriety; Twinobusingye rejected their characterizations in the same report
([Monitor](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/special-reports/shs13b-given-to-me-for-mbabazi-case-was-peanuts-says-lawyer-1537700)).
In 2016, he was named as part of Mbabazi's legal team in Presidential Election Petition No. 1, which challenged
the declared re-election of Yoweri Museveni; the Monitor's court coverage names him directly, reporting "For the
petitioner, we have Mohammed Mbabazi as lead counsel, assisted by Micheal Akampurira, Asuman Basalirwa, Severino
Twinobusingye, and Jude Byabakama"
([Monitor](https://www.monitor.co.ug/News/National/Legal-minds-flex-in-court-as-Mbabazi-seeks--to-quash/-/688334/3107230/-/x21gxvz/-/index.html)).

## Karamoja Peace and Technology University

Twinobusingye leads the Catholic Lawyers Society International (CLASI), which in 2023 launched a drive to build
a university in Uganda's Karamoja sub-region ([Monitor](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/catholic-lawyers-launch-drive-to-build-varsity-in-karamoja-4218644)).
He now serves as the resulting institution's vice president and university council chairperson. In 2026, Uganda's
National Council for Higher Education publicly found that the Karamoja Peace and Technology University's
governance structure did not meet accreditation requirements; Twinobusingye rejected the finding as "deliberately
designed to mislead" stakeholders and maintained the university had secured substantial government and
international funding and complied with its interim operating authority
([Monitor](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/nche-karamoja-university-promoters-clash-over-accreditation-5426282)).
This dispute was unresolved as of the reporting found for this profile.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not adjudicate the 2026 accreditation dispute between KAPATU and Uganda's National Council for
Higher Education — both sides' claims are reported as stated, not as settled fact. It does not take a position
on the propriety of the 2013 legal-fee award, reporting only that the award was made and that it was publicly
disputed at the time.
