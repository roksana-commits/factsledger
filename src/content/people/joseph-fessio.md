## Overview

Joseph Fessio is an American Jesuit priest best known as a co-founder of [Ignatius Press](/organizations/ignatius-press/), the San Francisco Catholic publishing house, and as a longtime editor of the English-language works of Pope Benedict XVI. He graduated from Santa Clara University in 1962 and entered the Society of Jesus, later studying theology in Germany under Joseph Ratzinger — the future Pope Benedict XVI — at the University of Regensburg from 1972 to 1975 ([SFGate](https://www.sfgate.com/bayarea/article/RELIGION-The-pope-has-a-friend-in-local-priest-2669665.php); [Santa Clara Magazine](https://magazine.scu.edu/magazines/spring-2025/jesuit-words-jesuit-works/)). That relationship shaped much of his later career: the San Francisco Chronicle described him as having become "the pope's primary editor for the English-speaking world," publishing more than 20 works by or about Ratzinger through Ignatius Press ([SFGate](https://www.sfgate.com/bayarea/article/RELIGION-The-pope-has-a-friend-in-local-priest-2669665.php)).

## Founding the St. Ignatius Institute, and a first removal

After returning from Europe, Fessio helped establish the St. Ignatius Institute at the University of San Francisco, a traditionalist "great books" program that a 2001 San Francisco Chronicle report described as roughly 25 years old at that point, placing its founding around the mid-1970s ([SFGate](https://www.sfgate.com/education/article/Pope-s-Help-Sought-in-Theology-Clash-at-USF-2937880.php)). The Institute drew tension with USF's more liberal theology faculty, and the Chronicle reported that provincial Fr. John LoSchiavo removed Fessio as the Institute's director in the late 1980s amid "management differences" ([SFGate](https://www.sfgate.com/bayarea/article/RELIGION-The-pope-has-a-friend-in-local-priest-2669665.php)). The dispute resurfaced in January 2001, when USF president Fr. Stephen Privett dismissed the Institute's two longtime co-directors, John Galten and John Hamlon, prompting all six of the program's core faculty to resign in protest and appeal to Vatican officials; Fessio and Institute loyalists mounted a public campaign against the reorganization, though the USF board of trustees voted 30-2 to support Privett, who separately disputed Fessio's claim that the pope was personally monitoring the situation ([SFGate](https://www.sfgate.com/education/article/Pope-s-Help-Sought-in-Theology-Clash-at-USF-2937880.php); [National Catholic Register](https://www.ncregister.com/news/the-late-great-st-ignatius-institute)).

## Campion College and a Jesuit reassignment

Roughly a year after the 2001 reorganization, Fessio and Ignatius Press announced the founding of a new, independent Campion College of San Francisco nearby. Within weeks, California Jesuit provincial Fr. Thomas Smolich wrote to Fessio that "Campion College was not and is not part of your assignment from the Society of Jesus," formally barring his involvement and reassigning him as assistant chaplain at Santa Teresita Hospital in Duarte, California, effective May 1, 2002 ([National Catholic Register](https://www.ncregister.com/news/father-fessio-barred-at-san-francisco-college)).

## Two dismissals from Ave Maria University

Fessio later became provost of Ave Maria University, a Catholic university in Naples, Florida. On March 21, 2007, the university asked him to step down, citing only "irreconcilable differences over administrative policies and practices"; Catholic Review reported that Fessio, described in the piece as "the 66-year-old priest," said he was given no specific reason, and he agreed to stay on in a non-administrative role as theologian in residence ([Catholic Review](https://www.archbalt.org/father-fessio-dismissed-as-provost-of-ave-maria/)). He was dismissed a second time on July 20, 2009, after telling board chairman Jack Donahue in a November 2008 conversation that the university faced financial risk without a change in administration; Religion News Service and National Catholic Register both reported that the university offered no detailed public explanation beyond saying unnamed faculty felt undermined by his comments, while Fessio maintained he was "not an instigator" and called the dismissal "another mistake in a long series of unwise decisions" ([Religion News Service](https://religionnews.com/2009/07/22/the-rev-joseph-fessio-a-close-friend-of-pope-benedict-xvi-has-been-fired-fo/); [National Catholic Register](https://www.ncregister.com/blog/father-fessio-fired-from-ave-maria-university-a-second-time)). He said he planned to devote his time afterward to Ignatius Press, the publishing house he had co-founded three decades earlier ([Religion News Service](https://religionnews.com/2009/07/22/the-rev-joseph-fessio-a-close-friend-of-pope-benedict-xvi-has-been-fired-fo/)).

Away from these institutional disputes, a California Catholic Daily feature marking Ignatius Press's 40th anniversary described Fessio as "the founder and director of the press" with outside interests in farming and winemaking in Sonoma County ([California Catholic Daily](https://www.cal-catholic.com/ignatius-press-where-it-stands-in-catholic-america/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state an exact birth date: the only independently fetched data point is a March 2007 report describing Fessio as "the 66-year-old priest," which implies a birth year of 1940 or 1941 but is not treated here as a confirmed date. It does not give an exact founding date for the St. Ignatius Institute, relying instead on a 2001 newspaper's approximate "25 years" reference. It also does not adjudicate the substance of any of the three institutional disputes described above — whether at the St. Ignatius Institute, Campion College, or Ave Maria University — beyond reporting what each side is independently reported to have said; in each case, the university or provincial side generally declined to give a detailed public reason, and this profile does not speculate about the missing specifics.
