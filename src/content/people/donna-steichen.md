## Overview

Donna Steichen is an American Catholic author from St. Cloud, Minnesota, who later settled in Ojai, California. During the 1970s and 1980s she worked as a Catholic journalist and grew concerned about feminism's influence on American Catholic institutions, attending religious feminist conferences — often undercover, by her own account — as part of her research ([Catholic World Report](https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2011/05/31/incalculable-damage/)). Catholic World Report also reported that she married her husband roughly 60 years before its 2011 profile was published and that the couple had four children, and it described her more broadly as an activist in the pro-life movement and in religious education alongside her journalism ([Catholic World Report](https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2011/05/31/incalculable-damage/)).

## "Ungodly Rage"

In 1991, [Ignatius Press](/organizations/ignatius-press/) published Steichen's book "Ungodly Rage: The Hidden Face of Catholic Feminism," based on the articles and research she had compiled over the preceding decade. The book became an unexpected bestseller for a first-time author, selling roughly 50,000 copies and leading to international speaking engagements across North America, Europe, Australia, New Zealand and the Philippines. The book was documented with more than 500 footnotes. According to Catholic World Report, orthodox Catholics — including Cardinal John O'Connor, who discussed it publicly — received the work favorably, while bishops, Church bureaucracy and feminist organizations reacted with strong opposition ([Catholic World Report](https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2011/05/31/incalculable-damage/)). Independently, the National Catholic Register has continued to cite "Ungodly Rage" as a reference point in the wider debate over feminism's influence on Catholic teaching and institutions, decades after its original publication ([National Catholic Register](https://www.ncregister.com/news/feminism-below-the-surface)).

## Other Books and Writing

Beyond "Ungodly Rage," Steichen edited "Prodigal Daughters: Catholic Women Come Home to the Church" (Ignatius Press, 1998), a collection of Catholic women's conversion narratives, and later "Chosen: How Christ Sent Twenty-Three Surprised Converts to Replant His Vineyard" (Ignatius Press, 2009) ([Ignatius Press](https://ignatius.com/authors/donna-steichen/)). Crisis Magazine, an independent Catholic commentary outlet, separately identifies "Chosen" as among her more recent work ([Crisis Magazine](https://crisismagazine.com/opinion/is-feminism-a-heresy)).

She also continued writing after "Ungodly Rage," contributing book reviews and commentary to Touchstone: A Journal of Mere Christianity and to Crisis Magazine. In "The Wiccan Myth," a June 2001 review in Touchstone, she examined Philip G. Davis's "Goddess Unmasked: The Rise of Neo-Pagan Feminist Spirituality" and its critique of claims about a peaceful, matriarchal Paleolithic civilization underlying neo-pagan Goddess movements ([Touchstone](https://www.touchstonemag.com/archives/article.php?id=14-05-038-b)). In a January 2012 Crisis Magazine piece, "Is Feminism a Heresy?," she restated her view that feminism's embrace of contraception and abortion, and its devaluation of motherhood, has caused social harm outweighing any professional gains for women ([Crisis Magazine](https://crisismagazine.com/opinion/is-feminism-a-heresy)).

## Critical Response

"Ungodly Rage" also drew sustained scholarly criticism independent of the mixed institutional reaction Catholic World Report described. In a critique first circulated online in June 1995, independent commentator Elizabeth Knuth argued that the book's scholarship was unreliable — citing quotations taken out of context, page numbers and book titles that did not match the sources cited, and characterizations of feminist writers that Knuth said misrepresented their original arguments. Knuth concluded that Steichen's scholarship "is sloppy" and that she is "not objective, nor is she accurate" ([Elizabeth Knuth](http://www.users.csbsju.edu/~eknuth/dsintro.html)). This profile does not attempt to adjudicate between Knuth's criticisms and the more favorable reception "Ungodly Rage" received from figures such as Cardinal John O'Connor, as reported by Catholic World Report.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile relies on a single independent profile (Catholic World Report, 2011) for biographical detail about Steichen herself; specific figures such as her age at the time of that profile's publication are not independently corroborated elsewhere, and no death record or obituary was found for her. It does not assess the substantive accuracy of "Ungodly Rage"'s claims about the Catholic feminist movement, which remains a matter of dispute between the book's supporters and critics.

Wikipedia's article on Steichen also states that she was born in Wadena, Minnesota; worked as a classroom teacher; and served as an officer of the Minnesota Catholic League for Religious and Civil Rights from 1980 to 1986. None of these specific claims could be independently verified in this pass — Catholic World Report says only that she is "originally from St. Cloud, Minnesota" (not necessarily her birthplace) and describes her as a pro-life and religious-education activist without identifying her as a classroom teacher or naming any organizational office — so they are left out of this profile rather than resolved.
