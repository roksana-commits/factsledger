## Overview

Satya Narayana Nadella is an American technology executive who has been chief executive officer of [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/) since February 4, 2014, its third CEO after Bill Gates and Steve Ballmer ([Britannica Money](https://www.britannica.com/money/Satya-Nadella)). Born August 19, 1967, in Hyderabad, India, he holds a bachelor's degree in electrical engineering from Mangalore University, a master's in computer science from the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee, and an MBA from the University of Chicago, completed while working full-time. He began his technology career at Sun Microsystems before joining Microsoft in 1992, where he worked on Windows NT and rose through engineering and business roles — including president of the server and tools business, generating roughly $19 billion annually — before becoming executive vice president of Microsoft's cloud computing platform just before his promotion to CEO ([Britannica Money](https://www.britannica.com/money/Satya-Nadella)).

## Path to CEO and the cloud pivot

Within months of becoming CEO, Nadella issued an internal memo on July 10, 2014 reframing Microsoft's strategy around "productivity and platforms," explaining that "cloud-first, mobile-first" served as the operational shorthand for the shift and declaring that "all strategy gets eaten by culture" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/07/10/satya-nadellas-vision-for-a-new-microsoft/)). That same month, on July 17, 2014, Microsoft announced it would cut up to 18,000 jobs — about 14% of its workforce and the largest layoff in the company's history — with more than 12,000 of the cuts concentrated in the Nokia devices and services unit Microsoft had acquired for $7.2 billion that April, in a deal negotiated under Ballmer. Nadella told employees by email that the restructuring was "part of the plan to make the 39-year-old company more agile and more productive" ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2014/07/17/332270944/microsoft-announces-biggest-layoffs-yet-up-to-18-000-workers); [Britannica Money](https://www.britannica.com/money/Satya-Nadella)).

Under Nadella, Microsoft made a series of large acquisitions to extend beyond its Windows and Office franchises: it closed a $26.2 billion purchase of the professional network LinkedIn on December 8, 2016, with Nadella framing the deal around creating "more opportunity for everyone to participate and share in economic growth" ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2016/microsofts-26b-linkedin-deal-closes/)); a $7.5 billion acquisition of the code-hosting platform GitHub, completed October 26, 2018, which Nadella said would "accelerate enterprise developers' use of GitHub" through Microsoft's sales channels and cloud infrastructure ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2018/microsoft-completes-7-5b-github-acquisition-says-user-base-grown-31m-developers/)); and, after a lengthy regulatory review, an acquisition of video-game publisher Activision Blizzard, reported at $69 billion and completed October 13, 2023 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/10/13/microsoft-closes-activision-blizzard-deal-after-regulatory-review.html)). In 2017, Nadella published "Hit Refresh," a book combining memoir with his account of Microsoft's strategy and culture ([Britannica Money](https://www.britannica.com/money/Satya-Nadella)).

## The OpenAI partnership

Microsoft invested $1 billion in [OpenAI](/organizations/openai/) in 2019 and became its cloud-computing partner ([Britannica Money](https://www.britannica.com/money/Satya-Nadella)). Nadella has since said the decision faced skepticism even from Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates, recalling in a 2026 interview that Gates warned him, "Yeah, you're going to burn this billion dollars," over what was then a nonprofit research lab. Nadella said Microsoft nonetheless proceeded because it had "a little bit of high risk tolerance," adding that he had not anticipated the investment would become as successful as it did ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/10/30/microsoft-ceo-satya-nadella-bill-gates-openai-investment-burn-billion-dollars)). On January 23, 2023, Microsoft confirmed a further multibillion-dollar investment, reported at $10 billion, extending the 2019 investment and a further round in 2021; Nadella said the deal meant Azure customers "will have access to new AI tools to build and run their applications" ([PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/amp/science/microsoft-announces-major-investment-in-artificial-intelligence-startup-openai); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-01-23/microsoft-makes-multibillion-dollar-investment-in-openai)).

### The November 2023 hiring announcement

After OpenAI's board removed [Sam Altman](/people/sam-altman/) as chief executive on November 17, 2023, Nadella announced on November 20, 2023 that Altman and OpenAI co-founder [Greg Brockman](/people/greg-brockman/) would join Microsoft to lead a new, independent advanced AI research team, alongside several other former OpenAI staff including Szymon Sidor, Jakub Pachocki and Aleksander Madry. Nadella said Microsoft had learned "over the years about how to give founders and innovators space to build independent identities and cultures within Microsoft, including GitHub, Mojang Studios and LinkedIn," and that it looked forward "to moving quickly to provide them with the resources needed for their success" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/20/openai-co-founders-sam-altman-and-greg-brockman-to-join-microsoft/)). The announcement came during the same crisis in which OpenAI employees threatened to resign en masse; Altman and Brockman were reinstated at OpenAI on November 21, 2023, before the Microsoft team materialized.

### The 2025 restructuring and its aftermath

OpenAI completed a restructuring into a for-profit public benefit corporation on October 28, 2025; Microsoft received a 27% stake in the new structure, reported at about $135 billion, and retained access to OpenAI's models through 2032 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-28/microsoft-to-get-27-of-openai-access-to-ai-models-until-2032)). No source read for this profile records a personal, on-the-record statement from Nadella at the time of that announcement itself. He addressed the arrangement publicly several months later, on Microsoft's fiscal third-quarter earnings call on April 29, 2026, saying of Microsoft's resulting rights: "we have a frontier model, with all the IP rights that we will have access to all the way to '32 and we fully plan to exploit it" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/29/satya-nadella-says-hes-ready-to-exploit-the-new-openai-deal/)).

### Trial witness in the Musk v. OpenAI lawsuit

[Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk/) sued OpenAI, Altman and Microsoft, alleging a breach of OpenAI's founding nonprofit mission; Nadella was among the witnesses called at the resulting federal trial ([PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/jury-sides-with-openai-saying-elon-musks-lawsuit-was-not-filed-on-time)). Testifying for roughly two and a half hours, Nadella disclosed an April 2022 internal email in which he worried Microsoft could become "the next IBM" — a reference to IBM's decline after ceding ground to Microsoft's operating systems decades earlier — while OpenAI became "the next Microsoft." He described the original 2019 investment as a "one-way door" and acknowledged that Microsoft was "outsourcing essentially a lot of the core IP development" while building substantial dependency on OpenAI's technology. He also testified that he had opposed seating two particular candidates on OpenAI's reconstituted board — Diane Greene, a former Google Cloud CEO, and venture capitalist Bing Gordon — because of their ties to Microsoft's AI competitors, and that he had instead suggested Sue Desmond-Hellman, who was subsequently appointed. Nadella further acknowledged a January 2023 memo from Microsoft president Brad Smith to the board projecting a $92 billion return on Microsoft's cumulative $13 billion OpenAI investment, with a 20% annual escalator beginning in 2025, while noting that the investment had carried genuine risk and could in principle have returned nothing ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/musk-v-altman-satya-nadella-was-worried-about-microsoft-being-the-next-ibm-in-openai-deal/); corroborated by [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nadella-feared-microsoft-would-become-the-next-ibm-the-trial-reveals-how-much-he-paid-to-make-sure-it-didnt)). On May 18, 2026, a federal jury unanimously dismissed all of Musk's claims, finding he had waited too long to sue ([PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/jury-sides-with-openai-saying-elon-musks-lawsuit-was-not-filed-on-time)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not assert an exact date for Nadella's trial testimony beyond a consistency check between two disagreeing sources (see openItems); it uses May 11, 2026 as the more internally consistent date. It does not state Nadella's personal reaction to OpenAI's October 2025 restructuring at the time it occurred, since no source read for this profile documents one — his on-the-record comments used here are limited to his April 2026 earnings-call remarks and his May 2026 trial testimony. It does not confirm whether Musk's attorney formally filed the appeal announced after the May 2026 verdict. It does not cover Nadella's personal life, family, or current net worth, none of which were documented in the independent sources read for this profile. It does not evaluate the merits of Musk's lawsuit or of OpenAI's restructuring; it reports only Nadella's documented public and testimonial statements about them.
