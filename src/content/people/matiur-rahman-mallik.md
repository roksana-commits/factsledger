## Overview

Matiur Rahman Mallik was a Bangladeshi schoolboy shot dead by police in Dhaka on 24 January 1969, during a
procession protesting the killing of a fellow student activist, Amanullah Asaduzzaman, four days earlier
([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/opinion/views/news/martyrdom-and-momentum-matrix-mass-uprising-3812416)).
He was a student at Nabakumar Institution in Bakshi Bazar, Old Dhaka
([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/city/mass-upsurge-day-today-1524313),
[Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/303385/historic-mass-upsurge-day-tuesday)). His death,
alongside Rustam Ali, a rickshaw-puller, and other demonstrators, is credited with helping galvanise the wider
1969 mass-upsurge movement against the government of Ayub Khan
([Banglapedia](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Mass_Upsurge,_1969)). The date of his death, 24 January, is
now observed in Bangladesh as Mass Uprising Day (Gono Uvvyutthan Dibosh).

## Death and the 1969 mass upsurge

The Daily Star records that "Matiur Rahman Mallik, a grade-IX student of Nabakumar Institution, and Rustam Ali,
a rickshaw-puller, were killed in police firing on demonstrators" in Dhaka on 24 January 1969
([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/city/mass-upsurge-day-today-1524313)). Banglapedia independently
corroborates that "a student of class IX" named Matiur died of police firing that day, alongside a second victim
named Rustam ([Banglapedia](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Mass_Upsurge,_1969)). Dhaka Tribune names further
victims of the same day's firing: "Matiur Rahman Mallik and Makbul of Nabakumar Institute, Anwar, Rustam, Milon,
Alamgir and many others" ([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/303385/historic-mass-upsurge-day-tuesday)).

A 2025 Daily Star retrospective on the mass upsurge states that a letter was found on Mallik after his death,
addressed to his mother: "Mother, I am going to the procession; if I don't return, I'm afraid that I have become
a martyr" ([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/opinion/views/news/martyrdom-and-momentum-matrix-mass-uprising-3812416)).
The same piece places his death "three days" after Asaduzzaman's; by the dates independently reported elsewhere
(Asaduzzaman killed 20 January, Mallik killed 24 January), four days separated the two deaths.

## Recognition

The Bangladesh government named Mallik a posthumous recipient of the Independence Award (Swadhinata Padak) for
2018, in the "independence and liberation war, martyr" category. Prothom Alo's report on that year's 18 recipients
lists him directly: "শহীদ মতিউর রহমান মল্লিক" appears among the honorees, alongside fellow 1969 martyrs Amanullah
Asaduzzaman and Sergeant Jahurul Haq
([Prothom Alo](https://www.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/স্বাধীনতা-পদক-পেলেন-১৮-জন)).

Separately, Banglapedia records that the government granted 10 million taka in 1998 toward memorial facilities
at his former school, which established a Matiur Rahman Memorial Library and a Matiur Rahman Memorial Museum
on campus ([Banglapedia](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Nabakumar_Institute)). The Bangladesh Awami League
places wreaths annually at a memorial to him at Nabakumar Institution
([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/303385/historic-mass-upsurge-day-tuesday)).
