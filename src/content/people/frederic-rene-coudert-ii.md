## Overview

Frederic René Coudert (11 February 1871 – 1 April 1955) was an American lawyer, the son of Frederic René Coudert Sr., a co-founder of the New York law firm Coudert Brothers. He graduated from Columbia University in 1890 and Columbia Law School in 1891, and was admitted to the New York Bar in 1892 ([Jane Addams Digital Edition](https://digital.janeaddams.ramapo.edu/items/show/4365); [The Century Association Biographical Archive](https://centuryarchives.org/caba/bio.php?PersonID=655)).

**Note on identity:** this is a different person from the U.S. congressman sometimes referred to as "Frederic Coudert Jr." (1898–1972). This profile concerns only the lawyer described here, confirmed by his 1871–1955 birth and death years.

## Wartime service and public life

Between 1915 and 1920, Coudert simultaneously served as a special assistant U.S. Attorney General and as legal adviser to the British Embassy in Washington, representing the British Crown in Public Trustee cases arising from WWI-era maritime law ([Jane Addams Digital Edition](https://digital.janeaddams.ramapo.edu/items/show/4365)). In 1916, he toured the country with Henry L. Stimson advocating military preparedness ahead of U.S. entry into the war ([The Century Association Biographical Archive](https://centuryarchives.org/caba/bio.php?PersonID=655)). He later championed American membership in the League of Nations, and in 1940 was an original member of the Committee to Defend America by Aiding the Allies ([The Century Association Biographical Archive](https://centuryarchives.org/caba/bio.php?PersonID=655)).

## Columbia University and reform work

Coudert served as a Columbia University trustee from 1912 to 1954, and in 1913 published "Certainty and Justice." He was also active in Progressive-era criminal-justice reform, serving on the American Institute of Criminal Law and Criminology's Crime and Immigration Committee alongside social reformer Jane Addams ([Jane Addams Digital Edition](https://digital.janeaddams.ramapo.edu/items/show/4365)).

## Social and club life

In 1902, at age 31, Coudert was elected a member of New York's Century Association, proposed by Joseph H. Choate and Edward Patterson; the association's own archive notes he went on to belong to "an astonishing number of clubs" across Paris, Washington and New York ([The Century Association Biographical Archive](https://centuryarchives.org/caba/bio.php?PersonID=655)). He died in Manhattan on 1 April 1955, at age 84.
