## Overview

Ömer Özsoy is a theologian from Kayseri, Turkey, who holds a professorship in Koranic exegesis at Goethe
University Frankfurt's Institute for Studies of Islamic Culture and Religion. After earning his doctorate at
the University of Ankara and receiving his professorship in Koranic exegesis in 2004, he was appointed in
2006 to an endowed chair at Frankfurt's Faculty of Protestant Theology — becoming, by his own account and
independent reporting, the first Muslim theology professor at a German university
([Qantara.de](https://qantara.de/en/article/portrait-turkish-koran-expert-%C3%B6mer-%C3%B6zsoy-modern-interpretation-islam-instead-missionary-0)).

## The "Ankara School" and historical-critical method

Özsoy is associated with the "Ankara School," a reformist current in Islamic theology that emerged at the
University of Ankara in the mid-1990s. He argues that the Koran should be understood as a text shaped by
historical circumstance and oral transmission rather than read as timeless and fixed, and that "only about
ten percent of what the Koran wants to convey can be found in the text" — meaning, in his view, that modern
believers must reinterpret its teachings contextually rather than apply them literally. This position puts
him at odds with more conservative currents within Islamic scholarship that treat Koranic injunctions as
absolute
([Qantara.de](https://qantara.de/en/article/portrait-turkish-koran-expert-%C3%B6mer-%C3%B6zsoy-modern-interpretation-islam-instead-missionary-0)).

## On academic freedom

In a later interview, Özsoy argued that Islamic theology "can only lay claim to authenticity if it is
truly academic and operates according to established academic standards," and defended applying
historical-critical analysis to Islamic texts against pressure to predetermine research outcomes: "If we
prescribe a certain ideological orientation, then we cannot pursue our research as an open-ended endeavour."
He also pushed back against external calls for a specifically "European" or reform-labeled Islam, saying
such framing "invites misunderstandings" and risks resembling a state-imposed theology
([Qantara.de](https://qantara.de/en/article/interview-theology-professor-%C3%B6mer-%C3%B6zsoy-academic-freedom-takes-precedence)).
