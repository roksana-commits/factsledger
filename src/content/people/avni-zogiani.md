## Overview

Avni Zogiani, born 5 July 1970, is a Kosovo human rights defender, investigative journalist
and civil society leader. He studied at the University of Pristina, took journalism training
at Indiana University in the United States, and earned a master's degree from the University
of Sussex's Sussex European Institute. Before turning to full-time civil society work he
reported for Kohavision and contributed to Transitions Online and Stringer magazine
([Front Line Defenders](https://www.frontlinedefenders.org/en/profile/avni-zogiani)).

## Anti-corruption work

Since 2005, Zogiani has led Çohu (the Organisation for Democracy, Anti-Corruption and
Dignity), a Kosovo civil society movement, and is affiliated with the Kosovo Centre for
Investigative Journalism, which trains and supports investigative reporters. His work
focuses on the intersection of organized crime and Kosovo's political system
([World Politics Review](https://www.worldpoliticsreview.com/corruption-and-organized-crime-in-kosovo-an-interview-with-avni-zogiani/)).

In 2014, commenting publicly on a new Pristina mayor's anti-corruption push, Zogiani
cautioned that corruption cases should be investigated by prosecutors before being
publicized, and warned that if reform did not extend beyond one official's individual
effort, the momentum for change could be lost
([The Christian Science Monitor](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Europe/2014/0421/In-Kosovo-s-capital-new-mayor-fights-corruption-by-doing-his-job)).

Front Line Defenders, an international human rights organization, has documented that
Zogiani and his family have faced threats connected to his anti-corruption and
organized-crime reporting.
