## Overview

Adam Stafford is a Scottish musician, writer and filmmaker, born in Sunderland in 1982 and based in
Falkirk. He fronted the band Y'all Is Fantasy Island from 2005 to 2011 before releasing solo
albums, including "Build a Harbour Immediately" (2011, produced by Paul Savage at Chem19 Studios)
and "Fire Behind the Curtain" (2018), the latter reviewed favourably by The Skinny
([The Skinny](https://theskinny.co.uk/music/reviews/albums/adam-stafford-fire-behind-the-curtain)).
He also runs his own label, Wise Blood Industries.

## Later albums, retirement and return

Stafford released "Imaginary Walls Collapse" in 2013, between his debut solo record and "Fire
Behind the Curtain." After "Fire Behind the Curtain," he announced his retirement from music in
late 2018 ([Clash Magazine](https://www.clashmusic.com/reviews/adam-stafford-diamonds-of-a-horse-famine)).
He returned unexpectedly in January 2019 with "The Acid Bothy," an experimental synth-based tape,
before releasing his full-length comeback album, "Diamonds of a Horse Famine," on October 26, 2020
— a record Clash Magazine called "a brave album that deserves praise for its honesty," awarding it
9/10 ([Clash Magazine](https://www.clashmusic.com/reviews/adam-stafford-diamonds-of-a-horse-famine)).
He followed it with "Trophic Asynchrony" on July 9, 2021 (Song, By Toad Records), an avant-classical
and electronica record addressing ecological themes that marked a deliberate departure from his
earlier folk-blues sound
([Backseat Mafia](https://www.backseatmafia.com/album-review-adam-stafford-trophic-asynchrony-falkirk-composer-moves-to-a-deep-cyclical-set-of-formal-minimalism-to-address-the-ecological-state-were-in/)).
By 2026 he had released "Prilitex," his 16th album, an 11-track song/lyric-based record co-produced
with Neil Pennycook of Meursault at Edinburgh's Geist studio, launched on Bandcamp on May 1, 2026 with
a limited vinyl edition planned for that August
([Falkirk Herald](https://www.falkirkherald.co.uk/whats-on/arts-and-entertainment/mayday-mayday-new-album-from-falkirks-adam-stafford-coming-in-hot-6564698)).

## Directing work

Alongside music, Stafford has directed short films and music videos. His video for The Twilight
Sad's "Seven Years of Letters" won Best Video at the 2011 Scottish New Music Awards. His short film
"The Shutdown" went on to win seven international awards, and he later directed "No Hope for Men
Below," about a 1923 mining disaster
([Louder Than War](https://louderthanwar.com/new-artist-day-adam-stafford/)).
