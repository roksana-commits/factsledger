## Overview

*The Bunny Game* is a 2011 American exploitation-horror film, the feature debut of writer
and director Adam Rehmeier, co-written with lead actress Rodleen Getsic. Shot in black and
white on 16mm with a 75-minute runtime, it depicts the abduction and sustained abuse of a
sex worker, Sylvia Grey (Getsic), by a trucker, J.R. (Jeff Renfro). Rehmeier and Getsic began
developing the project in 2006
([Love Horror](https://lovehorror.co.uk/horror-news/92585/from-uk-ban-to-us-release-the-bunny-game-brings-bloody-mayhem/)).

## BBFC rejection

In October 2011, the British Board of Film Classification formally refused to classify the
film for UK release — an outright ban rather than a cuts requirement. The board's published
reasoning, reported by JoBlo, held that the film "may encourage some viewers to enjoy and
share in the man's callousness" and that its treatment of the victim's nudity "tends to
eroticise what is shown," given that "the principal focus of *The Bunny Game* is the
unremitting sexual and physical abuse" of its central character. The BBFC noted that
re-editing might allow a future resubmission but gave "no assurances" of success, citing how
pervasively the abuse runs through the film
([JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/adam-rehmeiers-the-bunny-game-banned-by-british-board-of-film-classification/);
[Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/bbfc-bans-adam-rehmeiers-the-bunny-game-/5033304.article)).
As of Love Horror's reporting, a UK release remained uncertain
([Love Horror](https://lovehorror.co.uk/horror-news/92585/from-uk-ban-to-us-release-the-bunny-game-brings-bloody-mayhem/)).

## US release

The film had already drawn notice at the Downtown Heavy Metal Horror Film Festival in Los
Angeles, where sales agent Julian Richards of Jinga Films discovered it after its trailer
reportedly caused audience walk-outs. In March 2012, the newly launched distribution label
Autonomy Pictures — founded by Derek Curl, David Gregory and Lewis Tice — acquired all US
rights at SXSW, with Curl describing the film as "the last word on the 'torture-porn'
sub-genre" and committing to releasing it in America uncut despite the UK ban
([Dread Central](https://www.dreadcentral.com/news/31660/a-home-found-for-the-bunny-game/)).
The uncut US home-video release followed in July 2012.

## Critical reception

Writing for The A.V. Club, Noel Murray called the film "unforgettable in its way" for its
stark black-and-white cinematography and atonal score, but argued that "after its first 20
minutes, *The Bunny Game* has done just about all it's going to do," describing it as a
demonstration of the limits of a "how hardcore can you get?" aesthetic
([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-bunny-game-1798173832)).
