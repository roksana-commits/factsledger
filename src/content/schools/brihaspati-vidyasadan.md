## Overview

Brihaspati Vidyasadan (BVS) is a private day and boarding school in Naxal, Kathmandu, operated by Brihaspati
Vidyasadan Management Pvt Ltd. It was named a **Full Award** recipient in the British Council's 2019–20
International School Award programme
([British Council](https://www.britishcouncil.org.np/sites/default/files/press_release_isa-20_october_2020.pdf)).
Separately, the land its campus occupies — leased for more than three decades from the Nepal Children's
Organisation, a state-linked charity also known as Bal Mandir — is the subject of an unresolved fraud case in
Nepal's courts, following a government inquiry that found the lease had improperly favoured private interests.

## Recognition

### British Council International School Award, 2019–20

Brihaspati Vidyasadan is listed as a **Full Award** recipient in the 2019–20 ISA cycle (Batch 1) on the
[British Council Nepal ISA programme page](https://www.britishcouncil.org.np/programmes/education/connecting-classrooms/international-school-award).
The [British Council's ISA press release of 20 October 2020](https://www.britishcouncil.org.np/sites/default/files/press_release_isa-20_october_2020.pdf)
records the school at entry 4 of 23 accredited schools, as "Brihaspati Vidyasadan — Kathmandu — Full Award" — the
same document and award cycle that includes Golden Peak High School.

## The Bal Mandir land dispute

### Origins of the lease

The Nepal Children's Organisation (NCO), widely known as Bal Mandir, was established in 1964 under Nepal's National
Guidance Act, and the government transferred it a large tract of land in Naxal, Kathmandu
([Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/)).
In February 1992, NCO leased 9 ropani of that land to Brihaspati Vidyasadan; further parcels were added in
2001–2002, bringing the total to roughly 28–29 ropani
([Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/);
[The Himalayan Times](https://english.himalayatimes.com.np/2023/09/76323/)).
The Himalayan Times reported that the school itself was founded in order to secure this land, describing **Purushottam
Raj Joshi** as its founder, with a business network some outlets call "Vishal Group" holding a majority interest and
the Joshi family a minority stake — a characterization not repeated in later, more detailed reporting (see
[What this profile does not claim](#what-this-profile-does-not-claim)).

The lease was repeatedly renegotiated and extended over the following decades, at rent Nepal's anti-corruption body,
the CIAA, and multiple news outlets later described as far below market rates. In 2009 the CIAA directed NCO to
cancel the lease over this concern, but the directive was not enforced
([Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/)).
A further supplementary agreement in 2015 extended the lease again.

### Investigation and charges

NCO's government-appointed leadership changed in 2019, and the Nepali government formed an inquiry commission,
headed by Haribabu Bhattarai, to examine the lease. The commission's report, submitted in 2020, concluded that the
process had improperly favoured private interests — but the report went unimplemented for several years
([Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/)).

In 2024, NCO's leadership declared the lease legally void, and Nepal's Central Investigation Bureau opened a formal
probe, after NCO's president alleged the original arrangement was "selfish and dishonest"
([The Himalayan Times](https://english.himalayatimes.com.np/2023/09/76323/)). Courts issued arrest warrants for 20
people connected to the case, independently reported by both Kantipur/Ekantipur
([10 May 2024](https://ekantipur.com/news/2024/05/10/en/arrest-warrant-for-20-people-for-embezzlement-of-bal-mandir-land-00-27.html))
and Online Khabar
([9 May 2024](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/bal-mandir-land-case-arrest-warrant-issued-against-members-of-vishal-group.html)).

In May 2025, prosecutors filed fraud charges against 17 individuals at Kathmandu District Court, seeking roughly
NPR 604 million in damages. Republica's report of the charges names, among the 17, the school's chairperson,
**Purushottam Raj Joshi** (82), along with six other directors of Brihaspati Vidyasadan Management Pvt Ltd
([myRepublica](https://www.myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/cases-filed-against-17-persons-over-below-par-lease-rental-of-bal-mandir-la-74-23.html)).
The remaining ten people named in the charges held positions at NCO itself and are not affiliated with the school.
As of the most recent reporting found, from April 2026, the case remained before the Kathmandu District Court, and
the government had forwarded the long-dormant 2020 commission report to police for further action
([Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/)).

### Other reported scrutiny

Separately from the land case, a 2018 report by the Nepali outlet Naya Page alleged that the school charged fees
above its officially declared tuition through bundled mandatory charges for items such as food and transport
([Naya Page](https://www.nayapage.com/archives/44169)). This profile does not treat that allegation as resolved
either way; it is noted as a separate, independent line of reporting about the school, distinct from the land case.

## What this profile does not claim

**This profile makes no finding, and draws no inference, about the guilt or innocence of any individual named in
the fraud case described above.** Charges are allegations that a court has not yet resolved, not findings of fact;
this profile treats them as such throughout, and will be updated if the case's outcome is independently reported.

The characterization of a "Vishal Group" or "Bishal Group" as the school's controlling interest appears in three of
the independent sources used here (The Himalayan Times, Online Khabar and New Business Age) but not in the most
comprehensive later account (Nepalnews, 2026), which instead refers to named individuals and to Brihaspati
Vidyasadan Management Pvt Ltd. This profile therefore attributes that label to the specific outlets that used it,
rather than asserting it as an established, formally registered entity name — and notes it has no connection to the
unrelated apparel and retail company of the same name.

Of the 17 people charged in May 2025, only the school's chairperson is named here; the other six charged directors
of Brihaspati Vidyasadan Management Pvt Ltd are referred to collectively rather than individually, since they do
not otherwise appear to be public figures. No claim is made about the school's current enrollment, academic
programme or day-to-day operating status, since no independent source addressed these during this research.
