## Overview

Queens High School for the Sciences (QHSS) is a New York City public high school in Jamaica, Queens,
established in 2002 as the borough's only specialized high school, admitting students citywide based
exclusively on their score on the Specialized High School Admissions Test (SHSAT)
([Chalkbeat New York](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2025/11/26/specialized-queens-high-school-demand-to-be-relocated-to-bigger-building/)).
For its first 24 years the school had no building of its own, operating out of 14 classrooms on the second
floor of York College's Jamaica campus — despite being ranked the top high school in New York State
([Chalkbeat New York](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/02/21/queens-specialized-high-school-to-move-into-new-building-and-hbcu-has-funding/)).

## Years without a building

By the 2025-26 school year, QHSS enrolled about 500 students in space built for roughly 130 fewer. Students
and families described a school with a gym shared with the college two blocks away and open only until
12:30 p.m., a shared library, no dedicated cafeteria — students used two basement microwaves instead — and
no dedicated science laboratories or art rooms, despite the school's name and mission. Sophomore Vinny Dong
told Chalkbeat, "We have a gym shared with a college that's two blocks away, for 514 students. We have a
library that's also shared, even though it's critical for our educational content and classes."
([Chalkbeat New York](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2025/11/26/specialized-queens-high-school-demand-to-be-relocated-to-bigger-building/);
[Chalkbeat New York](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/02/21/queens-specialized-high-school-to-move-into-new-building-and-hbcu-has-funding/)).

The lack of a Queens-based specialized high school building had a broader effect: most Queens residents who
earn a specialized high school offer must commute to a school in another borough, in some cases traveling
more than an hour each way. Multiple parent boards and the citywide high school parent council passed
resolutions in November 2025 supporting QHSS's relocation to a proposed building nearby, and Schools
Chancellor Melissa Aviles-Ramos said the department was "exploring options" and planned to visit the school
([Chalkbeat New York](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2025/11/26/specialized-queens-high-school-demand-to-be-relocated-to-bigger-building/)).

Despite its facilities, demand for the school remained extraordinarily high: for the 2025-26 admissions
cycle, 11,809 students applied for 148 offers. One admitted student said, "I was ready to commit until I
learned that it had no building."
([Chalkbeat New York](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/02/21/queens-specialized-high-school-to-move-into-new-building-and-hbcu-has-funding/)).

## A contested building, then a unanimous vote

QHSS was not the only school seeking the proposed building at 165-15 88th Avenue in Jamaica; a second
school, HBCU Early College Prep, also sought the site. On February 25, 2026, New York City's Panel for
Educational Policy voted unanimously to approve QHSS's relocation there, a building that would allow the
school to grow toward 700-800 students by the 2029-30 school year. The school announced the result on
social media: "QHSS has a new home starting September 2026! Thank you to the PEP for their unanimous vote
last night."
([Queens Chronicle](https://www.qchron.com/editions/queenswide/qhss-will-relocate-to-a-new-building/article_fbb3cca7-a59d-57c9-b01d-674fa9922986.html);
[Chalkbeat New York](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/02/21/queens-specialized-high-school-to-move-into-new-building-and-hbcu-has-funding/)).
