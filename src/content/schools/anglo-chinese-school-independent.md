## Overview

Anglo-Chinese School (Independent) — usually shortened to ACS (Independent) or ACS(I) — is a secondary school in
Singapore that traces its origin to a Methodist mission school opened on 1 March 1886 by the Rev. William
Fitzjames Oldham, beginning in a shophouse on Amoy Street with 13 students
([National Heritage Board](https://www.roots.gov.sg/places/places-landing/Places/historic-sites/anglo-chinese-school)).
The original Anglo-Chinese School later split into several separate institutions carrying the ACS name —
Anglo-Chinese Junior College, ACS (Barker Road), ACS (Primary) and ACS (Independent) — and this profile covers only
the Independent school and facts specifically attributed to it.

ACS (Independent) became one of the first schools in Singapore to turn independent in January 1988, and offers two
tracks: a four-year programme leading to Singapore's Secondary Education Certificate examinations, and a six-year
Integrated Programme leading directly to the International Baccalaureate Diploma without sitting the SEC exams
([ACS (Independent)](https://www.acsindep.moe.edu.sg/about-acs-independent/school-profile/)). It was authorised as an
IB World School in 2005 and describes itself as the pioneer national school in Singapore to offer the IB Diploma
Programme ([ACS (Independent)](https://www.acsindep.moe.edu.sg/about-acs-independent/school-profile/)). The school's
stated motto is "For God and Humanity" ([ACS (Independent)](https://www.acsindep.moe.edu.sg/about-acs-independent/school-profile/)).

## Sport

ACS (Independent) has a long-running record in Singapore's National School Games rugby competition. In the 2023
season it lost the B Division final to Saint Andrew's Secondary School, then defeated Saint Andrew's Junior College
62–0 in the A Division semi-final — described by SportSG's own reporting as one of the heaviest score lines in the
tournament's history — before beating Raffles Institution 13–9 in the A Division final on 15 May 2023, extending its
run to an 8th consecutive A Division title
([Sport Singapore](https://www.activesgcircle.gov.sg/read/national-school-games-2023-rugby-acsi-rise-from-b-division-title-loss-to-lift-a-division-crown)).

## The 2025 scam-ring reporting controversy

On 30–31 October 2025, The Straits Times published a report identifying 34 people — 27 Singaporeans and 7
Malaysians — wanted by police over suspected links to a Cambodia-based scam ring. A former ACS (Independent) student,
Finan Siow, was the only individual in the report identified by his former school, in an accompanying graphic
([Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2025/10/acs-old-boys-association-finan-siow-letter/)). The ACS Old Boys'
Association wrote to the paper's editor-in-chief objecting to the framing, arguing that it "suggests that being a
former student of ACS (I) is a noteworthy trait of comparable significance to having a criminal record" and could be
read as implying the paper was singling out the school
([Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2025/10/acs-old-boys-association-finan-siow-letter/)).

On 3 November 2025, The Straits Times responded that it considered the school reference "necessary and useful" in
giving readers "verifiable information" as details of the case emerged, and said its intention was "not to cast the
school in a negative light, but to report the facts and include background which adds texture to the story"
([Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2025/11/st-responds-to-acs-old-boys-association/)).
