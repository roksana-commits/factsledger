## Overview

Trump International, Vietnam is a $1.5 billion luxury golf and residential development under construction in Hung Yen province, roughly 37 miles south of Hanoi — the Trump Organization's first branded project in Vietnam ([Vietnam Investment Review](https://vir.com.vn/construction-begins-on-15-billion-trump-organization-golf-complex-in-hung-yen-128932.html)). The project is a licensing partnership between the Trump Organization and Hung Yen Hospitality, a subsidiary of Vietnamese entrepreneur [Dang Thanh Tam](/people/dang-thanh-tam/)'s [Kinh Bac City Development Holding Corporation](/organizations/kinh-bac-city-development-holding-corporation/). The development spans roughly 1,000 hectares and is planned to include a 54-hole golf complex, luxury villas, a resort hotel and a modern urban area, with the golf course designed by two-time U.S. Open champion Bryson DeChambeau.

## Deal origins and structure

Dang Thanh Tam began building a business relationship with Eric Trump in early 2024; his industrial-park company, Kinh Bac City, had benefited from the Trump administration's first-term trade war as manufacturers relocated from China to Vietnam to avoid tariffs, a background that positioned him favorably for the partnership. The Trump Organization conducted due diligence on Dang before the two sides signed the licensing deal in New York in September 2024. Under the agreement, the developer paid a $5 million licensing fee to the Trump Organization, as disclosed in Donald Trump's June 2025 financial disclosure ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/giacomotognini/2025/10/15/trump-saved-this-vietnamese-real-estate-moguls-fortune-now-they-are-opening-a-golf-resort-together/)). A groundbreaking ceremony was held on May 21, 2025, with Eric Trump representing the Trump Organization.

## Investment approval and licensing structure

Vietnam's government formally approved the project's investment decision in mid-May 2025, signed by Deputy Prime Minister Tran Hong Ha; at that stage, official filings put the development's footprint at more than 990 hectares (about 2,446 acres) across communes along the Red River in Hung Yen's Khoai Chau district, organized into eco-residential zones with golf courses, a commercial urban zone, and a green park, with total capital estimated at roughly $1.5 billion and about VND4.1 trillion earmarked specifically for compensation and resettlement ([Vietnam News](https://vietnamnews.vn/economy/1717813/trump-group-s-1-5-billion-project-in-hung-yen-approved-for-investment.html)). That approval also specified the golf facility as two courses — one with 36 holes and one with 18 — together making up the "54-hole" complex reported by the Vietnam Investment Review. In June 2026, Hung Yen province leased Kinh Bac City Development Holding Corporation an additional 81 hectares for the project, with reporting at that point describing the total footprint as 888 hectares ([Golf Industry Asia](https://www.golfindustry.asia/1-5-billion-trump-international-hung-yen-golf-and-residential-complex-expands-with-additional-land/)).

The $5 million licensing fee is paid by Hung Yen Hospitality Services JSC, a Kinh Bac subsidiary, to DT Marks Vietnam LLC — a company that is 79% owned by DTTM Operation LLC, a Trump trademark-management entity, and 20% owned directly by the Trump family. As of the underlying U.S. federal ethics disclosure, DT Marks Vietnam LLC had recorded $4 million of that fee as received or receivable, with roughly $1 million still pending ([The Investor](https://theinvestor.vn/trump-family-to-receive-5-mln-in-license-fees-for-mega-golf-course-development-in-vietnam-d16040.html)). The Trump Organization has said it receives licensing fees and will operate the club once it is complete, but is not directly responsible for compensation payments to residents affected by the project, which it says are handled by the Vietnamese developer ([Asia Times](https://asiatimes.com/2026/07/trump-resort-rises-on-vietnam-graveyard-as-us-links-grow/)).

## Land acquisition and displacement

Asia Times reported in July 2026 that building the project required exhuming more than 3,500 ancestral graves and relocating more than 4,000 families, and that some residents protested the compensation offered as insufficient ([Asia Times](https://asiatimes.com/2026/07/trump-resort-rises-on-vietnam-graveyard-as-us-links-grow/)). This is broadly consistent with the scale of the compensation and resettlement budget disclosed in the project's May 2025 investment approval, described above.

## Government backing and other regulatory developments

Vietnamese officials have publicly and repeatedly backed the project: Asia Times reported that President To Lam, the finance ministry and the Hung Yen People's Committee have all endorsed it, and that the Ministries of Defense and Public Security have issued guidance on security-related matters, including foreign property sales. On March 18, 2025, Prime Minister Pham Minh Chinh met with the Trump Organization's project director, Charles James Boyd Bowman, and pressed for the project's completion to be accelerated; officials were reported to be targeting a faster, unofficial completion date of around March 2027, timed to Vietnam's hosting of the APEC summit — a target distinct from the Q2 2029 completion date set out in the official investment approval ([VietNamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/vietnam-pushes-for-early-completion-of-trump-organization-s-1-5-billion-project-2382244.html)).

Separately, Hung Yen's Department of Construction announced in early 2026 that foreign buyers may purchase homes in the development, subject to limits: up to 250 low-rise houses (excluding 12 villas restricted under Ministry of Public Security guidance), and no more than 30% of the apartments in any one building in one designated sub-zone ([VietNamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/foreigners-permitted-to-buy-homes-at-trump-international-hung-yen-2488332.html)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not reconcile the project's inconsistently reported land area — roughly 990 hectares at the time of formal investment approval in May 2025 versus 888 hectares in mid-2026 reporting after an additional land lease — or the differing figures given for Kinh Bac City Development Holding Corporation's exact ownership stake (85% in one account, 95%/99% in another); both discrepancies are presented as each outlet reported them (see openItems). It presents both the officially approved Q2 2029 completion date and a separately reported, unofficial push for completion by around March 2027 without asserting which is more likely to hold. It does not independently verify claims, found only in Wikipedia and not corroborated by any independent source this profile's author could fetch and read, about the identity of additional financing partners beyond Kinh Bac and the Trump Organization or about any personal background of Trump Organization project personnel — those leads were not added here for lack of independent verification. Beyond what the sources cited above report, this profile does not assess the project's broader environmental, legal or political context in Vietnam.
