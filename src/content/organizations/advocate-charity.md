## Overview

Advocate is a UK charity that matches volunteer barristers with people who need free legal help but cannot get legal aid or afford to pay for representation. It was founded in 1996 as the Bar Pro Bono Unit by the barrister Peter Goldsmith QC (later Lord Goldsmith KC), and is recorded by Companies House as incorporated on 13 August 1996 ([Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/03237309)). A Counsel magazine report from 30 June 2010 described the charity's growth from around 350 volunteer barristers at its founding to more than 2,300 by mid-2010, quoting its then Chief Executive, Rebecca Wilkie, saying "the need for pro bono assistance is steadily growing" ([Counsel](https://www.counselmagazine.co.uk/articles/new-headquarters-pro-bono)).

## Founding organisation of the National Pro Bono Centre

In October 2010 the Bar Pro Bono Unit became one of three national pro bono bodies — alongside [LawWorks](/organizations/lawworks/) (the Solicitors Pro Bono Group) and the ILEX Pro Bono Forum — housed together at the newly launched [National Pro Bono Centre](/organizations/national-pro-bono-centre/) at 48 Chancery Lane in London. Counsel magazine quoted the Unit's then Chairman, Robin Knowles CBE QC, describing the shared centre as "a further step change in the public service offered by all three branches of the legal profession, working together" ([Counsel](https://www.counselmagazine.co.uk/articles/first-ever-national-legal-pro-bono-centre-launched)). Companies House now records the charity's registered office not at that original Chancery Lane address but at the International Dispute Resolution Centre on Paternoster Lane — the same building the National Pro Bono Centre describes as its own current base, the Justice Hub ([Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/03237309)).

## Rebrand from Bar Pro Bono Unit to Advocate

On 22 October 2018 the charity rebranded from the Bar Pro Bono Unit to Advocate. Counsel magazine reported the change and quoted its then Chief Executive, Jess Campbell, saying the previous name "was not user-friendly for most of the people coming to us for help" and that the new name was chosen partly because research showed it made applicants feel "someone will speak up for them" ([Counsel](https://www.counselmagazine.co.uk/articles/bar-pro-bono-unit-now-advocate)). Legal Cheek separately reported the same rebrand, adding that the charity replaced its previous black-and-green branding with a "seesaw-style" logo intended to symbolise balance and fairness, and that the launch coincided with National Pro Bono Week ([Legal Cheek](https://www.legalcheek.com/2018/10/advocate-bar-pro-bono-unit-ditches-latin-name-in-bid-to-modernise-its-image/)). By its own account, the charity now reports over 5,000 volunteer barristers on its panel and more than 5,600 applications for help in 2025, and describes 2026 as its 30th-anniversary year ([Advocate](https://weareadvocate.org.uk/about-us/our-history.html)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile verifies Advocate's 1996 incorporation date and current registered office against Companies House records, and its 2010 growth figures, founding role at the National Pro Bono Centre, and 2018 rebrand against two Counsel magazine reports and an independent Legal Cheek report from that year. It does not independently verify the charity's current (2026) leadership, finances or the specific volunteer and case figures for 2025, which rest on the charity's own website and are flagged as first-party. It does not attempt a full history of the charity's activities across its three decades of operation.
