## Overview

The Promotion Marketing Association (PMA) was a U.S. trade association for the marketing and promotions industry,
founded in New York in 1911, according to the Association of National Advertisers, which later acquired it
([Association of National Advertisers](https://www.ana.net/content/show/id/pr-2014-acquire-baa)). PMA ran an
annual Marketing Law Conference addressed by senior U.S. government officials: the Federal Trade Commission's
Bureau of Consumer Protection Director spoke at the organization's 29th such conference in 2007, independently
noting that its first law conference had been held in 1978
([Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/public_statements/whos-control-now-navigating-tumultuous-marketing-change/071116pma.pdf)),
and a senior FTC official again addressed its 32nd Marketing Law Conference in Chicago in 2010
([Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/speeches/promotion-marketing-association)).

## The REGGIE Awards

PMA established the REGGIE Awards in 1983 to recognise promotional marketing campaigns. In 2008, trade publication
Chief Marketer reported that the organization's top "Super Reggie" honor went to 7-Eleven and agency FreshWorks
for a campaign that transformed a dozen 7-Eleven stores into real-life Kwik-E-Marts to promote The Simpsons Movie,
quoting PMA president Bonnie Carlson describing it as "the largest-scale promotion to hit the convenience retail
channel"
([Chief Marketer](https://www.chiefmarketer.com/live-from-pma-simpsons-makeover-wins-pmas-super-reggie-award/)).
The REGGIE Awards continue today, now run by the Association of National Advertisers.

## Rebranding and acquisition

The organization rebranded as the Brand Activation Association around 2012. The Association of National
Advertisers announced its acquisition of the Brand Activation Association on 22 May 2014, effective 1 July 2014,
with then-ANA chief executive Bob Liodice stating the organizations were "extremely pleased to bring the BAA into
the ANA family," and Bonnie Carlson, the Brand Activation Association's chief executive, saying the merger would
"accelerate the realization of our core strategies"
([Association of National Advertisers](https://www.ana.net/content/show/id/pr-2014-acquire-baa)). IRS filing data
compiled by ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer shows the organization's final filed fiscal year, ending June 2014,
recorded revenue of $1,350,739 against expenses of $1,735,718, leaving net assets of –$678,119, with Carlson's
reported compensation at $192,540
([ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/131847138)).
