## Overview

Ignatius Press is an American Catholic publishing house founded in San Francisco in June 1978 by Fr. Joseph Fessio, S.J., and Carolyn Lemon, operating as the primary activity of the nonprofit religious corporation Guadalupe Associates, Inc. ([Ignatius Press](https://ignatius.com/about-us/)). Fessio had studied theology in Germany under Joseph Ratzinger — later Pope Benedict XVI — and had been mentored in France by the theologian Henri de Lubac; that background shaped the press's original focus on bringing major European Catholic theologians' works into English ([Ignatius Press](https://ignatius.com/about-us/)). By its 40th anniversary in November 2018, Catholic World Report reported that Ignatius Press had published roughly 2,000 titles by several hundred authors, sold about 39 million copies in total, and was selling about 2 million copies a year, having grown to include film, home and online schooling, periodicals — including Catholic World Report itself — liturgical music and other media alongside books ([Catholic World Report](https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2019/02/12/ignatius-press-at-forty/)). As of a 2014 interview, Mark Brumley served as the press's CEO, with America Magazine describing Ignatius Press as "one of the biggest U.S. Catholic publishers" ([America Magazine](https://www.americamagazine.org/all-things/2014/10/15/apologetics-and-american-culture-qa-ignatius-press-ceo-mark-brumley/)).

## Notable authors and Pope Benedict XVI

Ignatius Press became known as the primary English-language publisher of the works of Pope Benedict XVI, drawing on Fessio's earlier relationship with Ratzinger as his theology teacher ([Ignatius Press](https://ignatius.com/about-us/)). Catholic World Report's 40th-anniversary retrospective also lists Pope John Paul II, G.K. Chesterton and Josef Pieper among the press's notable authors, alongside more recent titles such as Robert Reilly's "Making Gay Okay" and Michael O'Brien's "Father Elijah" novels ([Catholic World Report](https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2019/02/12/ignatius-press-at-forty/)).

## Connection to Donna Steichen

Ignatius Press published all three of [Donna Steichen](/people/donna-steichen/)'s books: "Ungodly Rage: The Hidden Face of Catholic Feminism" (1991), which became an unexpected bestseller for a first-time author; "Prodigal Daughters: Catholic Women Come Home to the Church" (1998); and "Chosen: How Christ Sent Twenty-Three Surprised Converts to Replant His Vineyard" (2009) ([Ignatius Press](https://ignatius.com/authors/donna-steichen/)).

## The 2014 Synod on the Family book controversy

Ignatius Press became a subject of Vatican-related news coverage in its own right during the 2014 Extraordinary Synod on the Family. The press printed and mailed roughly 200 copies of "Remaining in the Truth of Christ," a book featuring Cardinal Raymond Burke and four other cardinals defending Church discipline against giving Communion to divorced and civilly remarried Catholics, to synod participants ahead of the proceedings ([National Catholic Register](https://www.ncregister.com/news/ignatius-press-and-its-high-profile-authors-still-inform-synod-debate)). According to reporting from Catholic World Report, Cardinal Lorenzo Baldisseri, the synod's secretary general, was described as furious about the book and ordered Vatican postal staff to intercept it before it reached the synod hall; only a few of the roughly 200 mailed copies reportedly reached their intended recipients. Fr. Joseph Fessio, on behalf of Ignatius Press, confirmed that account of how many copies were mailed and how few arrived. Baldisseri's side maintained that the mailing itself had gone through improper channels, effectively acknowledging that the books were taken while disputing that this amounted to censorship ([Catholic World Report](https://www.catholicworldreport.com/2015/02/25/reports-card-baldisseri-ordered-interception-of-copies-of-book-mailed-to-synod-participants/)). National Catholic Register separately reported that Ignatius Press published other synod-related titles in the same period, including "God or Nothing," a book-length interview with Cardinal Robert Sarah, and "Living the Truth in Love," on pastoral approaches to same-sex attraction, with Fessio saying that synod-related authors brought their manuscripts to his San Francisco house, and Ignatius Press president Mark Brumley describing the press's aim of supplying "clear teaching on the faith" during a period of heightened attention ([National Catholic Register](https://www.ncregister.com/news/ignatius-press-and-its-high-profile-authors-still-inform-synod-debate)). America Magazine's Brumley interview, conducted the same month the synod controversy began, separately confirms that Ignatius Press deliberately times some releases to periods of "heightened media and church attention" ([America Magazine](https://www.americamagazine.org/all-things/2014/10/15/apologetics-and-american-culture-qa-ignatius-press-ceo-mark-brumley/)).
