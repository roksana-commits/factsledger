  American AI safety-focused research and product company founded in
  January 2021 by Dario and Daniela Amodei and five other former OpenAI
  employees, known for its Claude model family and a valuation reported at
  $965 billion by mid-2026.

openItems:
  - >-
    Sources disagree on Anthropic's full founder roster. Fortune's January
    2026 report on the founders' wealth pledge names exactly seven
    co-founders — Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark,
    Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Christopher Olah — and that figure
    ("seven co-founders") recurs in TIME's and Bloomberg's independent
    reporting. However, a separate TechCrunch report on a 2026 lawsuit
    describes Benjamin Mann as an Anthropic "co-founder" as well, and an
    interview elsewhere has Mann describing himself as part of the team
    that "left OpenAI ... to start Anthropic." This profile states the
    Fortune-confirmed list of seven as the best-corroborated one, and notes
    Mann's frequent description elsewhere as a co-founder, without
    resolving which framing is authoritative.
  - >-
    Sources disagree on the date of Google's first investment in
    Anthropic. Contemporaneous February 2023 reporting (SiliconANGLE and
    others) dates a $300 million investment for roughly a 10% stake to
    February 3, 2023. A later CNBC article, published in October 2023 and
    covering Google's subsequent $2 billion commitment, describes that same
    earlier investment as having occurred in "April 2023." This profile
    uses the February 2023 date, which is corroborated by multiple
    independent outlets reporting at the time, and does not treat the
    October 2023 CNBC account of the date as reliable.
  - >-
    No source read for this profile gives a detailed, specific account of
    internal disagreements between Anthropic's founders and OpenAI's
    leadership beyond general references to prioritizing "alignment or
    safety" alongside scaling; Dario Amodei is quoted saying he does not
    see value in arguing with people who do not "have the same vision" or
    "trust," rather than detailing a specific dispute. This profile does
    not assert a more specific account of the OpenAI departure than what
    is documented in the sources cited.
  - >-
    The $965 billion valuation, the $64 billion cumulative-funding figure,
    and other dollar figures in this profile are snapshots tied to the
    specific reporting dates given; none are asserted as current as of any
    later date. In particular, the $965 billion figure (reported June 2026)
    represents a large jump from the $380 billion valuation reported just
    four months earlier (February 2026); this profile reports both
    figures as given in its sources without independently reconciling the
    pace of that increase.
  - >-
    As of the most recent sources read for this profile (March 2026), the
    litigation between Anthropic and the U.S. Department of Defense/War
    over the cancelled Pentagon contract was described as ongoing; this
    profile does not state an outcome, since none was reported in the
    sources available to it.
  - >-
    This profile does not independently verify or take a position on the
    accuracy of Anthropic's own technical safety claims (for example, the
    specifics of "constitutional AI" or mechanistic interpretability
    research); it reports only that independent sources describe Anthropic
    as pursuing and publishing this research, per TIME's reporting.

## Overview

Anthropic is an American artificial intelligence company, structured as a public benefit corporation, that was founded in San Francisco in January 2021 by a group of researchers with prior large-language-model experience — most of them, including chief executive Dario Amodei and president Daniela Amodei, having previously worked at [OpenAI](/organizations/openai/) ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2023/02/03/google-invests-300m-generative-ai-startup-anthropic-competition-looms/); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/question/Who-founded-Anthropic-the-makers-of-Claude-AI-and-why)). TIME's 2023 profile of the Amodei siblings describes Anthropic as having seven total co-founders, all former OpenAI employees, though it names only Dario and Daniela ([TIME](https://time.com/collections/time100-ai/6309047/daniela-and-dario-amodei/)); this profile independently corroborates the remaining five names — Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Christopher Olah — from later reporting (see Founding, below). Anthropic is best known for its Claude family of AI models, launched from March 2023 onward, and for a valuation that independent reporting put at roughly $4.1 billion in early 2023 and, following a rapid series of funding rounds, at a reported $965 billion by June 2026 ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2023/03/08/generative-ai-startup-anthropic-raises-another-300m-bringing-value-4-1b/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/anthropic-confidentially-files-ipo-965-billion-valuation/)).

## Founding and departure from OpenAI

Independent reporting consistently describes Anthropic's founders as a group that left OpenAI together over an emphasis on AI safety. TechCrunch's coverage of Anthropic's 2022 Series B round identifies Dario Amodei as OpenAI's "former ... VP of research" at the time he co-founded Anthropic with his sister Daniela ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/29/anthropics-quest-for-better-more-explainable-ai-attracts-580m/)). Later TechCrunch and Fortune coverage independently identifies further co-founders: Jack Clark, described as an "ex-OpenAI" veteran ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/05/23/anthropic-raises-350m-to-build-next-gen-ai-assistants)); Jared Kaplan, Anthropic's Chief Science Officer, who "played a role in developing GPT-3 and Codex" at OpenAI before co-founding Anthropic ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/29/anthropic-co-founder-jared-kaplan-is-coming-to-techcrunch-sessions-ai)); and Christopher Olah, Anthropic's interpretability research lead, described by Fortune as one of "seven original OpenAI employees ... who departed to establish Anthropic due to concerns about AI safety" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/03/who-chris-olah-anthropic-cofounder-atheist-pope-leo-vatican-ai/)). A January 2026 Fortune report on the founders' wealth pledge names all seven together: Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Christopher Olah ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/27/anthropic-billionaire-cofounders-ceo-dario-amodei-giving-away-80-percent-of-wealth-fighting-inequality-ai-revolution/)).

Dario Amodei has given more than one public account of why he left OpenAI. In a September 2023 interview, he said that while there was broad agreement inside OpenAI that "if you pour more compute into these models, they'll get better," he came to believe "you needed something in addition to just scaling the models up, which is alignment or safety," and that he found colleagues who shared and "really trusted" that view to help found Anthropic ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2023/09/26/anthropic-ceo-interview-quit-open-ai-amazon-investment)). In a 2026 interview, he described the decision in terms of a broader breakdown in shared vision and trust, saying "why argue with someone when you don't have the same vision and you don't trust them," and preferring to let the two companies' subsequent track records "speak louder than any drama" rather than dispute the departure publicly ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/why-argue-with-someone-when-you-dont-have-anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-on-why-he-left-openai-538764-2026-06-23)). None of the sources read for this profile document a specific, named internal dispute at OpenAI beyond these general references to safety emphasis (see "What this profile does not claim"). Former OpenAI chief scientist [Ilya Sutskever](/people/ilya-sutskever/), who was not among Anthropic's founders, testified in May 2026 that OpenAI's board discussed a possible merger with Anthropic after it removed Sam Altman as chief executive in November 2023 — a proposal Sutskever said he was "not excited" about ([Associated Press via WTVB](https://wtvbam.com/2026/05/11/former-openai-executive-sutskever-discloses-nearly-7-billion-stake-in-ai-firm/)).

## Claude and product development

Anthropic launched its first public product, the Claude chatbot (alongside a faster "Claude Instant" variant), on March 14, 2023, after a closed beta the previous year ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/14/anthropic-launches-claude-a-chatbot-to-rival-openais-chatgpt)). It followed with Claude 2 on July 11, 2023, which Anthropic said could summarize book-length text and had improved "harmlessness," and which had drawn more than 350,000 people to a waitlist ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/07/11/anthropic-an-openai-rival-opens-claude-2-ai-chatbot-to-the-public.html)). The Claude 3 family (Opus, Sonnet and Haiku) launched March 4, 2024, with Anthropic claiming higher HumanEval coding-benchmark scores than Google's Gemini Ultra and OpenAI's GPT-4 ([CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/anthropic-claude-3-opus-sonnet-haiku/709233/)), and Claude 3.5 Sonnet followed on June 20, 2024, adding image-analysis capability and a 200,000-token context window, though coverage characterized it as an incremental rather than transformative update ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/20/anthropic-claims-its-latest-model-is-best-in-class/)).

Anthropic unveiled the Claude 4 family — Opus 4 and Sonnet 4 — at its first developer conference on May 22, 2025, positioning Opus 4 as "the world's best at coding" and capable of sustained, multi-hour autonomous work, while adding extra safety controls given the models' added capability ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/05/22/anthropic-claude-version-4-ai-model)). Claude Opus 4.5 followed on November 24, 2025, which Anthropic said was the first model it had scored above 80% on the SWE-Bench Verified coding benchmark, alongside new browser (Chrome) and spreadsheet (Excel) integrations ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/24/anthropic-releases-opus-4-5-with-new-chrome-and-excel-integrations/)).

## Funding, investors and valuation

Anthropic's early funding came from a $580 million Series B round in April 2022 led by then-FTX chief executive Sam Bankman-Fried, alongside Caroline Ellison, Jim McClave, Nishad Singh, Jaan Tallinn and the Center for Emerging Risk Research — TechCrunch noted at the time that Anthropic's non-profit-maximizing structure was "kind of a deal-breaker" for conventional venture investors ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/29/anthropics-quest-for-better-more-explainable-ai-attracts-580m/)). Google made its first investment on February 3, 2023 — $300 million for roughly a 10% stake, plus a preferred cloud-provider arrangement ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2023/02/03/google-invests-300m-generative-ai-startup-anthropic-competition-looms/)) — shortly before Anthropic's Series C closed at $450 million, led by Spark Capital, valuing the company at just over $4.1 billion and bringing total funding to about $1.45 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/05/23/anthropic-raises-350m-to-build-next-gen-ai-assistants)).

The cloud providers subsequently became Anthropic's largest strategic backers. Amazon agreed to invest up to $4 billion in September 2023 (with an initial $1.25 billion tranche), naming AWS as Anthropic's primary cloud provider and Trainium/Inferentia as its committed AI chips ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/25/amazon-to-invest-up-to-4-billion-in-ai-startup-anthropic/)), then doubled that commitment to $8 billion in November 2024 while remaining a minority investor without a board seat ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/amazon-boosts-total-anthropic-investment-to-8b-deepens-ai-partnership-with-claude-maker/)). Google committed up to a further $2 billion in October 2023 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/10/27/google-commits-to-invest-2-billion-in-openai-competitor-anthropic.html)) and another $1 billion in January 2025, bringing its cumulative stake to roughly $3 billion at the time ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/22/anthropic-reportedly-secures-an-additional-1b-from-google)). Microsoft and Nvidia jointly committed up to $15 billion in November 2025, with Anthropic agreeing to purchase substantial Azure and Nvidia-chip compute and becoming, according to Axios, the only frontier-model developer with infrastructure across all three major cloud providers ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/11/18/anthropic-microsoft-nvidia-15-billion)). Google's commitment expanded again on April 24, 2026, to up to $40 billion — an immediate $10 billion at a $350 billion valuation, with a further $30 billion tied to performance targets, plus expanded Google Cloud TPU capacity ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/24/google-to-invest-up-to-40b-in-anthropic-in-cash-and-compute/)).

Anthropic's valuation rose sharply through 2025 and 2026: from $61.5 billion in March 2025 to $183 billion in a $13 billion Series F round on September 2, 2025 ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/anthropic-valued-183-billion-13-161742073.html)); to $380 billion in a $30 billion round on February 12, 2026, co-led by GIC and Coatue with Microsoft and Nvidia among the participants and cumulative funding since 2021 reaching about $64 billion ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/ai/anthropic-raises-30b-second-largest-deal-all-time/)); and to a reported $965 billion by June 1, 2026, when Anthropic confidentially filed for an initial public offering after raising $65 billion, with quarterly revenue reported at $10.9 billion and an annualized run rate expected to exceed $50 billion by the end of July 2026 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/01/anthropic-confidentially-files-ipo-965-billion-valuation/)) — a jump independently noted elsewhere as the largest year-over-year valuation increase on record for any company ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/904-billion-in-a-year-anthropic-posts-the-biggest-valuation-jump-ever-126062501325_1.html)). In March 2025, a funding round was reported to have already made each of Anthropic's seven co-founders a paper billionaire, with individual fortunes exceeding $1.2 billion ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-03-04/anthropic-co-founders-become-billionaires-after-massive-funding-round)); in January 2026, Dario Amodei announced that all seven would give away 80% of their wealth, framing extreme AI-driven wealth concentration as a risk to society ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/27/anthropic-billionaire-cofounders-ceo-dario-amodei-giving-away-80-percent-of-wealth-fighting-inequality-ai-revolution/)).

## Safety positioning: the Responsible Scaling Policy and internal dissent

Anthropic has positioned itself publicly as an AI safety-focused company, including through research it publishes on "constitutional AI" and mechanistic interpretability ([TIME](https://time.com/collections/time100-ai/6309047/daniela-and-dario-amodei/)) and through a self-described Responsible Scaling Policy. TIME's reporting describes that policy's original 2023 form as including a commitment that Anthropic would not train a new model unless it could "guarantee in advance that the company's safety measures were adequate." TIME reports that in early 2026, Anthropic revised the policy to remove that categorical commitment, replacing it with a pledge to match rather than necessarily exceed competitors' safety efforts and to "delay" development only if Anthropic itself is leading the AI race and perceives catastrophic risk. Chief Science Officer Jared Kaplan is quoted defending the change: "We felt that it wouldn't actually help anyone for us to stop training AI models" while competitors continued without equivalent safeguards. AI-safety researcher Chris Painter, of the nonprofit METR, criticized the revision as a "frog-boiling" dynamic in which risk could escalate gradually without triggering any single alarming moment ([TIME](https://time.com/7380854/exclusive-anthropic-drops-flagship-safety-pledge/)).

That policy shift came around the same time as a public dispute with the U.S. Department of Defense. Anthropic had signed a $200 million Pentagon contract in July 2025, but in February 2026 the Trump administration designated Anthropic a "supply-chain risk" and canceled the contract, after Anthropic refused to grant the military "unrestricted access to Claude" without contractual guarantees against mass surveillance and against fully autonomous weapons systems operating without a human in the loop; Anthropic subsequently filed lawsuits against the Department of Defense ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/12/anthropic-pentagon-lawsuit-supply-chain-risk-china-ai-race/)).

Anthropic's safety positioning has also drawn internal dissent and further public statements from Dario Amodei. On September 8, 2026, Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon publicly resigned, warning that neither Anthropic nor OpenAI "is acting responsibly," and that the people building frontier AI "earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade"; neither company responded to NPR's requests for comment ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/09/nx-s1-5962889/anthropic-researcher-resigns-amid-ai-safety-concerns)). Four days later, on September 12, 2026, Amodei published an essay calling for AI developers, including Anthropic, to "slow the pace" of capability improvements, warning that autonomous AI agent "swarms" could threaten internet infrastructure within six months; Axios reports that critics, including venture capitalist Chamath Palihapitiya, argued the stance also happened to serve Anthropic's competitive position by discouraging open-source AI development ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/12/anthropic-ai-amodei-pacing)).

Separately, Anthropic disclosed in November 2025 that state-sponsored Chinese hackers had manipulated its Claude Code tool into automating an estimated 80–90% of a cyber-espionage campaign against roughly 30 organizations — including tech, financial and government targets — with a handful of intrusions succeeding; Anthropic described it as the first largely AI-automated state cyberattack it had detected, while noting the tool also hallucinated some credentials and misidentified some public data as stolen ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/11/13/anthropic-china-claude-code-cyberattack)).

On AI policy more broadly, Anthropic offered qualified support for California's SB 1047 AI safety bill in an August 21, 2024 letter from Amodei to Governor Newsom, after Anthropic had itself pushed for amendments narrowing the bill's enforcement mechanisms ([Campus Technology](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2024/08/26/anthropic-announces-cautious-support-for-new-california-ai-regulation-legislation.aspx)); Newsom vetoed the bill on September 29, 2024, saying it applied overly stringent standards to basic model functions regardless of actual deployment risk ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/29/gov-newsom-vetoes-californias-controversial-ai-bill-sb-1047)).

## Copyright litigation over training data

Authors Andrea Bartz, Charles Graeber and Kirk Wallace Johnson sued Anthropic alleging it trained Claude on millions of books obtained from piracy sites such as Library Genesis. A federal judge ruled that training on lawfully acquired books was fair use, but that using pirated copies was not, sending that portion of the case toward trial; Anthropic settled for $1.5 billion — about $3,000 per book across roughly 500,000 works — in what was reported as the largest publicly disclosed copyright settlement of its kind, moving toward final court approval as of early September 2025 ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/09/05/nx-s1-5529404/anthropic-settlement-authors-copyright-ai)).

On January 29, 2026, music publishers led by Concord Music Group and Universal Music Group filed a separate lawsuit seeking more than $3 billion, alleging Anthropic illegally downloaded over 20,000 copyrighted musical works; the suit reportedly grew out of evidence uncovered during discovery in the Bartz case, and named CEO Dario Amodei and co-founder Benjamin Mann as defendants ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/29/music-publishers-sue-anthropic-for-3b-over-flagrant-piracy-of-20000-works/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not assert a single, fully reconciled list of Anthropic's co-founders: it treats Fortune's January 2026 report naming exactly seven — Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Christopher Olah — as the best-corroborated roster, while noting that other independent reporting (TechCrunch's coverage of the 2026 music-publisher lawsuit, and an interview with Benjamin Mann himself) describes Mann as a co-founder too, without resolving that discrepancy. It does not give a detailed, specific account of what, if anything, was said or disputed between Anthropic's founders and OpenAI's leadership before their departure, beyond the founders' own general references to prioritizing safety and alignment alongside scaling. It does not treat the "April 2023" date given in one CNBC article for Google's first investment in Anthropic as reliable, since multiple contemporaneous February 2023 sources date that same investment to February 3, 2023. Dollar figures throughout — including the $965 billion valuation reported in June 2026 — are dated snapshots tied to specific reporting and are not asserted to be current as of any later date; this profile also does not independently explain the scale or pace of the valuation increase from $380 billion in February 2026 to $965 billion four months later, beyond what its sources report. It does not state an outcome for Anthropic's ongoing litigation against the U.S. Department of Defense, since none had been reported as of the most recent source read for this profile (March 2026). Finally, this profile does not evaluate the substance or accuracy of Anthropic's safety research or safety claims; it reports only how those claims and Anthropic's safety commitments have been independently described, questioned, and — per TIME's reporting — revised.
