## Overview

NLS Legal is a regulated teaching law firm run by [Nottingham Law School](/universities/nottingham-law-school/) at Nottingham Trent University, which began operating in 2006 as Nottingham Law School's Legal Advice Centre, providing legal guidance to individuals, small businesses, entrepreneurs and charities alongside pro bono practice for students ([National Centre for Universities & Business](https://www.ncub.co.uk/insight/legal-advice-centre-at-nottingham-law-school/)). It describes itself as the only law school in England and Wales with an on-campus teaching law firm ([Nottingham Trent University](https://www.ntu.ac.uk/study-and-courses/academic-schools/nottingham-law-school/nls-legal)).

## Structure and regulation

The clinic was incorporated on 25 September 2014 as a private company limited by guarantee under the name Nottingham Law School Legal Advice Centre Limited, and Companies House filing records show it was renamed NLS Legal Limited in July 2022 ([Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/09235463)). It has been licensed by the Solicitors Regulation Authority as an Alternative Business Structure since 8 October 2015, a status that permits it to provide reserved legal services including rights of audience, conduct of litigation and probate activities ([Solicitors Regulation Authority](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/firm-based-authorisation/abs-register/620872/)). According to the National Centre for Universities & Business, the clinic has advised or represented over 900 clients and involved more than 1,200 students, and it launched a dedicated Business and Enterprise Law Service for small businesses and charities in January 2017 ([National Centre for Universities & Business](https://www.ncub.co.uk/insight/legal-advice-centre-at-nottingham-law-school/)).

## Recognition

Reporting on the 2021 [LawWorks](/organizations/lawworks/) and Attorney General Student Pro Bono Awards, Legal Cheek said Nottingham Trent's clinic won "Best Contribution by a Law School," crediting 340 student volunteers with having helped secure over £730,000 for clients in the preceding academic year — including £680,000 in welfare benefits — and reporting an 82% success rate representing 34 individuals before the Social Security Tribunal ([Legal Cheek](https://www.legalcheek.com/2021/05/kings-cardiff-and-nottingham-trent-bag-awards-for-pro-bono-efforts/)). Nottingham Trent University's own award listing additionally states the clinic received an Access to Justice Foundation award in 2016, which it describes as the first such award to a university law clinic; that specific claim was not independently corroborated (see below).

## Relationship to the Free Representation Unit

Under its earlier name, Nottingham Law School's Legal Advice Centre is documented as the [Free Representation Unit](/organizations/free-representation-unit/)'s partner site in the Midlands: a National Pro Bono Centre case study describes an FRU-linked student volunteer at the clinic successfully challenging an incorrectly imposed stay of proceedings in an employment tribunal, after which the client was awarded over £25,000 for unfair dismissal and breach of contract ([National Pro Bono Centre](https://www.nationalprobonocentre.org.uk/casestudy/anitas-story/)). Companies House's record of the 2022 rename from Nottingham Law School Legal Advice Centre Limited to NLS Legal Limited confirms this is the same legal entity operating under its current name.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile verifies the clinic's 2014 incorporation, its 2022 rename and its 2015 regulatory licensing against Companies House and Solicitors Regulation Authority records, and its 2021 award and results against an independent trade-press report, but it does not independently verify its current (2026) caseload, staffing or client numbers — the client and student scale figures here are undated as reported by the source consulted. The claim that it was the first university law clinic to win an Access to Justice Foundation award, in 2016, rests solely on Nottingham Trent University's own account and was not corroborated against the awarding body or another independent source; it is reported here as the university's own claim, not as independently confirmed fact. It does not attempt a full history of the clinic's activities between 2006 and the present.
