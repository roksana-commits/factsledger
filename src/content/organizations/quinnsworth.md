## Overview

Pat Quinn, who had spent five years in Canada's grocery trade and worked as a concert promoter bringing acts like Johnny Cash and the Rolling Stones to Canada, returned to Ireland and opened the first Quinnsworth store at Dublin's Stillorgan Shopping Centre in December 1966. By 1971, the chain had grown to six stores with a turnover of £6 million, and Quinn earned recognition as "the whizz kid of the Irish grocery business," competing against Dunnes Stores, Superquinn, H Williams, Liptons and Five Star ([The Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/death-of-pat-quinn-founder-of-quinnsworth-1.778035)).

## Yellow Pack and later history

In the early 1970s, Quinn coined the term "Yellow Pack" for the chain's budget-priced products, marketing them heavily around his own flamboyant personality and personal appearances in advertising, before selling his controlling interest to Galen Weston's Associated British Foods ([The Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/death-of-pat-quinn-founder-of-quinnsworth-1.778035)). The chain later passed to Power Supermarkets while continuing to operate under the Quinnsworth name, and its low-cost Yellow Pack range was later described as "the Ryanair model of its time." In 1997, Quinnsworth was acquired by Tesco — the UK retailer's entry into the Irish market — and was subsequently rebranded, along with the associated Crazy Prices chain, as Tesco Ireland ([Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/opinion/commentanalysis/arid-40258610.html)).
