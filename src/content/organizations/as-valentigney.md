## Overview

AS Valentigney is a French amateur football club founded in 1920 in Valentigney, a Doubs factory town built around
the Peugeot automobile works, and drawn largely from Peugeot's own workforce
([Le Footichiste](https://www.footichiste.com/sochaux-peugeot/)). The club's defining moment came in 1926, when it
reached the final of the Coupe de France — France's national cup — before losing 4–1 to Olympique de Marseille, a
result confirmed by the French Football Federation's own archive of the match
([FFF TV](https://ffftv.fff.fr/video/6178691048001/finale-1926-olympique-de-marseille-as-valentigney-4-1)).

## From factory team to a professional club's origin story

Valentigney's cup run did more than make headlines locally: it caught the attention of Peugeot director
Jean-Pierre Peugeot, who according to independent football-history writing wanted to build on the enthusiasm it
generated among the company's workforce and give the Peugeot name a presence in the national game
([Le Footichiste](https://www.footichiste.com/sochaux-peugeot/)). Rather than simply expanding Valentigney,
Peugeot chose to invest in a separate club that had just been founded by two of the company's own employees —
Football Club de Sochaux — giving it the funding and organization to become a professional side within a few
years. Sochaux went on to become one of France's leading clubs, still competing in Ligue 1 today, while
Valentigney continued on as an amateur side. The two clubs' histories briefly merged during the Second World War,
from 1942 to 1944, when they combined to form FC Sochaux-Valentigney before separating again once the war ended
([Le Footichiste](https://www.footichiste.com/sochaux-peugeot/)).

Valentigney's own honors record, per Statfootballclubfrance, includes a cup run in 1928 and a title win in the
1960–61 season
([Statfootballclubfrance](https://statfootballclubfrance.fr/as-valentigney.php)) — not, as an earlier version
of this profile stated, a "1935–36 Championnat de France Amateur" title, which that source does not describe.
