## Overview

Microsoft Corporation is an American technology company founded in 1975 by Bill Gates and Paul Allen, headquartered in Redmond, Washington, and led since February 4, 2014 by chief executive [Satya Nadella](/people/satya-nadella/), who succeeded Steve Ballmer ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Microsoft-Corporation); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/02/04/microsofts-new-ceo-is-officially-satya-nadella/)). Britannica describes its business as organized around three segments: productivity and business processes (including Microsoft 365 and LinkedIn), intelligent cloud services (led by Azure), and personal computing (Windows, Xbox and Surface); for the fiscal year ended June 30, 2026, the company reported $331.8 billion in revenue and, as of September 12, 2026, a market capitalization of roughly $3.7 trillion ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Microsoft-Corporation)). This profile does not attempt a full corporate history; it instead focuses, after brief baseline facts, on Microsoft's extensively documented investment and partnership relationship with [OpenAI](/organizations/openai/) and its resulting role in that company's November 2023 board crisis and the 2024–2026 Musk v. OpenAI litigation.

## Founding and core business

Microsoft was founded in 1975 by Bill Gates and Paul Allen and grew, according to Britannica, into "one of the most powerful brand names and profitable companies in American history" by the mid-1990s on the strength of its dominance of personal-computer operating systems, before diversifying into cloud computing and artificial intelligence under Nadella ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Microsoft-Corporation)). Nadella took over as Microsoft's third CEO in company history after 22 years at Microsoft, most recently as Executive Vice President of Cloud and Enterprise; Gates, the founding CEO, took on a new "Founder and Technology Advisor" role at the time of the transition ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/02/04/microsofts-new-ceo-is-officially-satya-nadella/)).

## The OpenAI partnership

Microsoft's relationship with OpenAI began with a $1 billion investment announced July 22, 2019, under which Microsoft became OpenAI's exclusive cloud provider, committed to build new Azure AI supercomputing infrastructure, and received rights to commercialize some of OpenAI's "pre-AGI technologies"; the companies described the deal as resting on "shared principles on ethics and trust" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/07/22/microsoft-invests-1-billion-in-elon-musks-openai.html)). Microsoft extended the partnership with a further, undisclosed-amount round in 2021, and then a much larger commitment confirmed January 23, 2023 and reported at $10 billion ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-01-23/microsoft-makes-multibillion-dollar-investment-in-openai)).

By October 2025, OpenAI restructured into a for-profit public benefit corporation, OpenAI Group, controlled by the nonprofit OpenAI Foundation. In that restructuring, completed October 28, 2025, Microsoft received a 27% stake in OpenAI Group, reported at approximately $135 billion, on a cumulative $13.8 billion it had invested since 2019 — a return Al Jazeera's reporting characterized as nearly tenfold — and committed to a reported $250 billion Azure cloud-services agreement running at least through 2032. As part of the deal, Microsoft gave up its earlier right of first refusal to be OpenAI's compute provider and relinquished any claim to hardware developed through OpenAI's acquisition of Jony Ive's design firm, while retaining certain rights to OpenAI's models and products through at least 2032; Microsoft's own shares rose 2.5% on the news, pushing its market value back above $4 trillion at the time ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2025/10/28/openai-restructures-into-public-benefit-firm-microsoft-takes-27-stake)).

## Microsoft's role in the November 2023 board crisis

When OpenAI's board removed [Sam Altman](/people/sam-altman/) as chief executive in November 2023, Microsoft moved quickly to secure OpenAI's displaced leadership. On November 20, 2023, after three days of intense discussions, Nadella announced that Altman and [Greg Brockman](/people/greg-brockman/) would join Microsoft to lead a new, internal "advanced AI research team," with other former OpenAI staff — named in reporting as Szymon Sidor, Jakub Pachocki and Aleksander Madry — expected to join them, and with Microsoft reportedly extending job offers to OpenAI's wider staff. Nadella framed the move as consistent with the existing partnership, saying Microsoft had "learned a lot over the years about how to give founders and innovators space to build independent identities and cultures within Microsoft," while adding that Microsoft "remain[ed] committed to our partnership with OpenAI." At the time, Microsoft had invested more than $10 billion in OpenAI and held close to a 49% economic interest in the company ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/20/openai-co-founders-sam-altman-and-greg-brockman-to-join-microsoft/)). Altman was reinstated as OpenAI's CEO the following day and did not take up the Microsoft role; the fuller sequence of the board's decision, the employee revolt, and Altman's reinstatement is covered in this site's OpenAI profile.

## The Musk v. OpenAI litigation

Microsoft became a defendant in [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk/)'s lawsuit against OpenAI and Altman only after Musk's attorneys filed an amended complaint on November 14, 2024, adding Microsoft as a co-defendant along with new federal antitrust allegations. The amended complaint alleged that xAI, Musk's competing AI company, "has been harmed by ... an inability to obtain compute from Microsoft on terms anywhere near as favorable as OpenAI receives," that Microsoft and OpenAI shared "competitively sensitive information," and that their relationship amounted to a "de facto merger" given Microsoft's roughly 49% economic interest and its extensive cloud infrastructure supporting OpenAI. The complaint separately argued that a Microsoft vice president's position as a board observer at OpenAI facilitated improper coordination between the two companies ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/14/musks-amended-lawsuit-against-openai-names-microsoft-as-defendant)). A related report additionally describes the complaint alleging that Microsoft and OpenAI conditioned investment on agreements not to work with rival AI developers and that their exclusive licensing arrangement functioned as an "unapproved merger" aimed at monopolizing the generative AI market ([Nasdaq/Dow Jones](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/musk-expands-openai-lawsuit-adding-microsoft-and-antitrust-claims)).

Microsoft and OpenAI jointly moved to dismiss the amended complaint. In their February 2025 filing, Microsoft's lawyers called Musk's suit a "scattershot" effort designed to "slow Microsoft and OpenAI down" without succeeding on any individual claim, argued the complaint was "devoid of factual specificity and substantiation, repeatedly relying on unsupported 'information and belief,'" and separately contended that Microsoft should not be a defendant at all, since the underlying dispute centered on OpenAI's 2015 founding — a matter that predated Microsoft's involvement with the company entirely ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/02/04/openai_microsoft_musk_lawsuit/)).

On May 18, 2026, a federal jury in the Northern District of California unanimously dismissed all of Musk's claims, including the antitrust claim against Microsoft over its "$13 billion" in OpenAI investments made between 2019 and 2023; NPR reports that this dismissal, like the others, turned on the jury's finding that Musk had waited too long to sue rather than on a ruling on the merits of the underlying allegations ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/18/nx-s1-5822366/musk-altman-openai-jury-verdict-claims-dismissed)). The fuller account of the trial, the claims against OpenAI and Altman specifically, and the presiding judge's and Musk's attorney's public statements is covered in this site's OpenAI profile.
