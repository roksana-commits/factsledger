## Overview

Strathclyde Partnership for Transport (SPT) is the regional transport authority for the west of Scotland, established as a Regional Transport Partnership under Section 1 of the Transport (Scotland) Act 2005 ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2005/12/contents)). SPT describes itself as the largest of Scotland's seven regional transport partnerships, working with 12 constituent council areas — East Dunbartonshire, East Ayrshire, East Renfrewshire, Glasgow City, Inverclyde, North Ayrshire, North Lanarkshire, Renfrewshire, South Ayrshire, South Lanarkshire, West Dunbartonshire, and the Helensburgh and Lomond area of Argyll and Bute — to plan and deliver transport across the region ([SPT](https://www.spt.co.uk/about-us/who-we-are/history-background/)). Its core operational role is owning and running the Glasgow Subway and coordinating subsidised and specialist bus services across its member areas ([SPT](https://www.spt.co.uk/about-us/who-we-are/)).

## Finances and governance

SPT's 2024/25 annual audit, conducted by Audit Scotland, records revenue expenditure of £36.6 million for the year, funded by £35.9 million contributed by its 12 local authority partners and a further £0.617 million in Scottish Government grant. Capital expenditure for the same year totalled £37.8 million, secured despite an initial capital allocation of zero, after SPT obtained £32.1 million mid-year plus £5.7 million from the Subway Infrastructure Fund. The audit found usable reserves of £178.327 million, up from £173.801 million the previous year, and reported that SPT's annual accounts were free of material misstatement, with no significant governance weaknesses identified ([Audit Scotland](https://audit.scot/uploads/2025-10/aar_2425_strathclyde_transport.pdf)). The same report notes that SPT's long-term financial strategy projects its reserves declining from roughly £174 million to £90 million over the following decade, reflecting growing reliance on reserve drawdowns as government capital funding has tightened.

That funding pressure was made explicit in SPT's own written submission to the Scottish Parliament's Net Zero, Energy and Transport Committee, which stated that the Scottish Government cut its general capital grant to SPT to zero for 2024/25 without advance consultation, despite an earlier commitment of up to £246 million toward the Glasgow Subway Modernisation programme and roughly £70 million in contractual commitments still outstanding at the time ([Scottish Parliament, Net Zero, Energy and Transport Committee](https://www.parliament.scot/-/media/files/committees/net-zero-energy-and-transport-committee/correspondence/2024/budget-2024-25-spt-submission.pdf)). SPT identified bus infrastructure, tunnel engineering work and active travel initiatives as programmes at risk from the shortfall, and separately noted that a bid to the Scottish Zero Emission Bus Challenge Fund for a fully electric bus fleet on Arran had been rejected.

## Subway modernisation

Despite the funding gap, SPT's 2024/25 audit records £253.9 million spent of a £288.7 million Subway Modernisation budget, with all 17 new subway trains accepted into service by the end of the audit year ([Audit Scotland](https://audit.scot/uploads/2025-10/aar_2425_strathclyde_transport.pdf)). Reporting on the programme's remaining phase, Time Out described SPT as targeting driverless operation of the Glasgow Subway — one of the world's oldest underground metro systems — in the second half of 2026, contingent on installing new signalling systems to enable unattended running; SPT itself called this "a first of its kind in the UK" and acknowledged the signalling phase "was always expected to be the most complex phase of delivery," with potential for further delay ([Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/uk/news/the-major-uk-city-that-will-get-driverless-subway-trains-in-2026-121725)).

## Role in Clyde Metro

On 24 November 2023, SPT was designated to lead development of the Case for Investment (CFI) for [Clyde Metro](/places/clyde-metro/), the proposed multi-decade mass transit expansion for the Glasgow City Region. SPT's 2024/25 audit confirms that CFI Stage 1 — establishing a case for change, vision and objectives, and a set of corridor-based network options — was completed in March 2025, consistent with the timeline SPT has separately reported to its own board.
