## Overview

OpenAI is an American artificial intelligence research and product company, announced on December 11, 2015, as a nonprofit organization backed by roughly $1 billion in pledged funding from private investors ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2020/02/17/844721/ai-openai-moonshot-elon-musk-sam-altman-greg-brockman-messy-secretive-reality/); [Britannica Money](https://www.britannica.com/money/OpenAI)). It is best known as the developer of ChatGPT and the GPT family of large language models, and its chief executive since 2019 has been [Sam Altman](/people/sam-altman/), who was briefly removed and then reinstated by the company's board in November 2023. As of October 2025, OpenAI operates as a for-profit public benefit corporation, OpenAI Group, controlled by the nonprofit OpenAI Foundation, with Microsoft as its largest outside shareholder ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2025/10/28/openai-restructures-into-public-benefit-firm-microsoft-takes-27-stake)). OpenAI describes its mission as working to "ensure that artificial general intelligence benefits all of humanity" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/about/)).

## Founding

Individuals named as OpenAI's founders or earliest backers in the sources read for this profile include Sam Altman and [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk/), both described as investors and co-founders; [Greg Brockman](/people/greg-brockman/), who became chief technology officer; Ilya Sutskever, who became research director; and Peter Thiel, named as an early investor and co-founder ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2020/02/17/844721/ai-openai-moonshot-elon-musk-sam-altman-greg-brockman-messy-secretive-reality/)). The organization's original stated aim was to "build value for everyone rather than shareholders" and to hold "a primary fiduciary duty to humanity" while safely developing artificial general intelligence ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2020/02/17/844721/ai-openai-moonshot-elon-musk-sam-altman-greg-brockman-messy-secretive-reality/)).

Musk left OpenAI's board on February 23, 2018. OpenAI's own blog post at the time gave the reason as avoiding a looming conflict of interest, stating, "As Tesla continues to become more focused on AI, this will eliminate a potential future conflict for Elon," and said he would continue to donate to and advise the organization ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/international/elon-musk-leaves-openai-board-to-avoid-conflict-of-interest-with-tesla-118022300413_1.html)).

## Restructuring and the Microsoft partnership

In 2019, OpenAI moved from a pure nonprofit to a hybrid structure with a "capped-profit" commercial arm, after concluding that the cost of its research would exceed what conventional nonprofit fundraising could sustain. Microsoft invested $1 billion that year and became OpenAI's cloud-computing partner ([Britannica Money](https://www.britannica.com/money/OpenAI)). Microsoft expanded its stake with a further, reported $10 billion investment confirmed on January 23, 2023, on top of its 2019 investment and an additional round in 2021 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-01-23/microsoft-makes-multibillion-dollar-investment-in-openai)).

OpenAI completed a further restructuring on October 28, 2025, converting into a public benefit corporation — OpenAI Group — controlled by a nonprofit, the OpenAI Foundation. Microsoft received a 27% stake in the new structure, reported at approximately $135 billion, on a cumulative $13.8 billion it had invested since 2019, and committed to a reported $250 billion Azure cloud-services agreement; the commercial partnership is set to run at least through 2032, with Microsoft retaining certain rights to OpenAI's models even after any future declaration of artificial general intelligence. OpenAI Foundation board chair Bret Taylor said the new arrangement meant "the nonprofit remains in control of the for-profit, and now has a direct path to major resources before AGI arrives" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2025/10/28/openai-restructures-into-public-benefit-firm-microsoft-takes-27-stake)).

## ChatGPT and scale

OpenAI released ChatGPT, based on GPT-3.5, as a free research preview on November 30, 2022. It reached an estimated 100 million monthly users within about two months — faster than TikTok (nine months) or Instagram (roughly two and a half years) took to reach the same milestone ([History.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/november-30/chatgpt-released-openai)). At OpenAI's first developer conference on November 6, 2023, Altman announced that ChatGPT had reached 100 million weekly active users and that more than 2 million developers, across over 92% of Fortune 500 companies, were building on OpenAI's platform; the company also introduced GPT-4 Turbo, customizable "GPTs," an Assistants API and a planned GPT Store ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/06/Openais-Chatgpt-Now-Has-100-Million-Weekly-Active-Users)).

By October 2025, OpenAI's valuation had grown substantially: a $6.6 billion secondary sale of current and former employees' stock, completed October 2, 2025, to a group including Thrive Capital, SoftBank, Dragoneer Investment Group, Abu Dhabi's MGX and T. Rowe Price, valued the company at $500 billion — up from $300 billion in an earlier 2025 funding round led by SoftBank, and enough to make OpenAI, according to Bloomberg, "the world's largest startup," surpassing SpaceX ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-02/openai-completes-share-sale-at-record-500-billion-valuation)).

## The November 2023 board crisis

On November 17, 2023, OpenAI's board removed Sam Altman as chief executive and Greg Brockman as board chair. The board did not give a detailed public explanation; one contemporaneous account described the dispute as rooted in "apparent differences of balancing safety with artificial intelligence with the desire to release new AI tools into the world," and NPR's reporting the following week likewise found no more specific public reasoning had been offered ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2023/11/22/1214621010/openai-reinstates-sam-altman-as-its-chief-executive); [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/sam-altman-reinstated-as-openai-ceo-with-new-board-replacing-the-one-which-fired-him)). Brockman resigned from OpenAI in protest ([PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/sam-altman-reinstated-as-openai-ceo-with-new-board-replacing-the-one-which-fired-him)). Chief Technology Officer Mira Murati served as interim CEO for part of the crisis and later said she had raised concerns internally about "a race to the bottom on safety," while ultimately supporting Altman's return ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/09/25/openai-mira-murati-cto-stepping-down)).

Within days, an estimated 97% of OpenAI's employees signed a letter threatening to resign unless the board reversed its decision ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2023/11/22/1214621010/openai-reinstates-sam-altman-as-its-chief-executive)). On November 21, 2023, OpenAI reinstated Altman as CEO under a reconstituted board chaired by former Salesforce co-CEO Bret Taylor and including former U.S. Treasury Secretary Larry Summers and Quora CEO Adam D'Angelo ([PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/sam-altman-reinstated-as-openai-ceo-with-new-board-replacing-the-one-which-fired-him)).

## Leadership departures after the crisis

Two of OpenAI's other original leaders left within the following year. Chief scientist and co-founder Ilya Sutskever, who had been on the board that voted to remove Altman, left OpenAI in May 2024; on June 19, 2024, he launched a new company, Safe Superintelligence Inc., with co-founders Daniel Gross (a former Y Combinator partner) and Daniel Levy (a former OpenAI engineer) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/19/ilya-sutskever-openais-former-chief-scientist-launches-new-ai-company)). On September 25, 2024, Mira Murati announced her own resignation as CTO after more than six years, saying she wanted "to create the time and space to do my own exploration"; OpenAI's Vice President of Research, Barret Zoph, and its Chief Research Officer, Bob McGrew, departed the same day, with Altman saying the three had "made these decisions independently of each other and amicably" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/09/25/openai-mira-murati-cto-stepping-down)).

OpenAI's departures also seeded a rival: Anthropic, a competing AI company known for its Claude models, was founded in 2021 by siblings Dario and Daniela Amodei, both described as former OpenAI executives who left over concerns about AI safety ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/question/Who-founded-Anthropic-the-makers-of-Claude-AI-and-why)).

## The Musk lawsuit

Elon Musk sued OpenAI, Sam Altman and Microsoft, alleging that Altman and Brockman committed a "breach of charitable trust" — that Musk had helped fund a nonprofit meant to benefit humanity, and that its 2019 shift toward a for-profit structure instead benefited insiders. OpenAI's defense argued that Musk himself had supported creating the for-profit subsidiary to attract the investment OpenAI needed, and characterized the suit as driven by competitive rivalry, noting Musk had founded the competing AI company xAI roughly eighteen months before filing suit; OpenAI attorney Sarah Eddy also argued that Musk's initial contribution had come "with no strings attached," so there was no charitable trust to enforce ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/18/nx-s1-5822366/musk-altman-openai-jury-verdict-claims-dismissed)).

On May 18, 2026, a nine-member federal jury in the Northern District of California unanimously dismissed all of Musk's claims in under two hours, finding he had waited too long to sue; the verdict turned on the statute of limitations and did not resolve whether a breach of the founding agreement had actually occurred. Presiding judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers said, "I think there's a substantial amount of evidence to support the jury's finding." Musk's lead attorney, Marc Toberoff, said, "This one is not over," indicating he would appeal ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/18/nx-s1-5822366/musk-altman-openai-jury-verdict-claims-dismissed)).

## The 2026 Navier–Stokes claim

In September 2026, OpenAI announced that an internal AI system had produced a proof concerning one branch of the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize problem, a claim that drew both mathematical interest and a public dispute over credit and verification. That episode is covered in a dedicated profile: [OpenAI's 2026 Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize claim](/events/2026-openai-navier-stokes-claim/).
