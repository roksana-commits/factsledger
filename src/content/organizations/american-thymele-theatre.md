## Overview

American Thymele Theatre is a New York nonprofit theater company founded in 1993 by Stephen
Diacrussi, who continues to serve as its artistic director. Its mission is to preserve and
promote Hellenic culture in America through free public performances of Greek and classical
drama ([Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/american-thymele-theatres-free-production-of-medea-begins-july-12-in-nyc-com-180813)).
In 2004, the company was selected by the Cultural Olympiad Committee to present "The
Grocer's Daughter" at the Athens Olympics, and it has received grants from the Stavros S.
Niarchos Foundation
([The National Herald](https://www.thenationalherald.com/american-thymele-theatres-historic-30th-anniversary/)).

## The Euripides Summer Festival

In 2009 the company launched the New York Euripides Summer Festival, a long-running project
to stage every one of the 19 surviving plays of Euripides as free outdoor performances across
New York City. Independently documented productions along the way include "Medea" (2011),
"Children of Hercules" (2012) — reviewed by Time Out New York as a 25-person cast production
([Time Out New York](https://www.timeout.com/newyork/theater/children-of-hercules)) —
"Hippolytus" (2013), and "Bacchae" (2023), which completed the festival's 14-year goal
([TornosNews.gr](https://www.tornosnews.gr/en/greek-news/culture/49025-ny-euripides-free-summer-theater-festival-presents-bacchae-beginning-june-26.html)).
The company describes itself as the only touring company to have staged the complete
surviving Euripides corpus.
