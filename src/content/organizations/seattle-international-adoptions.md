## Overview

Seattle International Adoptions was a Seattle-based adoption agency that served as the U.S. processing arm for a Cambodia-to-United-States adoption pipeline run jointly by [Lynn Devin](/people/lynn-devin/) and her sister, Lauryn Galindo. While Galindo worked in Cambodia arranging placements, Devin ran the agency's U.S. side, screening American families and processing their applications. The two sisters together facilitated the adoption of roughly 700 to 800 Cambodian children by American families between 1997 and 2001 ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/cambodian-adoption-fraud-united-states/)). Both were later prosecuted on federal fraud charges tied to the agency's operations, and agreed to forfeit its profits.

## Business model and role in the adoption pipeline

The Cambodia Daily's 2002 profile of the operation, independently re-read for this profile, described Devin, "a social worker," as the person who screened prospective adoptive parents for suitability, while Galindo selected a child based on availability and the family's preferences. The paper reported a typical total adoption fee of $9,000, of which $3,500 went to donations — including $400 paid directly to the relinquishing orphanage for each child — and the remaining $5,500 went toward processing costs, in a country the paper noted had no formal, standardized adoption fee of its own. The same article reported that Devin had separately drawn up detailed proposed reforms to the Cambodian adoption process, including a central registry of children available for adoption, standardized fees, a ban on facilitators running their own orphanages, and random assignment of orphans to homes to reduce the profit motive — proposals Galindo said she discussed with Cambodia's Minister of Labor and Social Affairs and other officials under the label "Safe Children's Act" ([The Cambodia Daily](https://english.cambodiadaily.com/2002/06/22/is-lauryn-galindo-a-baby-buyer-a-benefactor-or-both/)).

## The fraud case against the agency

The Phnom Penh Post reported that the federal charges against Devin covered ten identified adoption cases occurring between January 1997 and December 2001, the period during which U.S. adoptions from Cambodia were eventually suspended, and that court documents described the pair having "faked documents to falsely pass off the adopted children as orphans and created fictitious identities for the children" to speed adoptions and increase profits ([The Phnom Penh Post](https://www.phnompenhpost.com/national/us-adoption-agent-guilty-visa-fraud)). The Garden Island, a Kauai, Hawaii newspaper covering a local adoptive family's separate civil suit against Galindo, reported that the federal indictment specifically named both Devin and "their Seattle International Adoptions" in describing two cases in which an ill baby was substituted for a healthy one without correcting the child's identity on U.S. visa paperwork ([The Garden Island](https://www.thegardenisland.com/2004/07/02/news/adoption-nightmare-continues-galindo-convicted-but-heartbreak-lives-on/)). Following Devin's December 2003 guilty plea, the U.S. Department of Justice's June 2004 press release described the business as "a Seattle-based company" and stated that Devin and Galindo had agreed that "all illegal profits made by them through SIA, including cash and real property, will be forfeited" ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/archive/opa/pr/2004/June/04_crm_434.htm)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state when Seattle International Adoptions was founded, what its formal corporate or nonprofit structure was, or whether it held a Washington state adoption-agency license or any national accreditation — no independent source located for this profile confirms those details. It does not state a closure or dissolution date for the agency; the sources read here confirm its 2001 operations and its 2004 forfeiture obligations but do not describe what became of it afterward. It also does not resolve the discrepancy between sources that name the agency "Seattle International Adoptions" (U.S. Department of Justice, The Cambodia Daily) and Vice's use of "Seattle International Adoption" — see the open items above. This profile does not describe Galindo's separate, Cambodia-based conduct in detail; that is covered in FactsLedger's [Cambodian adoption scandal](/events/cambodian-adoption-scandal/) profile and in the profile of Lynn Devin.
