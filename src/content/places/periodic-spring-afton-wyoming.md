## Overview

Periodic Spring is a rhythmic, or intermittent, spring located about five miles east of Afton, Wyoming, in
Swift Creek Canyon. It is reported to be the world's largest known spring of this kind — only a handful of
comparable rhythmic springs are known to exist anywhere. It was discovered in August 1894 by Bruce Gardner,
the great-great-grandfather of Olympic wrestler Rulon Gardner, while he was logging in the canyon
([Cowboy State Daily](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2024/05/04/why-the-heck-does-wyomings-rare-periodic-spring-turn-itself-on-and-off/)).

## How it works

The spring cycles on and off at fairly regular intervals, typically flowing for about 15 minutes before
shutting down for a similar period. University of Utah hydrologist Kip Solomon explains the mechanism as a
natural siphon: "As groundwater flows continuously into a cavern, it fills a narrow tube that leads out. As
it pours over the high point of the tube, it creates a siphon effect, sucking water out of the chamber.
Eventually, air rushes in and breaks the siphon." University of Utah studies conducted in the mid-2000s
found no gas content in the spring's water, a result that supports the siphon explanation over an
alternative theory involving cold-water geyser activity. The most pronounced on-off cycling is visible from
late August through November, when regional water tables are at their lowest
([Cowboy State Daily](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2024/05/04/why-the-heck-does-wyomings-rare-periodic-spring-turn-itself-on-and-off/)).
