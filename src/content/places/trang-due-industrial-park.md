## Overview

Trang Due Industrial Park is an industrial zone in Hai Phong, Vietnam, developed by Saigon - Hai Phong Industrial Park Joint Stock Company (SHP), a company majority owned by [Kinh Bac City Development Holding Corporation](/organizations/kinh-bac-city-development-holding-corporation/) — Kinh Bac held an 89.26% voting and beneficial stake in SHP as of September 30, 2024 ([DNSE](https://www.dnse.com.vn/senses/tin-tuc/kinh-bac-bao-lanh-cho-khoan-vay-ngan-hang-cua-chu-dau-tu-khu-cong-nghiep-trang-due-34062116)). It is one of northern Vietnam's most heavily invested electronics-manufacturing zones, anchored by LG Group facilities including LG Display's first OLED display factory built anywhere in the world ([VietnamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/lg-display-to-build-us15-billion-factory-in-hai-phong-E155867.html)).

## Development and scale

SHP, incorporated on July 20, 2006, began infrastructure construction on Trang Due after Hai Phong's People's Committee assigned it the project in 2007; the park was folded into the expanded Dinh Vu-Cat Hai Economic Zone in August 2013, and a second phase launched in September 2014 ([VCCI News](https://vccinews.com/news/48488/trang-due-industrial-park-asserting-position-as-attractive-investment-destination.html)). By 2021, Phases 1 and 2 together covered about 389.77 hectares and were fully leased, hosting 113 projects — 90 foreign-invested and 23 domestic — with more than $8.5 billion in registered investment, including nearly $2.2 billion registered by tenants in 2021 alone; a third phase of about 687 hectares was approved for planning that year ([VCCI News](https://vccinews.com/news/48488/trang-due-industrial-park-asserting-position-as-attractive-investment-destination.html)). Kinh Bac broke ground on that third phase, Trang Due 3, on May 11, 2025, reported at that point as covering 652.73 hectares across four communes in An Lao district, with more than VND8,000 billion in investment capital and a 50-year operating period; the same report described the combined Trang Due 1 and 2 parks as having attracted nearly $11 billion in total capital ([Vietnam Industry and Trade Information Center](https://asemconnectvietnam.gov.vn/default.aspx?ZID1=6&ID8=142917&ID1=2)).

## Anchor tenant: LG Display's OLED factory

In May 2016, LG Display was issued an investment certificate for a $1.5 billion factory described as its first OLED display facility anywhere in the world, sited on more than 40 hectares within Trang Due's second phase, with construction due to begin in June 2016 and production by 2017 ([VietnamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/lg-display-to-build-us15-billion-factory-in-hai-phong-E155867.html)). Hai Phong's Department of Finance later reported that LG Display expanded that investment by a further $1.4 billion, bringing its total capital committed in the city to $4.65 billion ([Department of Finance, Hai Phong City](https://sotc.haiphong.gov.vn/en/news/khoi-cong-du-an-lg-display-viet-nam-tai-hai-phong-voi-tong-von-dau-tu-15-ty-usd)). LG Electronics already operated a separate $1.5 billion manufacturing complex at Trang Due by that point, which by April 2016 had drawn 27 LG-linked supplier projects worth a combined $731 million in registered investment ([VietnamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/lg-display-to-build-us15-billion-factory-in-hai-phong-E155867.html)).

## Ownership and financing

As Kinh Bac's majority-owned subsidiary, SHP has also been financially backstopped by its parent company. In a disclosure reported by the securities-focused outlet DNSE, Kinh Bac said it would guarantee SHP's obligations to a bank for a credit facility — but that facility financed office and factory construction at Kinh Bac's separate Que Vo and Que Vo Extended industrial parks in Bac Ninh province, not at Trang Due itself, illustrating how financing moves between Kinh Bac's various industrial-park subsidiaries rather than staying tied to a single project ([DNSE](https://www.dnse.com.vn/senses/tin-tuc/kinh-bac-bao-lanh-cho-khoan-vay-ngan-hang-cua-chu-dau-tu-khu-cong-nghiep-trang-due-34062116)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not reconcile Trang Due 3's differently reported area — about 687 hectares in 2021-era planning coverage versus 652.73 hectares at its actual May 2025 groundbreaking — presenting both figures as each source reported them (see openItems). It does not give a single "established" date for the park itself as distinct from SHP's July 2006 incorporation, since no source read addresses that distinction directly. It does not detail SHP's full tenant roster beyond the LG Group companies specifically documented above, and it does not assess environmental, labor or land-acquisition issues at the park, as no independently verified source addressing those topics was found.
