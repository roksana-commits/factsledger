## Overview

The Raipur–Dhamtari branch line was a narrow-gauge railway in what is now Chhattisgarh, India,
running from Raipur through Kurud to Dhamtari. It opened in 1900 at a gauge of 2 ft 6 in (762
mm) and about 35 miles (56 km) in length, built and worked by the Bengal-Nagpur Railway
([FIBIwiki](https://wiki.fibis.org/w/Raipur-Dhamtari_Railway)). In 1909 it was extended by the
roughly 11-mile Rajim Branch Railway, running from Abhanpur Junction to Rajim
([FIBIwiki](https://wiki.fibis.org/w/Raipur-Dhamtari_Railway)).

## Closure in 2017

The line's last journey ran on 30 April 2017, ending 117 years of continuous operation. The
Asian Age's closure-day report put the line's reach at roughly 500,000 people across about
10,000 villages in the Raipur–Dhamtari region, describing it as the primary way people travelled
to weddings, festivals, family visits and the cinema — "the journey was short, but romantic," as
the report put it. The immediate cause of closure was the removal of three stations, Telibandha,
Mana and Bhatgaon, to build a 22-km, ₹258-crore expressway connecting Raipur railway station
directly to Swami Vivekanand Airport
([The Asian Age](https://www.asianage.com/india/all-india/150517/raipur-narrow-gauge-train-chugs-its-way-into-history.html)).

Senior assistant loco pilot Sunil Kumar was among those who spoke to the paper about the line's
closing days, and an 88-year-old passenger recalled that his first ride had cost four annas,
against ₹15 for his final journey on the line
([The Asian Age](https://www.asianage.com/india/all-india/150517/raipur-narrow-gauge-train-chugs-its-way-into-history.html)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not independently verify the Asian Age's population and village-count figures
against any government or railway-authority dataset; they are reported here as that
publication's own reporting, not as officially confirmed statistics. It also does not resolve
minor discrepancies found across other public accounts of the line's history — some giving an
1871 date for the route's original conception, or citing mid-May 2017 rather than 30 April 2017
as the closure date — since those alternative accounts were not independently confirmed for this
profile. Finally, this profile does not report on what became of the corridor after 2017 beyond
the reason given for its closure; any later conversion, realignment or reopening of the route was
not checked here.
