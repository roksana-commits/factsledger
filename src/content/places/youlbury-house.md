## Overview

Youlbury House is a Grade II listed modernist house on Boars Hill, three miles southwest of Oxford, built in
1969–1971 on the site of a much larger Victorian mansion that had belonged to the archaeologist Sir Arthur
Evans, excavator of Knossos ([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2016/may/15/classic-1970s-house-in-oxfordshire)).
Evans built his 26-bedroom house and elaborate pleasure gardens on the site in 1893; after his death in 1941 the
mansion fell into disrepair and was eventually demolished
([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2016/may/15/classic-1970s-house-in-oxfordshire)).

## A rare Moggridge house

William Goodhart — a human rights lawyer and Liberal Democrat politician later made a life peer as Baron
Goodhart — and his wife Celia acquired the site in 1966
([Country Life](https://www.countrylife.co.uk/property/a-very-private-property-79719)). Their new house was
designed by Hal Moggridge, Celia's brother-in-law, and remains one of only three residential buildings Moggridge
— later known chiefly as a landscape architect — ever designed
([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2016/may/15/classic-1970s-house-in-oxfordshire)).
Moggridge has said he drew on Frank Lloyd Wright and Alvar Aalto, and that the house was originally designed
with a fully glazed front before concerns about overexposure led to windows "punched into the wooden walls"
instead ([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2016/may/15/classic-1970s-house-in-oxfordshire)).
Country Life adds that the structural engineering was by Ove Arup and Partners, and describes the design as
recalling Wright's Fallingwater, with the building stepping down the hillside from a single storey at one end to
three storeys at the other above a lake
([Country Life](https://www.countrylife.co.uk/property/a-very-private-property-79719)).

The house received Grade II listed status in July 2009, which Country Life notes is unusual for a building of
its era ([Country Life](https://www.countrylife.co.uk/property/a-very-private-property-79719);
[Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1393396)).

## Family memories

The Goodharts' daughter, Laura Watts, told the Guardian the house felt "a bit like a treehouse," recalling deer
and frogs in the surrounding woodland and games played in the built-in storage cupboards
([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2016/may/15/classic-1970s-house-in-oxfordshire)). The
same article describes the house as a hub for political and academic gatherings, given William Goodhart's career,
while noting the family "usually avoided talking politics" with a Conservative-MP relative who lived nearby
([The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2016/may/15/classic-1970s-house-in-oxfordshire)).

## Sale

By 2015, the estate — by then comprising the main house on 9.6 acres plus separate cottages and land totalling
around 27 acres — was put on the market through Savills, split into several lots with a combined guide price of
about £4.5 million ([Country Life](https://www.countrylife.co.uk/property/a-very-private-property-79719)).
