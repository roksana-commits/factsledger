## Overview

Katowice-Muchowiec Airfield (ICAO: EPKM) is a general-aviation aerodrome in central Katowice, Poland,
roughly two kilometres from the city centre. It began as one of interwar Poland's most modern airports: the
Silesian Provincial Committee of the League for Air and Anti-Gas Defence, founded on 24 January 1924, drove
its development, and on 30 March 1926 a 30-year lease was signed for 56 hectares of land, on which a
passenger terminal, wooden hangar, workshops, fuel station and pilot-training school were built
([Dziennik Zachodni](https://dziennikzachodni.pl/muchowiec-supernowoczesne-lotnisko-miedzywojennych-katowic/ar/c3-13653910)).

## Interwar passenger service

Scheduled passenger flights began on 2 January 1929. LOT Polish Airlines, in which the Silesian regional
treasury held a 10 percent stake, operated domestic routes from Muchowiec to Warsaw, Poznań and Kraków and
international routes to Vienna and Brno. Over the following decade the airfield handled about 27,151
passengers and 826 tonnes of cargo and mail across roughly 10,943 takeoffs and landings
([Katowice Airport corporate blog](https://blog.katowice-airport.com/statystyki-lot-na-katowickim-muchowcu-1929-1938/),
citing official interwar statistics). The Poznań and Vienna routes ran only in 1929, and the Kraków and
Brno routes from 1929 to 1933; Warsaw remained the sole continuous destination until commercial service
stopped in August 1939.

## A 2006–2007 dispute between two aeroclubs

By the mid-2000s the airfield's roughly 80 hectares of centrally located land had become a point of
conflict between two aeroclubs. The Polish Aeroclub, led by Gromosław Czempiński, sued the Silesian
Aeroclub, led by Andrzej Wernicke and then managing the airfield, alleging that a transfer of perpetual
land-use rights in the late 1990s had never been properly notarized. Katowice's mayor at the time, Piotr
Uszok, signed a separate agreement directly with the Polish Aeroclub, bypassing the Silesian Aeroclub — a
move reported to have raised concerns about the dispute jeopardizing potential EU modernization funding for
the airfield
([Nasze Miasto Katowice](https://katowice.naszemiasto.pl/prezesi-aeroklubow-polskiego-i-slaskiego-wzieli-sie-za-lby/ar/c3-6649307)).

## Resolution and renewed investment, 2023–2026

Following a February 2023 Katowice city council vote, the city and the Silesian Aeroclub reached a new
agreement: the city took over the bulk of the airfield's land — reported as 710,000 square metres by one
outlet and "roughly 75 hectares" by another — including the runway and access roads, in exchange for
resolving the aeroclub's outstanding debt to the city, with the aeroclub retaining its own buildings
([Portal Samorządowy](https://www.portalsamorzadowy.pl/inwestycje/katowice-zainwestuja-w-lotnisko-muchowiec-jest-porozumienie-z-aeroklubem,432075.html)).
Under a December 2024 contract with Drogopol, a modernized 1,200-metre runway was built at a cost of over 7
million złoty, with the finished surface and lighting accepted by the city in May 2026; a separate apron
and taxiway project worth about 9.5 million złoty began that April. City officials described the plan as
shifting the airfield, ahead of its centenary, toward combined training-sports and business use
([Dziennik Zachodni](https://dziennikzachodni.pl/katowickie-lotnisko-ma-juz-prawie-100-lat-muchowiec-doczekal-sie-nowego-pasa-startowego-i-teraz-otworzy-sie-tez-na-biznes/ar/c1p2-28955939)).

## What this profile does not resolve

Two sources give conflicting accounts of the same 2 January 1929 date: Katowice Airport's own blog
describes a Junkers F13 landing at Muchowiec after flying in from Warsaw, while a Dziennik Zachodni
historical feature describes a Dutch-operated Fokker F.VII departing Muchowiec for Vienna via Brno with
eight passengers aboard. These cannot both describe the same flight, and this profile does not attempt to
adjudicate which account is accurate. Separately, reported figures for the land Katowice acquired in 2023
vary slightly between sources (710,000 m² vs. "roughly 75 hectares") and are both reported here rather than
reconciled into a single number.
