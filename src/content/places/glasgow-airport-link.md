## Overview

The Glasgow Airport Link refers to a long-running, repeatedly stalled proposal for a direct rail connection between Glasgow city centre and Glasgow Airport. First announced in 2006, the project was scrapped in 2009 amid UK government spending cuts, then revived in 2014 as part of the £1.13 billion Glasgow and Clyde Valley City Deal with a target completion of 2025 ([GlasgowWorld](https://www.glasgowworld.com/news/future-glasgow-airport-rail-link-6577871)).

## The 2016 tram-train plan and its 2019 cancellation

In November 2016, planners selected a "tram-train" system — combining the existing railway network with a new light rail spur from Paisley to the airport — promising a direct 16.5-minute journey with no changes required ([The Scotsman](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/the-scotsman/20161125/281724089152372)). On December 12, 2016, the Glasgow City Region Cabinet formally approved a £144 million version of this plan, jointly developed by Glasgow City and Renfrewshire councils ([Glasgow City Council](https://www.glasgow.gov.uk/article/8910/Glasgow-City-Region-Cabinet-approves-plans-for-direct-rail-link-between-Glasgow-Airport-Paisley-and-the-city-centre)). This plan was itself scrapped in 2019; the Scottish Government instead considered a Personal Rapid Transit (PRT) system, with then-Transport Secretary Michael Matheson citing capacity constraints at Glasgow Central Station as an obstacle — a justification business groups publicly disputed, arguing there were "conflicting expert opinions" on whether that constraint was genuine, and criticising the decision as further delay on a project already "in development for over 12 years" ([Business Insider Scotland](https://www.insider.co.uk/news/glasgow-airport-rail-link-prt-13937770)).

## Current status

Transport Scotland's December 2022 [Clyde Metro](/places/clyde-metro/) proposal, a multi-decade, multi-billion-pound mass transit scheme, subsequently absorbed the airport connection as one of many components. As of April 2026, ahead of that year's Scottish Parliament election, a tram-train connection to the airport is being "explored" only within this wider Clyde Metro package, with no funded implementation timeline. Scottish Labour leader Anas Sarwar has publicly criticised "delay and wasteful spending" over nearly two decades of on-again, off-again planning, while the Scottish Greens have argued for prioritising the full Clyde Metro scheme over a standalone airport link ([GlasgowWorld](https://www.glasgowworld.com/news/future-glasgow-airport-rail-link-6577871)).

In the run-up to that election, Sarwar went further, pledging that "a Scottish Labour government under my leadership would back the development of a rail link once and for all," to be funded through a mix of public seed money and private investment. He explicitly declined to attach a cost or completion date, saying he did not want to "give a false timeline or a false costing and repeat the mistakes made by the SNP" ([The Scotsman](https://www.scotsman.com/news/politics/anas-sarwar-glasgow-airport-rail-link-scottish-labour-5616077); [STV News](https://news.stv.tv/west-central/scottish-labour-would-build-glasgow-airport-rail-link)). That pledge was not tested in office: Scottish Labour went on to lose the May 2026 election in what Sarwar called the party's worst result under devolution, and the SNP retained power ([Holyrood](https://www.holyrood.com/news/view,anas-sarwar-concedes-defeat-in-scottish-parliament-election-my-party-is-hurting)). First Minister John Swinney formed a new government on 20 May 2026, appointing Stephen Flynn as Cabinet Secretary for Economy, Tourism and Transport ([The Press and Journal](https://www.pressandjournal.co.uk/fp/politics/scottish-politics/7022504/stephen-flynn-scottish-government-reshuffle/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not assert that any version of the airport rail link will be built, since as of its most recent independently reported status the project has no funded timeline. It does not take a position on the political dispute over prioritising a standalone airport link versus the broader Clyde Metro scheme, and it does not independently verify the Scottish Government's cited capacity concerns at Glasgow Central Station, which business groups have disputed. It also does not describe the incoming Swinney government's own policy toward the airport link specifically: independent, non-Wikipedia reporting confirming or denying any change of position under the new transport secretary could not be found or verified as of September 2026, so the project's status is presented here only through the change of government, not beyond it.
