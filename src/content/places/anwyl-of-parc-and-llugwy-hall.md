## Overview

The Anwyl family were a gentry family of Merionethshire (now part of Gwynedd), seated first at Parc in
Llanfrothen and later at Llugwy Hall in Pennal. Cadw's official listing describes Parc as "the ancient and chief
seat of the Anwyls, one of the most notable families of Meirionnydd in the Tudor and Stuart periods"
([Cadw](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/300004773-parc-llanfrothen)). The line traces to Robert ap Morris,
who died in 1576 and was a junior branch of the Clenennau family; his descendants either adopted the surname
Roberts or retained the "Anwyl" baptismal name of a forebear
([Dictionary of Welsh Biography](https://biography.wales/article/s-ANWY-PAR-1500)).

## Parc, Llanfrothen

William Lewis Anwyl, who served as High Sheriff of Merioneth in 1610 and 1623 and of Caernarfonshire in 1636,
substantially expanded Parc in the early 17th century; his 1641 will refers to the "newly-built plas," corroborated
by a contemporary poem by Huw Machno praising "his new house of great construction" and describing its gardens,
orchards, walls and towers ([Cadw](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/300004773-parc-llanfrothen)). The house
standing today at Parc is dated externally to 1671, its stone bearing the initials of Lewis and Katherine Anwyl,
who had married in 1668 ([Cadw](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/300004773-parc-llanfrothen)). Katherine Anwyl,
who outlived her sons, is separately recorded as a patroness of Welsh literature
([Dictionary of Welsh Biography](https://biography.wales/article/s-ANWY-PAR-1500)). Multiple family members held
the office of High Sheriff of Merioneth across the 17th century, including Richard Anwyl (1658–59) and Robert
Anwyl (1650) ([Dictionary of Welsh Biography](https://biography.wales/article/s-ANWY-PAR-1500)).

## Llugwy Hall, Pennal

A separate branch of the family became associated with Llugwy Hall in Pennal from at least 1695, when Maurice
Anwyl came into his wife Joan's inheritance there
([Cadw](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/300023327-llugwy-hall-pennal)). The earlier part of the surviving
building is said to date from 1611, with a 1746 datestone marking a later enlargement and a substantial c. 1890
expansion in neo-Jacobean style; part of the building was demolished around 1963 after the hall had become a hotel
([Coflein](https://coflein.gov.uk/en/site/96579/details/llugwy-hallllugwy-hotel)). Robert Charles Anwyl, a
Justice of the Peace and High Sheriff of the county, died in 1900 and was among the last of the family connected
with the house; the family's heraldic motto and coat of arms are reported to survive, plastered over, within its
porch ([Cadw](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/300023327-llugwy-hall-pennal)).

## What this profile does not claim

The family's recorded tradition of descent from Owain Gwynedd, King of Gwynedd, is reported as a tradition
documented within the listed-building records themselves, not as independently established fact. No claim is
made about any living person as a current head of the family, since no independently published source could be
found to support one. This profile does not use the title "Anwyl of Tywyn," since the historic seats documented
in primary sources are Parc and Llugwy Hall; a later junior branch's move to a place called "Towyn" in
Denbighshire — a different location from the town of Tywyn in Gwynedd — is noted but is not treated as the
family's defining seat.
