## Overview

The Umarkot Shiv Mandir is a Shiva temple about 6 km from Umerkot in Sindh, Pakistan, near Rana Jaageer Goth. It
is recorded by the federal Department of Archaeology and Museums as "Shiv Temple Umerkot", at coordinates
25.416028° N, 69.776306° E ([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373)).

## Legal status and ownership

The department's site record lists the temple's legal status as **"Not Protected"** and its ownership as
**private** ([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373)).

This is worth stating plainly, because a site that draws crowds on this scale is often assumed to hold protected
heritage status. On the national archaeology department's own record, it does not.

## The Shivratri festival

The department records that the temple hosts a three-day Shiva festival each March, and that Maha Shivaratri
there draws approximately 250,000 people, with visitors travelling from lower and upper Sindh. It states that the
All-Hindu Panchayat of Umarkot funds the festival's expenses
([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373)).

Writing in Dawn in February 2018, Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro reported that the festival that year "was attended by
around 250,000 people", and that the temple is managed by the All Hindu Panchayat of Umarkot
([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1392074)).

## History

The same Dawn article reports that "it is said that this temple is perhaps the oldest in Sindh", and that its
structure "was actually expanded a century ago by a Muslim man"
([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1392074)).

The claim of antiquity is presented in the source as something said of the temple rather than as an established
finding, and is recorded here in the same terms.

## What this profile does not claim

No founding date, construction period or architectural dating is given, because no source read for this profile
supplies one from a heritage authority. No account is offered of the temple's condition or of any conservation
work, as none was located.
