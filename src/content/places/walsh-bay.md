## Overview

Walsh Bay is a historic wharf precinct on Sydney Harbour, on land used before 1788 by the Indigenous Gadigal people for camping, canoeing, cockle-gathering and spear-fishing. Following the 1788 establishment of the Port Jackson convict settlement at nearby Sydney Cove, the bay developed through the 19th century into a major cargo port as Sydney's docklands era took hold ([walshbayhistory.net](https://walshbayhistory.net/)).

## Construction and naming

Between 1912 and 1921, the Sydney Harbour Trust built five piers with ten wharves along Hickson Road, using turpentine piles and ironbark beams and cutting the structures into the sandstone cliff at Dawes Point. In 1918 the precinct was officially named "Walsh Bay" after engineer Henry Deane Walsh, who designed the Trust's modernization scheme for the harbour ([Dictionary of Sydney](https://dictionaryofsydney.org/structure/walsh_bay_wharves); [walshbayhistory.net](https://walshbayhistory.net/)). The wharves shifted toward cruise shipping in the 1920s-1940s before declining after World War II as cargo transport modes changed, and the precinct remained closed to the public as an operating port from 1910 until 2005.

## From industrial decline to arts precinct

In January 1985, architect Vivian Fraser's adaptive-reuse renovation opened The Wharf, converting the former cargo wharves into theatre and arts venues and residential apartments; the Sydney Theatre Company and Sydney Dance Company were among the organizations later housed there. The precinct's historical significance was formally recognized with Royal Australian Historical Society Green Plaque No. 18, installed as part of the Sydney Green Plaques Bicentennial project between 1984 and 1988 ([Dictionary of Sydney](https://dictionaryofsydney.org/structure/walsh_bay_wharves)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile covers Walsh Bay's construction, naming and adaptive-reuse history as documented by these two sources; it does not attempt a full account of the precinct's later 2000s public-access redevelopment or its present-day roles as an arts and residential precinct beyond what is stated above.
