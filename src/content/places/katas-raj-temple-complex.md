## Overview

The Katas Raj temple complex is a group of Hindu temples built around a sacred pond in Chakwal District, in the
Salt Range of Punjab, Pakistan. It is managed by the Evacuee Trust Property Board, the state body responsible for
properties left by those who migrated at Partition
([Dawn, 21 July 2025](https://www.dawn.com/news/1925504)).

The name derives from the Sanskrit *Kataksha*, meaning "tearful eyes". In Hindu tradition the pond formed from
Shiva's tears at the death of Sati ([Dawn, 13 December 2017](https://www.dawn.com/news/1376354)).

## The site

Dawn's 2025 survey of the complex records the **Satgraha**, a group of seven temples described as built between
the 7th and 10th centuries, together with the Ramchandra Temple — a three-storey structure overlooking the pond —
the Hari Singh Nalwa Haveli, a Hanuman Temple and a Shiva Temple. The remains of a Buddhist stupa and of an
ancient Sanskrit university are also on the site.

An earlier Dawn account describes the temples as mostly built on square platforms, with elevations forming a
series of cornices and small rows of pillars crowned by a ribbed dome, and the site as preserving at least 1,500
years of history from the Buddhist era to British rule
([Dawn, 9 May 2016](https://www.dawn.com/news/1256209)).

## Heritage status

Pakistan submitted "The Salt Range and Khewra Salt Mine" to the UNESCO World Heritage tentative list on 12 April
2016. The submission names Katas Raj among the ancient centres of religious pilgrimage within the Salt Range
([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6118/)). A tentative list entry is a state's declaration of
intent to nominate, not an inscription.

## Supreme Court proceedings over groundwater

The temple pond became the subject of Supreme Court proceedings over groundwater extraction by cement plants in
the area.

On 13 December 2017, Dawn reported that the court ordered Bestway Cement to refill the pond within a week, with
Chief Justice Mian Saqib Nisar stating that the court would "not allow cement factories to operate at the cost of
people's health". The pond is recorded as covering two kanals and 15 marlas, with a maximum depth of 20 feet
([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1376354)).

On 8 May 2018, Dawn reported that the court wrapped up the case after Bestway Cement and DG Khan Cement proposed
submitting Rs2 billion in security — Rs1.5 billion and Rs0.5 billion respectively — until they could find an
alternative water source. The same report states that the court "has yet to pass an order or formally accept the
factories' proposal" ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1406378)).

Accounts of how this case ended differ considerably between publications. This profile reports only what Dawn
recorded at the time.

## Condition

A water filtration plant inaugurated at the site in January 2017 had been non-functional for years by the time of
Dawn's July 2025 report. That report also describes flooding on 16 and 17 July 2025 which collapsed the boundary
wall in three places; officials stated that only the boundary wall, built a few years earlier, was destroyed and
that the temple structures themselves were undamaged. The report notes the ceiling of the Hari Singh Nalwa Haveli
as needing urgent attention and visible cracking in temple tiles
([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1925504)).
