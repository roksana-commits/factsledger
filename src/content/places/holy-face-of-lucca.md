## Overview

The Volto Santo di Lucca is an eight-foot-tall wooden crucifix housed in Lucca Cathedral (the Cathedral of San Martino) in Tuscany, Italy. Radiocarbon dating places its wood and canvas between 770 and 880 CE, making it among the oldest surviving wooden sculptures in the West. The figure depicts Christ fully robed in a long tunic, an iconographic choice more common in Eastern Christian traditions than Western ones ([Aleteia](https://aleteia.org/2024/02/28/the-holy-face-of-lucca-did-nicodemus-hav-angels-help-on-this-sculpture/)).

## Legend and early history

Medieval hagiography credits the disciple Nicodemus with carving the crucifix from cedar wood, saying he fell asleep before finishing the face and awoke to find it miraculously completed by an angel — an example of the "acheiropoieta" tradition of images "not made by human hands." A parallel legend holds that the crucifix arrived in Lucca around 782 CE aboard a crewless boat that resisted capture until a local bishop called upon it ([Aleteia](https://aleteia.org/2024/02/28/the-holy-face-of-lucca-did-nicodemus-hav-angels-help-on-this-sculpture/)).

## Medieval fame

The relic's reach across medieval Europe is documented independently of its own legend. England's King William II invoked it in oaths between 1087 and 1100, saying "per Sanctum Vultum de Luca" ("by the Holy Face of Lucca"). In the 13th century, the chronicler Gervasius of Tilbury described its wide-open eyes as "terrible." Its location on the Via Francigena, the pilgrimage route to Rome, helped spread its fame further through word of mouth among travelers ([Indagini e Misteri](https://www.indaginiemisteri.it/en/the-holy-face-of-lucca-historical-analysis-and-recent-studies/)).

## Later history and study

In 1484, a Renaissance temple designed by the sculptor Matteo Civitali was built within Lucca Cathedral specifically to house the crucifix. In 2020, to mark the cathedral's 950th anniversary, the Institute of Nuclear Physics in Florence carried out radiocarbon analysis of wood and canvas fragments, confirming the 8th-9th century date ([Indagini e Misteri](https://www.indaginiemisteri.it/en/the-holy-face-of-lucca-historical-analysis-and-recent-studies/)). As of 2024, the crucifix was undergoing restoration within the cathedral ([Aleteia](https://aleteia.org/2024/02/28/the-holy-face-of-lucca-did-nicodemus-hav-angels-help-on-this-sculpture/)).
