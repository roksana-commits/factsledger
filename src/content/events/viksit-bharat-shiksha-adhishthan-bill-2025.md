## Overview

The Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhishthan Bill, 2025 is a pending Indian bill that would dissolve three of the
country's existing higher-education regulators — the University Grants Commission (UGC), the All India
Council for Technical Education (AICTE), and the National Council for Teacher Education (NCTE) — and replace
them with a single apex authority, the Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhishthan, organized into three subordinate
councils covering regulation, academic standards, and accreditation
([PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-viksit-bharat-shiksha-adhishthan-bill-2025)).
The Union Cabinet approved the bill on 12 December 2025, and Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan
introduced it in the Lok Sabha on 15 December 2025
([Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2204351&reg=3&lang=1)).

## Provisions

The bill would repeal the UGC Act (1956), the AICTE Act (1987) and the NCTE Act (1993), consolidating their
functions under the new Adhishthan. Its three councils — described in the bill as the Viksit Bharat Shiksha
Viniyaman Parishad (Regulatory Council), the Viksit Bharat Shiksha Manak Parishad (Standards Council), and
the Viksit Bharat Shiksha Gunvatta Parishad (Accreditation Council) — are together tasked with setting
minimum institutional standards, framing learning-outcome benchmarks, and building an accreditation system
for technical, teacher-training and architecture education, while explicitly excluding medical and legal
education from its scope
([Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2204351&reg=3&lang=1); [PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-viksit-bharat-shiksha-adhishthan-bill-2025)).

## Political reaction and JPC referral

The bill drew immediate pushback. FEDCUTA, a federation of central-university teachers' associations, called
it "fundamentally flawed" and demanded its "complete withdrawal," arguing that although the bill describes
its three councils as independent, the apex council's power to "issue direction and ensure coordination"
effectively places the whole regulatory structure under central government control, and separately warning
that the bill leaves teachers' pay and service conditions outside a defined regulatory framework
([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/viksit-bharat-shiksha-adhishthan-bill-2025-fundamentally-flawed-must-withdraw-central-university-teachers-ugc-aicte-ncte-heci-hei/amp)).

Facing objections raised in Parliament's Business Advisory Committee, Parliamentary Affairs Minister Kiren
Rijiju had the bill referred, on 16 December 2025, to a 31-member Joint Parliamentary Committee chaired by
Daggubati Purandeswari, to report back by the end of February
([PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-viksit-bharat-shiksha-adhishthan-bill-2025)).
At the time of referral, several opposition MPs went on record with specific objections: Congress's Manish
Tewari argued the bill "suffers from excessive delegation of legislative power" over accreditation,
degree-granting and penalties; his party colleague Jothimani argued it overrides states despite their
universities enrolling 81% of students and objected to the "Adhishthan" name as excessive Sanskritisation;
DMK's G. Selvam called the bill "nothing but abuse of the process of law"; Kerala MP N.K. Premchandran of the
Revolutionary Socialist Party objected to the bill's nomenclature and its central override of state
universities; and Trinamool Congress's Sougata Roy criticized both the short notice given for the bill and
its centralization of funding previously controlled by the UGC
([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/viksit-bharat-shiksha-adhishthan-bill-vbsa-2025-centre-power-grab-higher-education-state-universities-hindi-congress-tmc-dmk-jpc/amp)).
