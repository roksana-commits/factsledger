## Overview

In September 2026, a five-storey building housing a boys' hostel collapsed in the Satya Niketan area near Delhi University's South Campus, killing seven people.

## The collapse and its cause

The roughly 50-year-old building was undergoing basement renovation work at the time it collapsed; police attributed the failure to that renovation work, compounded by accumulated water in the basement. Seven people were killed and approximately eleven to twelve others were rescued from the debris ([Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/delhi-building-collapse-live-satya-niketan-rescue-operation)). Delhi Police arrested the building's owner, Hariram Gupta, his wife Urmila Gupta (in whose name the building was registered), and their son Mahesh Gupta, booking them for culpable homicide, negligence in the upkeep of a structure, and endangering others' safety ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/top-headlines/hc-says-mc-du-cant-escape-blame-as-delhi-hostel-owner-among-3-held-toll-7/)).

## Court and regulatory response

The Delhi High Court held that responsibility for the tragedy extended beyond the building's owners, stating that the Municipal Corporation of Delhi and Delhi University "can't escape responsibility." The court pointed to inadequate university hostel capacity that pushes students toward unsafe private accommodation, and remarked that the building appeared to have been constructed with six storeys despite permission for only two ([Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/delhi-building-collapse-live-satya-niketan-rescue-operation); [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/top-headlines/hc-says-mc-du-cant-escape-blame-as-delhi-hostel-owner-among-3-held-toll-7/)). The Municipal Corporation of Delhi subsequently suspended five officials, including the South Zone Deputy Commissioner and four engineers ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/top-headlines/hc-says-mc-du-cant-escape-blame-as-delhi-hostel-owner-among-3-held-toll-7/)).

## What this profile does not claim

The two independent national news accounts used for this profile disagree on the exact date of the collapse (6 September 2026 per one, 7 September 2026 per the other) and give slightly different rescue counts; this profile states both figures rather than silently picking one. A separate, unrelated building collapse that occurred in the same Satya Niketan neighbourhood in 2022 — also during renovation, also involving a paying-guest conversion — was identified during research and is deliberately not referenced here, to avoid conflating the two incidents.
