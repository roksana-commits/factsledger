## Overview

The Viksit Bharat – Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025 — widely referred to by its abbreviation, "VB-G RAM G" — is Indian legislation that repeals and replaces the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005 (MGNREGA), the two-decade-old law guaranteeing 100 days of paid rural employment per household per year. The Bill was introduced in the Lok Sabha on 16 December 2025 and passed by both houses of Parliament on 18 December 2025, before receiving presidential assent from President Droupadi Murmu on 21 December 2025 ([PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-viksit-bharat-%E2%80%93-guarantee-for-rozgar-and-ajeevika-mission-gramin-vb-%E2%80%93-g-ram-g-bill-2025); [Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2207187&reg=3&lang=2)).

The new Act raises the statutory employment guarantee from 100 to 125 days per rural household per year, and restructures how the scheme is funded: rather than the funding arrangement under MGNREGA, the new law establishes it as a centrally sponsored scheme with a 60:40 central-to-state cost-sharing ratio for most states, a more generous 90:10 ratio for north-eastern and Himalayan states, and full central funding for Union Territories ([PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-viksit-bharat-%E2%80%93-guarantee-for-rozgar-and-ajeevika-mission-gramin-vb-%E2%80%93-g-ram-g-bill-2025)). The government has stated the new Act is scheduled to take effect nationwide, with MGNREGA repealed, on 1 July 2026 ([Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2259691&reg=3&lang=1)).

## Passage and parliamentary conflict

The Bill's passage was contentious. Deccan Herald, a national Indian newspaper, reported that the Lok Sabha passed the Bill "amid ruckus" on 18 December 2025, with opposition MPs tearing up copies of the legislation on the floor of the House ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/lok-sabha-passes-vb-g-ram-g-bill-amid-opposition-protests-3835324)). Much of the opposition's public criticism centred on the renaming of the scheme away from its association with Mahatma Gandhi.

## Independent criticism of the Act's structure

Beyond the immediate parliamentary conflict, the new Act has drawn substantive policy criticism. In an interview published on the London School of Economics' South Asia research blog in January 2026, economist Jean Drèze — widely credited as one of the architects of the original MGNREGA — argued that the new law converts what had been a legally enforceable employment guarantee into a more discretionary scheme. Drèze pointed to a provision he characterised as a "switch-off clause," which he said lets the central government decide where the guarantee applies, and argued that the 60:40 central-state cost-sharing ratio functions as a funding ceiling that could particularly burden poorer states required to cover costs beyond their normative central allocation ([South Asia @ LSE](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/southasia/2026/01/19/india-repealing-the-employment-guarantee-act/)).
