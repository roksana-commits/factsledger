## Overview

Chovqan is a traditional horse-riding game from the Karabakh region of Azerbaijan, played by two teams of
five riders — two fullbacks and three forwards — on a flat grass field, using wooden mallets to strike a
small leather or wooden ball toward the opposing team's goal. Matches are accompanied by instrumental folk
music called janghi, and players traditionally wear large astrakhan hats, long fitted coats, and
specialized trousers and shoes
([UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/USL/chovqan-a-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-in-the-republic-of-azerbaijan-00905)).

## Cultural role and transmission

The game is rooted in the nomadic traditions of the region and reflects the horse's central role in local
life, serving to strengthen community identity among the male farmers and skilled riders who typically play
it. Knowledge of the game passes between generations through collective training, in which experienced
players teach newcomers specific techniques and rules rather than through formal instruction
([UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/USL/chovqan-a-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-in-the-republic-of-azerbaijan-00905)).

## UNESCO recognition

In 2013, UNESCO inscribed Chovqan on its List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent
Safeguarding (decision 8.COM 7.a.1). The inscription documented specific threats to the tradition's
survival: diminishing interest among younger generations, urbanization, rural-to-urban migration, and a
shortage of both players and the Karabakh horses the game requires. Under the inscription, periodic
safeguarding reports are required every four years, with the next submission due by December 2029
([UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/USL/chovqan-a-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-in-the-republic-of-azerbaijan-00905)).
