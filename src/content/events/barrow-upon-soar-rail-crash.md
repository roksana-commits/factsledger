## Overview

On the morning of 1 February 2008, a tipper lorry working on rail maintenance near Barrow upon Soar station in
Leicestershire struck a Victorian wrought-iron footbridge with its raised body, pulling a section of the bridge
down onto the Leicester–Nottingham/Derby line. Before the line could be closed, a Class 158 service running as
1L03 from Nottingham to Norwich collided with the debris at around 06:32 and derailed
([RAIB Report 18/2008](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a80131ced915d74e622c510/080925_R182008_BarrowUponSoar.pdf)).

## Cause and findings

The UK's Rail Accident Investigation Branch found that the lorry driver had forgotten to lower the vehicle's body
after being distracted by a request to unlock the cab's passenger door for a site safety controller; an in-cab
visual alarm meant to warn of a raised body was easily overlooked. The report also identified a broader failure:
Network Rail had no formal process for assessing and managing the risks of road-vehicle movements near live
railway lines, and incorrect line-protection procedures had been used at the site. RAIB recommended new
risk-assessment processes, a review of tipper-lorry alarm requirements, and clearer protection procedures
([RAIB Report 18/2008](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a80131ced915d74e622c510/080925_R182008_BarrowUponSoar.pdf)).

## Injuries and aftermath

The train driver, guard and one passenger were hospitalised but released within 24 hours; the lorry driver was
uninjured, and a site safety controller was treated for shock ([RAIB Report 18/2008](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a80131ced915d74e622c510/080925_R182008_BarrowUponSoar.pdf)).
Independent trade-press coverage at the time confirmed the same account of the collision and the scope of the
coming investigation ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/Rail-News/investigation-into-an-accident-at-barrow-on-soar-on-1-february-2008)).
The damaged Victorian footbridge was replaced later that year with a new steel structure, assembled from three
sections and lifted into place by a 400-tonne crane over a single weekend; residents welcomed the improved,
non-slip replacement while noting it lacked the original ironwork's Victorian character
([Barrow Heritage Group](https://www.barrowuponsoarheritage.org.uk/articles/village-history/local-history/barrow-train-crash-2008.html)).
