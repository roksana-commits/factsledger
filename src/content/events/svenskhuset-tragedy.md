## Overview

The Svenskhuset Tragedy refers to the deaths of 17 seal hunters who overwintered at Svenskhuset ("Swedish
House"), a trapping station at Kapp Thordsen on Spitsbergen, the main island of the Svalbard archipelago,
during the winter of 1872-73. In autumn 1872, a sealing ship had become trapped in ice at Gråhuken in
northern Svalbard; after the Nordenskiöld expedition rescued the crew, 17 hunters without families rowed
roughly 200 miles south to Svenskhuset to overwinter using stores left at the station. All 17 died there
over the winter, and their fate was not discovered until a rescue team arrived from mainland Sweden months
later ([PolarQuest](https://www.polar-quest.com/blog/svalbard-mixed/the-tragedy-of-svenskhuset)).

## From presumed scurvy to a 2008-2010 forensic finding

For over a century, the deaths were generally attributed to scurvy, a common risk for Arctic overwintering
parties of the era. A forensic investigation conducted between 2008 and 2010 — including exhumation of the
remains — overturned that assumption. Researchers found no objective signs of scurvy in the skeletal
remains, but measured high concentrations of lead (102.05 micrograms per gram in bone samples), pointing
instead to lead poisoning, likely from poorly soldered food tins, as the probable cause of death. The
findings were published in the peer-reviewed journal Polar Record
([Polar Record, via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19965937/)).
