## Overview

The Prohibition of Child Marriage (Amendment) Bill, 2021 was introduced in India's Lok Sabha on 21 December 2021, proposing to raise the minimum legal marriage age for women from 18 to 21, bringing it in line with the existing minimum for men ([PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/prs-products/prs-bill-summary-3898)). The bill would have amended five separate marriage laws — the Indian Christian Marriage Act, the Parsi Marriage and Divorce Act, the Special Marriage Act, the Hindu Marriage Act, and the Foreign Marriage Act — and extended the period during which a child marriage could be judicially annulled from two years past the age of majority to five years ([PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/prs-products/prs-bill-summary-3898)).

The proposal traced back to a February 2020 Union Budget speech, in which Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman announced the government would examine raising the marriage age, leading to a ten-member task force studying the question before the bill's eventual introduction ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india/bill-seeking-to-increase-marriage-age-of-girls-among-four-to-lapse-2889891)).

## Committee review and lapse

After introduction, the bill was referred to the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports. The committee sought repeated extensions because "discussions did not conclude, and reservations were expressed from several quarters" — reporting notes the government itself "also appeared not keen on the bill." A seventh extension, of four months, effectively guaranteed the bill would not be taken up before the 17th Lok Sabha's term ended, and it lapsed along with three other pending bills ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india/bill-seeking-to-increase-marriage-age-of-girls-among-four-to-lapse-2889891)).

## Legal critique

An independent legal commentary published on SCC Online Blog argued the bill would have created an internal contradiction with the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act: courts have applied a judicially recognised "Romeo-Juliet clause" acquitting defendants in consensual relationships where a partner is between 16 and 18, meaning a woman could legally consent to a sexual relationship at 16 but not to marriage before 21. The same commentary disputed the bill's stated justification — reducing infant and maternal mortality — noting that infant mortality had already declined substantially in the preceding decade independent of any marriage-age change, and that the government's own data did not establish a credible link between child marriage and those mortality rates. The commentary instead argued for incentive-based measures, such as conditional cash transfers for unmarried girls pursuing education, over a punitive age restriction ([SCC Online Blog](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2023/06/01/critique-on-the-prohibition-of-child-marriage-amendment-bill-2021-in-the-light-of-romeo-juliet-clause-under-pocso-act-and-impact-of-the-bill-on-infant-mortality-rate-and-maternal/)).
