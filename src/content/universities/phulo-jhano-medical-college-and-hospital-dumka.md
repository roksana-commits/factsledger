## Overview

Phulo Jhano Medical College and Hospital is a government medical college in Dumka, in the Indian state of
Jharkhand. Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated the college building on 17 February 2019, alongside similar
new government medical colleges at Palamau and Hazaribagh, as part of a wider state development push that
included a Rs 1,904-crore project to build four 500-bed hospitals across Jamshedpur, Palamau, Hazaribagh and
Dumka; academic sessions at the three new colleges were scheduled to begin in June 2019
([Business Standard/IANS](https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ians/modi-inaugurates-3-medical-colleges-in-jharkhand-119021700345_1.html)).

## Admission ban and reinstatement

Despite the 2019 inauguration, [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/news/education/no-admission-in-three-jharkhand-medical-colleges-inaugurated-by-pm)
reported that the National Medical Commission barred Dumka's college, together with its counterparts at
Hazaribagh and Palamu, from admitting any students for the 2020-21 academic session, citing incomplete
infrastructure, missing instruments and equipment, and a roughly 30% faculty shortfall. The move cut Jharkhand's
statewide MBBS seat count from 580 in 2019-20 to 355 in 2020-21. Chief Minister Hemant Soren wrote to the NMC on
5 November 2020, stating that "certain finishing works in the building infrastructure and installation of some
equipment and furniture, etc., could not be completed due to the lockdown imposed in March this year," and asked
that admissions be permitted.

The ban was subsequently lifted for a single admission cycle: [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/education/dumka-medical-college-gets-nmc-nod-for-100-mbbs-seats-82741)
reported that the NMC's Medical Assessment and Rating Board issued a letter dated 30 September 2021 approving
admission of one 100-seat MBBS batch for 2021-22. The approval was explicitly conditional: it was valid for one
year only, covered a single batch against the sanctioned 100-seat annual intake, and stated that any future batch
would require fresh MARB recognition, with admissions made in violation of the condition to be "treated as
irregular" and subject to action under the NMC Act.

## Continued faculty shortages

A follow-up two-day NMC inspection in December 2021, reported by [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/education/no-faculty-in-5-departments-nmc-inspection-points-out-faculty-shortage-at-dumka-medical-college-86235),
found a complete absence of faculty in five departments — biochemistry, pharmacology, radiology, dermatology and
anaesthesia — along with shortages in others. To retain recognition, the principal in charge, Dr Arun Kumar,
submitted an affidavit to the MARB committing to fill the vacant posts before the 2022-23 session, warning that
"if the shortages in faculty members are not addressed very soon, the lacunae can hurt the college's prospects in
the near future."
