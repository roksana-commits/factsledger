## Overview

Pennsylvania College of Technology, in Williamsport, Pennsylvania, traces its origins to 1914, when the
Williamsport School District established a vocational-technical division — a lineage independently
documented in a Pennsylvania state library digital archive of the institute's own historical photographs
([POWER Library digital archive](https://digitalarchives.powerlibrary.org/papd/islandora/object/papd:willtech)).
The program became an independent institution, the Williamsport Technical Institute, in 1941, the same year
it launched an Emergency Training Commission for wartime defense-industry training after the Nazi invasions
of the Netherlands and Belgium — described by the institute's own history as one of the nation's first
training programs adapted for war preparation
([Pennsylvania College of Technology](https://www.pct.edu/about/history/williamsport-technical-institute)).
In 1951 it launched what it describes as the nation's first Vocational Diagnostic Program, combining
job-trial exploration with counseling for disabled students. In 1989 the institution reorganized as
Pennsylvania College of Technology, becoming a special-mission affiliate of Penn State University
([Pennsylvania College of Technology](https://www.pct.edu/about/history)).

## Recent public funding

On 11 July 2024, Acting U.S. Secretary of Labor Julie Su and White House Domestic Policy Advisor Neera
Tanden visited the college to announce a $4 million federal grant, part of a nationwide $244 million
Registered Apprenticeship investment, supporting the college's MIDAS (Modular, Industry-Driven Apprenticeship
Strategies) program for training in advanced manufacturing, clean energy, information technology and
cybersecurity. Su said the investment "demonstrates the Biden-Harris administration's commitment to both
America's workers and employers," while Tanden noted the program "works to meet employer demand for advanced
manufacturing occupations in Pennsylvania, New Jersey, New York and nationwide." Williamsport Mayor Derek
Slaughter also attended
([Williamsport Sun-Gazette](https://www.sungazette.com/news/top-news/2024/07/historic-investment-pennsylvania-college-of-technology-welcomes-white-house-officials-for-4m-grant-announcement/)).
In May 2025, the college separately received $1 million from Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro's
administration, part of a $14 million statewide Dual Credit Innovation Grant Program distributed to 15
public institutions, to expand tuition-free dual-enrollment courses for high school students — a program
through which more than 2,000 students earned over 7,000 college credits in the 2023–24 academic year
([Williamsport Sun-Gazette](https://www.sungazette.com/news/top-news/2025/05/pennsylvania-college-of-technology-gets-1-million-in-state-funds-to-expand-course-offerings/)).
