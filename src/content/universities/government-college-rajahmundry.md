## Overview

Government College, Rajahmundry, on the banks of the Godavari river in Andhra Pradesh, India, is described as the oldest government college in the state, tracing its origins to an 1853 Zilla Parishad high school that was upgraded to a Provincial school of Madras Presidency in 1868 and became a college in 1885 ([Hindu Post](https://hindupost.in/education/oldest-government-college-in-andhra-pradesh/)). It was affiliated with the University of Madras in 1891, re-affiliated to Andhra University in 1926, and granted autonomous status with affiliation to [Adikavi Nannaya University](/universities/adikavi-nannaya-university/) in 2000. As of the source describing its campus, it operates on a 38-acre site with roughly 5,100 students and 180 faculty across 24 departments offering undergraduate and postgraduate programs in arts, commerce and science.

## Accreditation

The college received a National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC) 'A' grade in 2004, a rating it retained in a subsequent 2011 assessment, valid through November 2018 with a two-year bonus period. Around 2018, it was pursuing a higher 'A++' grade under NAAC's revised, CGPA-based assessment framework — a step that would extend its accreditation validity to ten years and make it eligible for central government funding of ₹10 crore ([Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/nation/current-affairs/180318/rajahmundry-government-arts-college-aims-naac-a-grade.html)).

## Notable alumni

Koka Subba Rao, who went on to serve as Chief Justice of India, graduated from the college — then Government Arts College, Rajahmundry — before studying law at Madras Law College ([SCC Online Blog](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2020/07/15/118th-birth-anniversary-of-chief-justice-koka-subba-rao-the-custodian-of-the-fundamental-rights/)). This is independently confirmed and is separate from the additional, single-sourced alumni claims discussed below.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not assert that the college was "the first government college in India" to receive an 'A+' NAAC rating, a claim reported by one source (Hindu Post) but not corroborated by, and in tension with, an independent 2018 report describing the college as holding a plain 'A' grade and still pursuing a higher grade at that time (see openItems). It also does not independently verify the reported list of notable alumni — including Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan, Tanguturi Prakasam and V. K. Krishna Menon — since these claims rest on a single source and no second independent confirmation was found. A further check for additional alumni named elsewhere (P. S. Rau, Kurma Venkata Reddy Naidu, Gadicherla Harisarvottama Rao, Damerla Rama Rao, Adivi Bapiraju, Kavikondala Venkat Rao) found no independent source directly confirming their connection to this specific college; one source found on Damerla Rama Rao describes only that the college's then-principal, an artist himself, mentored him and arranged his later admission elsewhere, not that Rama Rao studied at the college. This profile also does not assert the college's own claims — made only on its official website — of a more recent NAAC 'A+' grade (CGPA 3.38) or a 2016 University Grants Commission "College with Potential for Excellence" designation, since no independent source corroborating or dating either claim was found (see openItems). It does rely on that same official website for the uncontested, non-evaluative fact of the college's current principal, Dr. K. Ramachandra Rao.
