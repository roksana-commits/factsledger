## Overview

Nottingham Law School is a law school of Nottingham Trent University in Nottingham, England, founded in 1964 as part of what was then Nottingham Regional College of Technology and offering its first undergraduate law degree that year ([Legal Cheek](https://www.legalcheek.com/law-school/nottingham-law-school/); [Nottingham Trent University](https://www.ntu.ac.uk/study-and-courses/academic-schools/nottingham-law-school/celebrating-60-years-of-excellence-in-legal-education)). Its parent institution became Trent Polytechnic in 1970 and was granted university status, as Nottingham Trent University, in 1992 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nottingham-Trent-University)). The school runs [NLS Legal](/organizations/nls-legal/), a regulated on-campus teaching law firm that has operated since 2006.

## Research assessment

In the UK's 2021 Research Excellence Framework, the official results database records that 39% of Nottingham Law School's submitted research to Unit of Assessment 18 (Law) was rated 4-star ("world-leading") and 56% was rated 3-star ("internationally excellent"), a combined 95%, with the remaining 5% rated 2-star ([Research Excellence Framework 2021](https://results2021.ref.ac.uk/profiles/institutions/10004797)). Nottingham Trent University's own promotional material describes its law research as "100% world-leading or internationally excellent" in the same exercise; that figure does not match the primary results database, and this profile reports the official breakdown rather than the university's rounded claim.

## Recognition and leadership

The East Midlands Business Link, an independent regional business publication, reported that NLS Legal was named "Law Firm of the Year" at the LexisNexis Legal Awards 2022, and that it launched a dedicated pro bono family law service that year covering divorce, financial remedies, child arrangements and domestic abuse matters ([East Midlands Business Link](https://www.eastmidlandsbusinesslink.co.uk/mag/ooo/double-award-win-for-inspirational-nls-legal-team/)). The same report said that at the Nottinghamshire Law Society Awards 2023, NLS Legal solicitor Katie Smith won "Solicitor of the Year" for that service, having assisted more than 30 clients and secured about £85,000 in financial settlements, while practice manager Carlota Gonzalez Laynez won "Business Support Champion," credited partly with establishing the clinic's partnership with Support Through Court. It quoted Laura Pinkney, head of NLS Legal, and Jenny Chapman, Nottingham Trent University's Executive Dean, both praising the two staff members.

## Community partnership with Support Through Court

In November 2024, Legal Cheek reported that the national helpline of the charity Support Through Court — which handles around 17,000 calls a year assisting people representing themselves in civil and family court proceedings — relocated its operations to Nottingham Law School, to be staffed by a minimum of 150 trained students annually and up to 48 students working across its services on a given day ([Legal Cheek](https://www.legalcheek.com/2024/11/legal-helpline-finds-new-home-at-nottingham-law-school/)). The report quoted NLS Legal head Laura Pinkney describing the move as building on an existing partnership between Support Through Court and the school, and Support Through Court's Lizzy Parkes describing it as an opportunity to reach more clients nationally.

## Facilities and programmes

Legal Cheek's independent profile of the school, cited above, also describes it as based in the Chaucer Building on Nottingham Trent University's city-centre campus, with a mock criminal courtroom and a mock civil courtroom built as replicas of working courts, used for advocacy training, mock trials and mooting competitions run through the school's Mooter's Guild. The same profile describes an undergraduate offering of several LLB variants (including Business Law, International Law, and Law combined with Psychology, Criminology or Business), alongside postgraduate LLMs in subjects such as technology, sport, health, environmental, corporate and international law, and preparation courses for the Solicitors Qualifying Examination (SQE).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile independently verifies the school's 1964 founding year, its parent institution's 1970 and 1992 status changes, its official REF 2021 research ratings, and its 2022-2024 award and partnership record, against Legal Cheek, East Midlands Business Link, Encyclopaedia Britannica and the primary REF 2021 results database. It does not independently verify the school's current student or staff numbers, course rankings, or the full detail of its history between 1970 and 2021, which rest on the institution's own published account and are not repeated here as independently confirmed. The £85,000 client settlement figure and the description of Jenny Chapman as Executive Dean rest on the single regional-press report cited above and were not corroborated elsewhere. The discrepancy between the university's "100%" REF claim and the official 95% figure is noted above rather than resolved, since only the primary results database could be independently checked.
